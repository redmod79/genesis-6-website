    Analysis: Jude 1:6-7 "In Like Manner"¶

    The Interpretive Question¶

    In Jude 1:7, the phrase τὸν ὅμοιον τρόπον τούτοις ("in like manner to these") appears. The question is: What does τούτοις ("these") refer to?

    Two Possible Antecedents¶

    Option τούτοις refers to Meaning
    A The angels (v6) Sodom sinned "in like manner to the angels"
    B The inhabitants of the cities (v7) The surrounding cities sinned "like the people of Sodom"

    Grammatical Analysis¶

    The Gender Problem¶

    τούτοις is Dative Plural MASCULINE

    But the nouns in verse 7 are all FEMININE (or neuter): - Σόδομα (Sodom) - N-NSF - Γόμορρα (Gomorrah) - N-NSF - πόλεις (cities) - N-NPF

    Note on Σόδομα/Γόμορρα gender: The N1904 Text-Fabric database parses these as feminine. However, Strong's classifies Σόδομα as neuter* (G4670) and Γόμορρα as neuter or feminine (G1116). Hebrew place names transliterated into Greek can have variable gender. This discrepancy does not affect the argument: whether feminine or neuter, these nouns are not masculine, so the grammatical tension with τούτοις (masculine) remains.

    The only MASCULINE plural noun in the immediate context is: - ἀγγέλους (angels) - N-APM (v6)

    Initial Conclusion¶

    Strict grammatical agreement suggests τούτοις refers to the angels.

    Counter-Evidence: Constructio ad Sensum¶

    Greek authors sometimes use constructio ad sensum (construction according to sense) - where grammatical gender follows the meaning rather than strict agreement.

    Example 1: Luke 10:13¶

    Greek: καθήμενοι (sitting) - V-PNP-NPM (Nominative Plural MASCULINE)

    Context: Describes Tyre and Sidon (both FEMININE cities) "sitting in sackcloth"

    Word Gender Meaning
    Τύρῳ Feminine Tyre
    Σιδῶνι Feminine Sidon
    καθήμενοι Masculine sitting

    Pattern: Feminine cities → Masculine participle (referring to the inhabitants)

    Example 2: Matthew 23:37¶

    Greek: Jerusalem is addressed with feminine participle (ἀποκτείνουσα - "killing"), but then the verb switches to 2nd person plural (ἠθελήσατε - "you [plural] were not willing") addressing the people.

    Example 3: Galatians 3:16¶

    Word Gender Meaning
    σπέρματι Neuter seed
    ὅς Masculine who

    Paul uses a masculine pronoun for a neuter noun because he's referring to Christ (a person).

    Example 4: Revelation 4:8¶

    Word Gender Meaning
    τὰ τέσσερα ζῷα Neuter plural the four living creatures
    λέγοντες Masculine plural saying

    Neuter noun takes masculine participle (treating creatures as persons).

    Example 5: Acts 8:5 (Most Directly Parallel)¶

    Greek: Φίλιππος δὲ κατελθὼν εἰς τὴν πόλιν τῆς Σαμαρίας ἐκήρυσσεν αὐτοῖς τὸν Χριστόν.

    Word Parsing Gender Meaning
    τὴν πόλιν N-ASF Feminine the city
    τῆς Σαμαρίας N-GSF Feminine of Samaria
    αὐτοῖς P-DPM Masculine Plural to them

    Philip preached to "the city" (feminine) but used αὐτοῖς (masculine dative plural) - referring to the inhabitants. This is the most directly parallel construction to Jude 1:7's τούτοις.

    Example 6: Acts 8:14¶

    Greek: ...ὅτι δέδεκται ἡ Σαμάρια τὸν λόγον τοῦ Θεοῦ, ἀπέστειλαν πρὸς αὐτοὺς Πέτρον καὶ Ἰωάνην

    Word Parsing Gender Meaning
    ἡ Σαμάρια N-NSF Feminine Samaria
    αὐτοὺς P-APM Masculine Plural them

    "Samaria received the word" (feminine subject), but apostles sent πρὸς αὐτοὺς ("to them" - masculine plural) - again referring to the inhabitants.

    Application to Jude 1:7¶

    Could τούτοις (masculine) refer to the inhabitants of Sodom/Gomorrah/cities rather than the cities themselves?

    Passage City/Cities Gender Pronoun/Participle Refers to
    Luke 10:13 Tyre, Sidon Fem καθήμενοι (Masc Pl) Inhabitants sitting
    Matt 23:37 Jerusalem Fem ἠθελήσατε (2pl) Inhabitants not willing
    Acts 8:5 City of Samaria Fem αὐτοῖς (Masc Pl) Inhabitants preached to
    Acts 8:14 Samaria Fem αὐτοὺς (Masc Pl) Inhabitants sent to
    Jude 1:7 Sodom, cities Fem τούτοις (Masc Pl) ?

    Conclusion: The pattern exists in the NT with multiple clear examples. Constructio ad sensum is a grammatically valid and well-attested option. The Acts 8:5 example is particularly strong because it uses the same dative case (αὐτοῖς) as Jude 1:7 (τούτοις).

    Additional Examples: Comprehensive NT Data¶

    More Feminine → Masculine Examples¶

    Passage City/Town (Gender) Referring Word Gender Pattern
    Matt 8:34 πᾶσα ἡ πόλις (F sg) ἰδόντες "seeing" M pl participle F → M
    John 4:39 τῆς πόλεως ἐκείνης (F) πολλοί "many" M pl adj F → M
    Acts 21:30 ἡ πόλις ὅλη (F) ἐπιλαβόμενοι "seizing" M pl participle F → M

    Feminine → Feminine Examples (Strict Agreement)¶

    Passage City/Town (Gender) Referring Word Gender Pattern
    Matt 23:37 Ἰερουσαλήμ (F voc) ἀποκτείνουσα "killing" F sg participle F → F
    Matt 23:37 Ἰερουσαλήμ (F voc) λιθοβολοῦσα "stoning" F sg participle F → F
    Matt 23:37 Ἰερουσαλήμ (F voc) αὐτήν "to her" F sg pronoun F → F
    Mark 1:33 ὅλη ἡ πόλις (F) ἐπισυνηγμένη "gathered" F sg participle F → F
    Rev 11:8 τῆς πόλεως τῆς μεγάλης (F) ἥτις "which" F sg relative F → F

    What Determines the Pattern?¶

    The key question is: Can the action be predicated of a collective entity, or does it require individual agents?

    When F → F (Strict Agreement) is Used¶

    Used when the city is personified as a unified entity:

    Passage Text Why Feminine?
    Matt 23:37 Jerusalem... ἡ ἀποκτείνουσα (she who kills) Deliberate personification - Jerusalem as a mother figure (hen/chicks metaphor follows)
    Mark 1:33 the whole city was ἐπισυνηγμένη (gathered) Collective action - a crowd "gathers" as a unit
    Rev 11:8 the great city ἥτις (which) is called... City as a symbolic entity with a name

    Pattern: Feminine is maintained when the author conceptualizes the city as a whole - a collective personality, a symbolic entity, or when the predicate can naturally apply to a collective noun.

    When F → M (Constructio ad Sensum) is Used¶

    Used when the focus shifts to individual human actions:

    Passage Text Why Masculine?
    Luke 10:13 Tyre and Sidon would have repented καθήμενοι (sitting) in sackcloth Physical posture - people sit, cities don't
    Matt 8:34 the whole city went out... ἰδόντες (seeing) him, they begged Sensory perception - people see, cities don't
    Acts 8:5 preached αὐτοῖς (to them) Receiving a message - people hear, not geography
    Acts 21:30 the city was stirred... ἐπιλαβόμενοι (seizing) Paul Physical action - people grab, cities don't

    Pattern: Masculine appears when the predicate requires individual human agents - seeing, sitting, hearing, seizing, repenting. These actions cannot be predicated of a collective abstraction.

    Summary: The Decisive Factor¶

    Action Type Agreement Example
    "The city gathered" F → F Collective entity can "gather"
    "The city saw him" F → M Individuals see, not collectives
    "The city kills prophets" F → F Personification - city as agent
    "The city sat in sackcloth" F → M Physical posture requires bodies

    Application to Jude 1:7¶

    The question for τούτοις is: What kind of action is "going after strange flesh"?

    • If Jude is thinking of individual human actors committing sexual sin → masculine for inhabitants makes sense (F → M)
    • If τούτοις refers back to angels (v6) → masculine is strict agreement (M → M)

    The action "going after flesh" (ἀπελθοῦσαι... ὀπίσω σαρκός) describes individual pursuit of sexual gratification - an action requiring individual agents, not a collective abstraction. This would support the F → M reading for inhabitants.

    However, this does not rule out the angelic referent - both remain grammatically viable.

    OT Pattern: Cities and Inhabitants¶

    The OT frequently refers to cities by their inhabitants:

    Explicit "Men of [City]" Phrases¶

    • Genesis 13:13 - "the men of Sodom were wicked" - explicit masculine
    • Genesis 19:4 - "the men of the city, even the men of Sodom" - explicit masculine
    • Isaiah 1:10 - "ye rulers of Sodom... ye people of Gomorrah" - explicit reference to inhabitants

    Ezekiel 16:49-50 - Grammatical Analysis¶

    In Ezekiel 16, Sodom is personified as Jerusalem's "sister" (feminine). The Hebrew maintains feminine agreement:

    • Ezekiel 16:50 - וַֽתִּגְבְּהֶ֔ינָה (wattigbəhenāh) = "and they were haughty"
    • Parsing: Qal Consecutive Imperfect - 3rd person FEMININE plural

    This shows the Hebrew OT maintains strict grammatical agreement with feminine cities, unlike the more flexible Koine Greek of the NT.

    Significance¶

    The NT shows more grammatical flexibility (constructio ad sensum) than the Hebrew OT when referring to cities by their inhabitants. This reflects Koine Greek usage patterns and strengthens the case that such constructions were acceptable in NT Greek.

    Contextual Analysis¶

    Jude's Three Examples of Judgment (vv. 5-7)¶

    Jude provides three historical examples of divine judgment:

    1. Israel in the wilderness (v5) - destroyed for unbelief
    2. Angels who sinned (v6) - reserved in chains for judgment
    3. Sodom and Gomorrah (v7) - destroyed by fire as an example

    Structure Question¶

    How does v7 relate to v6?

    Option A (angels reading): - v6 and v7 are connected - Sodom sinned "in like manner to" the angels - Both involved sexual transgression across categories ("strange flesh") - The sin of v7 parallels the sin of v6

    Option B (inhabitants reading): - v7 is self-contained - the surrounding cities sinned like the people of Sodom - The three examples are separate illustrations of judgment - v7 explains how the surrounding cities shared Sodom's sin

    2 Peter 2:4-6 Parallel¶

    Comparison¶

    Element Jude 2 Peter
    Angels v6: "kept not their first estate... left their own habitation" v4: "angels that sinned"
    Connection "in like manner" (τὸν ὅμοιον τρόπον τούτοις) No connecting phrase
    Sodom v7: "giving themselves over to fornication, going after strange flesh" v6: "condemned with an overthrow, making them an ensample"

    Key Observation¶

    2 Peter does NOT connect the angels and Sodom with a comparative phrase.

    2 Peter treats the three examples (angels, flood, Sodom) as parallel but separate illustrations.

    If Peter and Jude drew from a common tradition, Jude's "in like manner" phrase is unique to Jude and represents his interpretive addition.

    Implications¶

    1. If Jude meant to parallel the sins of angels and Sodom, his phrasing is intentional and significant
    2. If Jude meant the surrounding cities sinned like Sodom's inhabitants, the phrase is clarifying the scope of the example

    "Strange Flesh" (σαρκὸς ἑτέρας)¶

    The Word ἕτερος (heteros)¶

    ἕτερος means "other of a different kind" - distinct from ἄλλος which means "another of the same kind."

    What is "Different" Flesh?¶

    Interpretation "Strange flesh" means Parallel to angels?
    Homosexuality Flesh of same sex (different from natural) No direct parallel
    Angelic beings Non-human flesh (Genesis 6 connection) Yes - both crossed categories
    Foreigners Non-Israelite flesh No parallel

    If Reading A (angels):¶

    The "strange flesh" Sodom pursued was angelic flesh (Gen 19 - the angels who visited Lot), paralleling the angels of v6 who pursued human flesh (Gen 6 connection).

    If Reading B (inhabitants):¶

    The "strange flesh" refers to homosexual relations - flesh "different" from what is natural.

    Evaluation of Both Readings¶

    Reading A: τούτοις = Angels¶

    Strengths: - Strict grammatical agreement (masculine → masculine) - Creates theological parallel: both angels and Sodomites crossed flesh-categories - Explains why Jude connects these two examples with "in like manner" - Fits with Genesis 6 / 1 Enoch tradition that Jude likely knew

    Weaknesses: - Requires reading Gen 19 as Sodomites pursuing angelic flesh specifically - Less intuitive reading of the sentence flow - 2 Peter parallel doesn't make this connection

    Reading B: τούτοις = Inhabitants of Cities¶

    Strengths: - Follows documented NT pattern (constructio ad sensum) - More natural sentence flow: "Sodom... and the cities around them in like manner [to Sodom's people]" - Explains scope: not just Sodom, but all the cities sinned similarly - Doesn't require specific view on Gen 6

    Weaknesses: - Requires grammatical gender to shift from feminine to masculine - Why would Jude specify that surrounding cities sinned like Sodom? (Seems obvious) - Leaves the connection to v6 unexplained

    Summary of Grammatical Evidence¶

    Factor Favors Reading A Favors Reading B
    Strict gender agreement ✓
    Constructio ad sensum precedent ✓
    Sentence flow ✓
    Theological coherence ✓
    2 Peter parallel Neither - 2 Peter is silent

    Conclusion: Grammar alone does not definitively resolve the question. Both readings are grammatically defensible.
