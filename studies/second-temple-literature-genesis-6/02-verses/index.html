    Verse Texts: Second Temple Literature and Genesis 6¶

    Primary Texts¶

    Genesis 6:1-4 (The Source Text)¶

    Genesis 6:1 And it came to pass, when men began to multiply on the face of the earth, and daughters were born unto them,

    Genesis 6:2 That the sons of God saw the daughters of men that they [were] fair; and they took them wives of all which they chose.

    Genesis 6:3 And the LORD said, My spirit shall not always strive with man, for that he also [is] flesh: yet his days shall be an hundred and twenty years.

    Genesis 6:4 There were giants in the earth in those days; and also after that, when the sons of God came in unto the daughters of men, and they bare [children] to them, the same [became] mighty men which [were] of old, men of renown.

    Note: These four verses are the ENTIRE biblical text that 1 Enoch 6-16 (eleven chapters) expands upon.

    Jude 14-15 (Quoting 1 Enoch 1:9)¶

    Jude 1:14 And Enoch also, the seventh from Adam, prophesied of these, saying, Behold, the Lord cometh with ten thousands of his saints,

    Jude 1:15 To execute judgment upon all, and to convince all that are ungodly among them of all their ungodly deeds which they have ungodly committed, and of all their hard [speeches] which ungodly sinners have spoken against him.

    Paul Quoting Pagan Sources¶

    Acts 17:28 For in him we live, and move, and have our being; as certain also of your own poets have said, For we are also his offspring.

    Paul quotes Epimenides of Crete ("in him we live and move and have our being") and Aratus of Soli ("for we are also his offspring") -- pagan Greek poets.

    Titus 1:12 One of themselves, [even] a prophet of their own, said, The Cretians [are] alway liars, evil beasts, slow bellies.

    Paul calls Epimenides "a prophet of their own" (prophetes, G4396) -- using the same Greek word used for biblical prophets.

    1 Corinthians 15:33 Be not deceived: evil communications corrupt good manners.

    Paul quotes Menander's comedy Thais -- a pagan theatrical work.

    Supporting Texts -- Moses Strips Pagan Mythology¶

    Genesis 1:14-18 (Luminaries Stripped of Deity Status)¶

    Genesis 1:14 And God said, Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years:

    Genesis 1:15 And let them be for lights in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth: and it was so.

    Genesis 1:16 And God made two great lights; the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night: [he made] the stars also.

    Genesis 1:17 And God set them in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth,

    Genesis 1:18 And to rule over the day and over the night, and to divide the light from the darkness: and God saw that [it was] good.

    Note: Moses calls the sun and moon "lights" (maor, H3974) -- functional terms. In pagan mythology, the sun was Shamash (a deity), the moon was Sin (a deity). Moses strips them of divine identity.

    Genesis 1:21 (Sea Creatures Stripped of Deity Status)¶

    Genesis 1:21 And God created great whales, and every living creature that moveth, which the waters brought forth abundantly, after their kind, and every winged fowl after his kind: and God saw that [it was] good.

    Note: The Hebrew word translated "whales" is tanninim (H8577), which in pagan mythology were divine sea monsters (cf. Tiamat in Enuma Elish). Moses presents them as created beings, not divine rivals.

    Genesis 2:7 (Human Origin Stripped of Pagan Myth)¶

    Genesis 2:7 And the LORD God formed man [of] the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul.

    Note: In the Babylonian Enuma Elish, man is made from the blood of a slain god (Kingu). Moses says man is from dust -- radically de-divinizing human origin.

    Deuteronomy 4:19 (Celestial Worship Forbidden)¶

    Deuteronomy 4:19 And lest thou lift up thine eyes unto heaven, and when thou seest the sun, and the moon, and the stars, [even] all the host of heaven, shouldest be driven to worship them, and serve them, which the LORD thy God hath divided unto all nations under the whole heaven.

    Note: Moses explicitly warns against celestial worship -- treating heavenly bodies as deities is prohibited precisely because other nations had divinized them.

    Genesis 4:26 (Seth's Godly Line Marked)¶

    Genesis 5:21-24 (Enoch the Person)¶

    Genesis 5:21 And Enoch lived sixty and five years, and begat Methuselah:

    Genesis 5:22 And Enoch walked with God after he begat Methuselah three hundred years, and begat sons and daughters:

    Genesis 5:23 And all the days of Enoch were three hundred sixty and five years:

    Genesis 5:24 And Enoch walked with God: and he [was] not; for God took him.

    Isaiah 14:12-15 (Demythologized Canaanite Myth)¶

    Isaiah 14:12 How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning! [how] art thou cut down to the ground, which didst weaken the nations!

    Isaiah 14:13 For thou hast said in thine heart, I will ascend into heaven, I will exalt my throne above the stars of God: I will sit also upon the mount of the congregation, in the sides of the north:

    Isaiah 14:14 I will ascend above the heights of the clouds; I will be like the most High.

    Isaiah 14:15 Yet thou shalt be brought down to hell, to the sides of the pit.

    Note: Isaiah 14 uses imagery that parallels Canaanite mythology about the god Helel (Day Star), but applies it to the king of Babylon and to Satan's fall. This demonstrates the prophetic pattern of using pagan imagery while transforming its meaning.

    Greek Grammatical Analysis¶

    Jude 1:14 -- Greek Text and Parsing¶

    Greek: Ἐπροφήτευσεν δὲ καὶ τούτοις ἕβδομος ἀπὸ Ἀδὰμ Ἑνὼχ λέγων Ἰδοὺ ἦλθεν Κύριος ἐν ἁγίαις μυριάσιν αὐτοῦ,

    Word Lemma Strong's Parsing
    Ἐπροφήτευσεν προφητεύω G4395 Aorist Active Indicative 3rd Singular
    ἕβδομος ἕβδομος G1442 Nominative Singular Masculine ("seventh")
    ἀπὸ Ἀδὰμ ἀπό + Ἀδάμ G575 + G76 "from Adam"
    Ἑνὼχ Ἑνώχ G1802 Nominative Singular Masculine (subject)
    λέγων λέγω G3004 Present Active Participle Nom Sg M ("saying")
    ἦλθεν ἔρχομαι G2064 Aorist Active Indicative 3rd Sg ("came/cometh")
    Κύριος κύριος G2962 Nominative Singular Masculine ("Lord")
    ἁγίαις μυριάσιν ἅγιος + μυριάς G40 + G3461 Dative Plural Feminine ("holy myriads")

    Key Observations: - Ἐπροφήτευσεν (epropheteusen) -- Aorist tense, simple past action: "prophesied" - The subject is Ἑνὼχ (Enoch), identified as ἕβδομος ἀπὸ Ἀδὰμ ("seventh from Adam") - Jude attributes the prophecy to the MAN Enoch, not to the BOOK of 1 Enoch - The verb propheteuo (G4395) means "to speak forth, to prophesy" -- used for genuine prophetic speech

    Jude 1:15 -- Greek Text and Parsing¶

    Greek: ποιῆσαι κρίσιν κατὰ πάντων καὶ ἐλέγξαι πάντας τοὺς ἀσεβεῖς...

    Word Lemma Strong's Parsing
    ποιῆσαι ποιέω G4160 Aorist Active Infinitive ("to execute")
    κρίσιν κρίσις G2920 Accusative Singular Feminine ("judgment")
    ἐλέγξαι ἐλέγχω G1651 Aorist Active Infinitive ("to convict/convince")
    ἀσεβεῖς ἀσεβής G765 Accusative Plural Masculine ("ungodly")
    ἀσεβείας ἀσέβεια G763 Genitive Singular Feminine ("ungodliness")
    ἠσέβησαν ἀσεβέω G764 Aorist Active Indicative 3rd Plural ("they committed ungodly")
    ἁμαρτωλοὶ ἀσεβεῖς ἁμαρτωλός + ἀσεβής G268 + G765 Nom Pl M ("sinners ungodly")

    Key Observations: - The word "ungodly" (aseb-) appears FOUR times in this verse, emphasizing moral judgment - The content of the quoted prophecy is about God coming in judgment against the ungodly - Nothing in the quoted text relates to angels, marriage, or Genesis 6 specifically - The prophecy concerns universal judgment -- a truth consistent with all Scripture

    Acts 17:28 -- Greek Text and Parsing¶

    Greek: ἐν αὐτῷ γὰρ ζῶμεν καὶ κινούμεθα καὶ ἐσμέν, ὡς καί τινες τῶν καθ' ὑμᾶς ποιητῶν εἰρήκασιν Τοῦ γὰρ καὶ γένος ἐσμέν.

    Word Lemma Strong's Parsing
    ποιητῶν ποιητής G4163 Genitive Plural Masculine ("of poets")
    εἰρήκασιν εἶπον G2046 Perfect Active Indicative 3rd Pl ("have said")
    γένος γένος G1085 Nominative Singular Neuter ("offspring/race")

    Key Observations: - Paul calls pagan poets ποιητῶν (poieton, "poets") -- not prophets - The Perfect tense εἰρήκασιν indicates the poets' statement has enduring relevance - Paul uses their TRUE statement ("we are his offspring") without endorsing their theology - Paul does not validate Stoic pantheism by quoting Aratus on Mars Hill

    Titus 1:12 -- Greek Text and Parsing¶

    Greek: εἶπέν τις ἐξ αὐτῶν ἴδιος αὐτῶν προφήτης Κρῆτες ἀεὶ ψεῦσται, κακὰ θηρία, γαστέρες ἀργαί.

    Word Lemma Strong's Parsing
    εἶπέν λέγω G3004 Aorist Active Indicative 3rd Sg ("said")
    ἴδιος ἴδιος G2398 Nominative Singular Masculine ("their own")
    προφήτης προφήτης G4396 Nominative Singular Masculine ("prophet")
    ψεῦσται ψεύστης G5583 Nominative Plural Masculine ("liars")
    θηρία θηρίον G2342 Nominative Plural Neuter ("beasts")
    ἀργαί ἀργός G692 Nominative Plural Feminine ("idle/lazy")

    Key Observations: - Paul calls Epimenides ἴδιος αὐτῶν προφήτης ("their own prophet") -- using the SAME word (prophetes, G4396) that the NT uses for biblical prophets - This is remarkable: Paul applies the title "prophet" to a PAGAN POET - The word prophetes is applied to Epimenides in the same grammatical form as it would be applied to Isaiah or Jeremiah - Paul affirms the TRUTH of Epimenides' statement (v.13: "this witness is true") without endorsing Epimenides' theology or other writings - PARALLEL TO JUDE: Jude says Enoch "prophesied" (same word family, G4395); Paul says Epimenides is a "prophet" (G4396). Both cite a true statement from a non-canonical source without endorsing everything in that source.
