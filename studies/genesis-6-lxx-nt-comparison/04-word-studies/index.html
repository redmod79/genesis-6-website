    Word Studies¶

    υἱός (huios) - G5207¶

    Original: υἱός Transliteration: huios Pronunciation: hwee-os' Definition: A son; used of immediate, remote, or figurative kinship

    Semantic Range¶

    The word huios has a broad semantic range in Greek: 1. Literal son - biological male offspring 2. Descendant - posterity, even remote (e.g., "sons of Abraham") 3. Follower/disciple - those belonging to a group or teacher 4. One characterized by - "sons of disobedience," "sons of light" 5. Adopted son - legal sonship without biological connection 6. Divine relationship - "Son of God" (for Christ and believers)

    Usage in LXX Genesis 6:2¶

    οἱ υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ

    The LXX translators chose huios (son) rather than angelos (angel) to translate the Hebrew בֵּן (ben). This is significant because they had both options available.

    Usage in NT for Human Believers¶

    Passage Greek Context
    Romans 8:14 υἱοὶ θεοῦ Those led by Spirit
    Romans 8:19 τῶν υἱῶν τοῦ θεοῦ Awaiting manifestation
    Galatians 3:26 υἱοὶ θεοῦ By faith in Christ
    Galatians 4:6-7 υἱοί ... υἱός No longer servants

    Key Finding¶

    The word huios is used identically in: - LXX Genesis 6:2, 4 (υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ) - NT passages for human believers (υἱοὶ θεοῦ)

    This establishes that huios theou CAN refer to humans.

    ἄγγελος (angelos) - G32¶

    Original: ἄγγελος Transliteration: angelos Pronunciation: ang'-el-os Definition: Messenger, angel

    Semantic Range¶

    1. Human messenger - one sent with a message
    2. Celestial being - angel, heavenly messenger
    3. Pastor/leader - "angel of the church" (Revelation)

    Usage in LXX Job 1:6, 2:1¶

    οἱ ἄγγελοι τοῦ θεοῦ

    The LXX translators chose angelos (angel) for the Hebrew בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים in Job, but NOT in Genesis 6.

    Why the Distinction?¶

    The LXX translators made a contextual judgment:

    Passage Context Greek Choice
    Genesis 6 Earthly marriage scene υἱοί (sons)
    Job 1-2 Heavenly throne room ἄγγελοι (angels)

    If the translators believed Genesis 6 referred to angels, they would have used ἄγγελοι as they did in Job.

    τέκνον (teknon) - G5043¶

    Original: τέκνον Transliteration: teknon Pronunciation: tek'-non Definition: Child, offspring (as produced)

    Relationship to huios¶

    Both huios (son) and teknon (child) are used for "sons/children of God" in the NT:

    Term Emphasis Example
    huios Legal status, inheritance Romans 8:14
    teknon Origin, relationship 1 John 3:1-2

    NT Usage for Human Believers¶

    • John 1:12: τέκνα θεοῦ ("children of God")
    • Romans 8:16: τέκνα θεοῦ ("children of God")
    • 1 John 3:1-2: τέκνα θεοῦ ("children of God")

    Significance¶

    The NT uses both huios and teknon interchangeably for believers: - Romans 8:14 uses υἱοί (huioi) - Romans 8:16 uses τέκνα (tekna)

    This confirms the phrases are semantically equivalent when referring to human believers.

    σάρξ (sarx) - G4561¶

    Original: σάρξ Transliteration: sarx Pronunciation: sarx Definition: Flesh, body, human nature

    LXX Genesis 6:3¶

    διὰ τὸ εἶναι αὐτοὺς σάρκας "because they are flesh"

    The LXX translators rendered the Hebrew בָּשָׂר (basar) as σάρκας (sarkas), applying it to the "sons of God."

    Significance for Interpretation¶

    The term "flesh" (sarx/σάρξ) in Greek consistently refers to: 1. Human physical body 2. Human nature (especially in contrast to spirit) 3. Mortal, earthly existence

    The LXX calling the "sons of God" flesh strongly supports a human identification. Angels are never called "flesh" in Scripture.

    NT Parallel¶

    In NT usage, "flesh" consistently means human nature: - John 1:14: "The Word was made flesh" (became human) - Romans 8:3: "God sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh" - Galatians 5:16-17: Flesh vs. Spirit (human nature vs. divine)

    Comparative Table: LXX vs NT Terminology¶

    Element LXX Genesis 6 NT (Human Reference) Match?
    "Sons" υἱοί (huioi) υἱοί (huioi) EXACT
    "of God" τοῦ θεοῦ (tou theou) θεοῦ / τοῦ θεοῦ (theou) EXACT
    Full phrase υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ υἱοὶ θεοῦ EQUIVALENT
    Called "flesh" σάρκας (sarkas) σάρξ for humans CONSISTENT

    The LXX Translators' Interpretive Choice¶

    What They Could Have Written¶

    If the LXX translators understood Genesis 6 to refer to angels, they had clear options: 1. ἄγγελοι τοῦ θεοῦ (angeloi tou theou) - "angels of God" (as in Job) 2. οἱ ἄγγελοι (hoi angeloi) - "the angels" 3. ἄγγελοι (angeloi) - "angels"

    What They Actually Wrote¶

    They wrote: οἱ υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ (hoi huioi tou theou) - "the sons of God"

    The Implication¶

    The LXX translators: 1. Knew the word for "angels" (ἄγγελοι) 2. Used it in Job for the same Hebrew phrase 3. Deliberately chose NOT to use it in Genesis 6 4. Selected "sons" (υἱοί) instead

    This is a contextual, interpretive decision - they saw Genesis 6 differently than Job.

    Summary: Word Studies and the Question¶

    The Question¶

    Does NT usage of "sons of God" allow the linguistic range of Genesis 6 "sons of God" to refer to humans?

    The Answer from Word Studies¶

    YES - The word studies demonstrate:

    1. Same Greek word (huios) used in both LXX Genesis 6 and NT for human believers
    2. Same construction (huioi + genitive theou)
    3. Same meaning - "sons of God"
    4. LXX translators distinguished contexts - using "sons" for Genesis 6, "angels" for Job
    5. LXX Genesis 6:3 calls them "flesh" - human terminology
    6. NT proves huioi theou = humans - Romans 8:14; Galatians 3:26

    The linguistic evidence is clear: the phrase οἱ υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ / υἱοὶ θεοῦ ("sons of God") CAN and DOES refer to humans. The LXX and NT use identical terminology.

    Whether Genesis 6 DOES refer to humans is a matter of contextual interpretation, but linguistically, the phrase is fully compatible with human reference - as the NT demonstrates.
