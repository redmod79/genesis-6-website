    Verse Analysis¶

    The Core Question¶

    Does the linguistic terminology in LXX Genesis 6 match NT "sons of God" passages that clearly refer to humans? If so, the phrase's semantic range must include humans.

    Terminology Comparison Analysis¶

    LXX Genesis 6:2, 4¶

    Greek: οἱ υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ Breakdown: - οἱ (hoi) = the (article, nominative plural) - υἱοὶ (huioi) = sons (nominative plural of huios) - τοῦ (tou) = of the (genitive article) - θεοῦ (theou) = God (genitive)

    Construction: Article + "sons" + "of the God"

    NT Romans 8:19¶

    Greek: τῶν υἱῶν τοῦ θεοῦ Breakdown: - τῶν (ton) = of the (genitive article) - υἱῶν (huion) = sons (genitive plural of huios) - τοῦ (tou) = of the (genitive article) - θεοῦ (theou) = God (genitive)

    Construction: (genitive form due to context) + "sons" + "of the God"

    Direct Comparison¶

    Element LXX Gen 6:2 NT Rom 8:19 Match?
    Root word for "sons" υἱός (huios) υἱός (huios) YES
    Word for "God" θεός (theos) θεός (theos) YES
    Construction huioi + tou + theou huion + tou + theou YES
    Meaning sons of God sons of God YES

    Result: EXACT TERMINOLOGICAL MATCH

    The LXX Genesis 6 and NT passages for human believers use the same Greek phrase for "sons of God."

    LXX Translator Choice Analysis¶

    The Translators Had Options¶

    The LXX translators were bilingual scholars who had two obvious choices: 1. υἱοί (huioi) = "sons" - emphasizing relationship/kinship 2. ἄγγελοι (angeloi) = "angels/messengers" - emphasizing celestial nature

    They Made Different Choices Based on Context¶

    Passage Hebrew LXX Greek Choice Context
    Gen 6:2 בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ (sons) Earthly scene, marriage
    Gen 6:4 בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ (sons) Earthly scene, marriage
    Job 1:6 בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים ἄγγελοι τοῦ θεοῦ (angels) Heavenly throne room
    Job 2:1 בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים ἄγγελοι τοῦ θεοῦ (angels) Heavenly throne room
    Job 38:7 בְּנֵי אֱלֹהִים ἄγγελοί μου (my angels) Creation, before humans

    Significance of This Distinction¶

    The same Hebrew phrase (בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים) was translated TWO DIFFERENT WAYS: - Genesis 6: "sons of God" (υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ) - Job: "angels of God" (ἄγγελοι τοῦ θεοῦ)

    This demonstrates that the LXX translators: 1. Interpreted contextually - not mechanically 2. Saw Genesis 6 differently than Job - different Greek words chosen 3. Did NOT understand Genesis 6 as referring to angels - or they would have used angeloi

    NT Evidence: "Sons of God" = Humans¶

    From the baseline study (nt-sons-of-god-humans), the NT unambiguously applies "sons of God" to humans:

    Present Reality (On Earth)¶

    • Romans 8:14: "they ARE the sons of God" (υἱοὶ θεοῦ)
    • Galatians 3:26: "ye ARE all the sons of God by faith" (υἱοὶ θεοῦ)
    • 1 John 3:2: "NOW are we the sons of God" (τέκνα θεοῦ)
    • Philippians 2:15: "sons of God...in the midst of a crooked nation" (τέκνα θεοῦ)

    Future Manifestation¶

    • Romans 8:19: "manifestation of the sons of God" (τῶν υἱῶν τοῦ θεοῦ)
    • 1 John 3:2: "what we shall be...we shall be like him"

    Greek Terms Used¶

    • υἱοὶ θεοῦ (huioi theou) - "sons of God" (Rom 8:14; Gal 3:26)
    • τέκνα θεοῦ (tekna theou) - "children of God" (John 1:12; 1 John 3:1-2)

    Both huios and teknon are used interchangeably for human believers.

    The Linguistic Range Argument¶

    Premise 1: LXX Genesis 6 uses οἱ υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ¶

    This is established from the critical LXX text (Rahlfs-Hanhart).

    Premise 2: NT uses υἱοὶ θεοῦ / τέκνα θεοῦ for humans¶

    This is established from Romans 8:14, Galatians 3:26, 1 John 3:2, etc.

    Premise 3: The terminology is identical¶

    • LXX Gen 6:2: υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ
    • NT Rom 8:19: υἱῶν τοῦ θεοῦ
    • Same word (huios), same genitive construction (tou theou)

    Conclusion: The phrase CAN refer to humans¶

    If the NT uses υἱοὶ θεοῦ for humans, and the LXX Genesis 6 uses the same phrase, then linguistically the phrase in Genesis 6 CAN refer to humans.

    The question of whether it DOES refer to humans in Genesis 6 is a matter of context, not linguistic possibility.

    Contextual Factors in Genesis 6¶

    Supporting Human Reference¶

    1. LXX Gen 6:3 calls them "flesh" (σάρκας) - human terminology
    2. Marriage vocabulary is normal human language - "took wives"
    3. Moses never used "bene elohim" for angels - he used "malak"
    4. LXX translators chose "sons" not "angels" - contextual interpretation

    The Contrast with Job¶

    In Job 1-2, the context is: - Heavenly throne room - Satan appears among them - Presenting before the LORD - No earthly marriage or human activity

    The LXX translators recognized this different context and translated as "angels."

    In Genesis 6, the context is: - Earthly scene - Marriage with "daughters of men" - God limits their days to 120 years - Called "flesh"

    The LXX translators recognized this different context and translated as "sons."

    Patterns Identified¶

    Pattern 1: Terminology Match¶

    LXX Genesis 6 and NT passages for humans use the same Greek terminology for "sons of God."

    Pattern 2: LXX Contextual Distinction¶

    The LXX translators distinguished Genesis 6 from Job based on context, using "sons" for Genesis and "angels" for Job.

    Pattern 3: NT Confirms Human Usage¶

    The NT proves that υἱοὶ θεοῦ CAN and DOES refer to humans, specifically believers.

    Pattern 4: Genesis 6 Context Supports Human Reading¶

    • Called "flesh" (v.3)
    • Normal marriage language
    • Moses's terminology patterns

    Connection to Baseline Study¶

    The NT Sons of God study (nt-sons-of-god-humans) established:

    Finding Relevance to Genesis 6
    Believers ARE sons of God NOW The phrase applies to humans on earth
    They exist "in midst of crooked nation" Human context, not heavenly
    Full manifestation requires change Current sons are still human/mortal
    Greek: υἱοὶ θεοῦ Same as LXX Genesis 6

    The NT proves the phrase "sons of God" (υἱοὶ θεοῦ) is fully compatible with human reference. The LXX using this same phrase in Genesis 6 demonstrates that the translators saw it as compatible with the Genesis 6 context.

    Counter-Argument Consideration¶

    Objection: "The phrases are slightly different"¶

    Some might note: - LXX Gen 6: οἱ υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ (with article) - NT Rom 8:14: υἱοὶ θεοῦ (without article)

    Response:¶

    1. The difference is merely the presence/absence of the article τοῦ before θεοῦ
    2. This is a minor grammatical variation, not a semantic difference
    3. Romans 8:19 DOES include the article: τῶν υἱῶν τοῦ θεοῦ
    4. Both constructions mean "sons of God"
    5. Greek often varies article usage without changing meaning

    The terminology is substantively identical.
