    Word Studies: Septuagint Translation of Genesis 6:2¶

    Hebrew Term: בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים (bene ha'elohim)¶

    Component Words¶

    בֵּן (ben) - Strong's H1121 - Transliteration: ben - Pronunciation: bane - Definition: son, child, descendant - Root: From בָּנָה (banah, H1129) = "to build" - Usage: 4,906 times in KJV

    אֱלֹהִים (elohim) - Strong's H430 - Transliteration: elohim - Pronunciation: el-o-heem - Definition: God, gods, divine beings, judges - Usage: 2,606 times in KJV

    The Full Phrase¶

    בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים = "sons of THE God" or "sons of God"

    All Old Testament Occurrences¶

    Reference Hebrew Context
    Genesis 6:2 בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים Taking wives from daughters of men
    Genesis 6:4 בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים Coming unto daughters of men
    Job 1:6 בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים Presenting before the LORD with Satan
    Job 2:1 בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים Presenting before the LORD with Satan
    Job 38:7 בְּנֵי אֱלֹהִים Shouting for joy at creation

    Total occurrences: 5 (only in Genesis 6 and Job)

    Greek Translation Terms¶

    υἱός (huios) - "Son"¶

    Strong's G5207 - Transliteration: huios - Pronunciation: hwee-os - Definition: son, descendant, offspring - Usage: 382 times in NT

    LXX Usage in Genesis 6:2:

    οἱ υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ = "the sons of God"

    ἄγγελος (angelos) - "Angel/Messenger"¶

    Strong's G32 - Transliteration: angelos - Pronunciation: ang-el-os - Definition: messenger, angel - Root: From ἀγγέλλω (aggello) = "to announce" - Usage: 186 times in NT

    LXX Usage in Job 1:6:

    οἱ ἄγγελοι τοῦ θεοῦ = "the angels of God"

    The Translation Choice Comparison¶

    Genesis 6:2¶

    Hebrew: בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים (bene ha'elohim)

    LXX: οἱ υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ (hoi huioi tou theou)

    Literal: "the sons of the God"

    Translation choice: The LXX translators chose υἱός (son), preserving the Hebrew's "sonship" language.

    Job 1:6¶

    Hebrew: בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים (bene ha'elohim)

    LXX: οἱ ἄγγελοι τοῦ θεοῦ (hoi angeloi tou theou)

    Literal: "the angels/messengers of the God"

    Translation choice: The LXX translators chose ἄγγελος (angel/messenger), interpreting based on the heavenly context.

    Hebrew Term for "Angel": מַלְאָךְ (mal'ak)¶

    Strong's H4397¶

    • Transliteration: mal'ak
    • Pronunciation: mal-awk
    • Definition: messenger, angel
    • Root: Related to work of a messenger
    • Usage: 213 times in OT

    Moses's Usage of mal'ak¶

    The moses-angel-terminology study documented:

    Term Count in Pentateuch
    malak YHWH (angel of the LORD) 14 occurrences
    malak elohim (angel of God) 3 occurrences
    malakim (angels, plural) 4 occurrences
    malak (angel/his angel/etc.) 7 occurrences

    Total: 28+ occurrences of "malak" for celestial beings in the Pentateuch.

    Critical finding: Moses NEVER used "bene elohim" (sons of God) to refer to angels. He had a word for angels (malak) and used it consistently.

    Greek Terms in LXX Comparison¶

    θεός (theos) - "God"¶

    Strong's G2316 - Definition: God, deity - Used in both phrases: - Genesis 6:2: υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ ("sons of God") - Job 1:6: ἄγγελοι τοῦ θεοῦ ("angels of God")

    Grammatical Structure¶

    Passage Greek Structure Literal
    Gen 6:2 οἱ υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ the sons of-the God
    Job 1:6 οἱ ἄγγελοι τοῦ θεοῦ the angels of-the God

    The genitive construction (τοῦ θεοῦ = "of the God") is identical. The difference is solely in the noun: υἱοί (sons) vs. ἄγγελοι (angels).

    Other Relevant Hebrew/Greek Terms¶

    בָּנִים (banim) - "Children/Sons"¶

    Strong's H1121 (plural form)

    Deuteronomy 14:1: בָּנִים אַתֶּם לַיהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם - "You are children to the LORD your God" - Moses calls Israel "children of God" - Same root as "bene" in Genesis 6:2

    τέκνον (teknon) - "Child"¶

    Strong's G5043 - Definition: child, offspring - Used in NT for "children of God" (e.g., John 1:12)

    Note: The LXX in Deuteronomy 14:1 uses υἱοί (sons), same word as Genesis 6:2.

    Summary: What Word Studies Reveal¶

    The Hebrew Original¶

    Finding Evidence
    Same phrase in all 5 occurrences בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים (bene ha'elohim)
    Moses never used this for angels Moses used מַלְאָךְ (mal'ak) for angels
    Moses used "children of God" for Israel Deuteronomy 14:1

    The LXX Translation Choices¶

    Finding Evidence
    Genesis 6: "sons" (υἱοί) Standard LXX text
    Job 1, 2, 38: "angels" (ἄγγελοι) Same Hebrew, different Greek
    Context drove the choice Earthly (sons) vs. heavenly (angels)
    "Angels" in Gen 6 is late variant Codex Alexandrinus, 5th century

    The Significance¶

    The LXX translators were capable scholars who: 1. Knew the difference between υἱός (son) and ἄγγελος (angel) 2. Chose to use different words based on context 3. Did NOT interpret Genesis 6 as referring to angels 4. Did interpret Job as referring to angels

    This contextual distinction is itself evidence that ancient interpreters saw a difference between the Genesis 6 and Job contexts.

    Connection to Previous Studies¶

    genesis-6-sons-of-god Word Studies¶

    That study established: - "bene ha'elohim" occurs only 5 times in OT - Context differs significantly between Genesis and Job - Genesis 6:3 calls the subjects "flesh" (unusual for angels) - Marriage vocabulary in Genesis 6 is normal human language

    moses-angel-terminology Study¶

    That study established: - Moses consistently used "malak" for angels (28+ times) - Moses used "cherubim" for specific celestial class - Moses NEVER used "bene elohim" for angels - If Moses meant angels, why use different terminology?

    This study confirms: The LXX translators recognized Moses's vocabulary and did not impose "angels" on Genesis 6.
