    Word Studies¶

    Kerusso (G2784) - "Preached"¶

    Greek: κηρύσσω (kēryssō) Meaning: To herald, to proclaim as a public crier

    Definition¶

    The verb describes the action of "heralding" or publicly announcing important messages with formality and authority. According to Strong's: "to herald (as a public crier), especially divine truth (the gospel)."

    Usage¶

    • "The public proclamation of the gospel and matters pertaining to it"
    • Made by John the Baptist, Jesus, the apostles, and other teachers
    • Used 61 times in KJV: "preach" (51x), "publish" (5x), "proclaim" (2x)

    Significance for This Study¶

    This is the word for public proclamation - what Noah did for 120 years while building the ark. 2 Peter 2:5 calls Noah "a preacher (kerux, the noun form) of righteousness."

    Pneuma (G4151) - "Spirit" / "Spirits"¶

    Greek: πνεῦμα (pneuma) Meaning: Breath, wind; spirit (human, angelic, or divine)

    Primary Meanings¶

    1. The Holy Spirit - The third person of the Godhead
    2. Human spirit - The vital principle, rational soul
    3. Supernatural beings - Angels, demons, or spirits
    4. Disposition - The influence that governs the soul

    Usage in KJV (385 times)¶

    • "Spirit" (111x) - usually Holy Spirit
    • "Holy Ghost" (89x)
    • "spirit" (47x) - evil spirits
    • "spirit" (49x) - human spirit

    Significance for This Study¶

    In 1 Peter 3:18-19, "pneuma" appears twice: 1. v.18: "quickened by the Spirit" - referring to the Holy Spirit 2. v.19: "spirits in prison" - referring to either fallen angels or departed humans

    The connection "by which" links the Spirit of v.18 to the action of preaching to the spirits of v.19.

    Phylake (G5438) - "Prison"¶

    Greek: φυλακή (phylakē) Meaning: Guard, guarding; prison, place of confinement

    Definition¶

    The term encompasses both the act of guarding and the place of confinement. Can mean: 1. Guard/Watch - Active vigilance 2. Prison - Place where captives are confined (most common - 36 of 47 uses) 3. Time period - A watch of the night

    Etymology¶

    Derived from phylassō (G5442), meaning "to keep, observe, guard."

    Usage in KJV (47 times)¶

    • "prison" (36x)
    • "watch" (6x)
    • "imprisonment" (2x)

    Significance for This Study¶

    The "spirits in prison" are in a place of confinement or restraint. This matches: - 2 Peter 2:4 - angels in "chains of darkness" - Jude 6 - angels in "everlasting chains under darkness"

    Whether these are fallen angels or human dead, they are confined awaiting judgment.

    Poreuomai (G4198) - "Went"¶

    Greek: πορεύομαι (poreuomai) Meaning: To go, to traverse, to travel, to journey

    Definition¶

    The word describes purposeful movement - "to take one's way, betake oneself, set out, depart."

    Usage in KJV (154 times)¶

    • "go" (117x)
    • "depart" (11x)
    • "walk" (9x)

    Significance for This Study¶

    The "going" in v.19 is connected to the Spirit of v.18 by "by which" (en hō). The question is: WHEN did Christ "go" and preach?

    The text specifies: "when once the longsuffering of God waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was a preparing" (v.20).

    This was not a journey between death and resurrection, but a "going" by the Spirit through Noah during the 120-year preparation period.

    Related: Kerux (G2783) - "Preacher"¶

    Greek: κῆρυξ (kērux) Meaning: A herald, public messenger, preacher

    Significance¶

    This is the noun form of kerusso. 2 Peter 2:5 uses this word to describe Noah:

    "saved Noah the eighth person, a preacher (kerux) of righteousness"

    This confirms that Noah was a herald/proclaimer of God's message to his generation.

    En Hō (ἐν ᾧ) - "By Which"¶

    This prepositional phrase in v.19 is crucial. It connects back to "the Spirit" in v.18.

    Grammatical Analysis¶

    • En = "in, by, with" (preposition)
    • Hō = "which" (relative pronoun, dative masculine/neuter singular)

    The antecedent (what "which" refers to) is "Spirit" (pneumati) - the closest appropriate noun.

    Meaning¶

    "By which [Spirit]" - The Spirit by whom Christ was quickened is the same Spirit by whom He went and preached.

    This is consistent with 1 Peter 1:11: "the Spirit of Christ which was in them" (the prophets).

    Summary Table¶

    Greek Term Meaning Significance
    Kerusso To herald, proclaim Noah proclaimed God's message for 120 years
    Pneuma Spirit Links v.18 to v.19; Christ worked by His Spirit through Noah
    Phylake Prison, confinement The disobedient are now confined awaiting judgment
    Poreuomai To go, journey The "going" was by the Spirit during Noah's time
    En hō By which Connects the Spirit to the action of preaching
    Kerux Herald, preacher Noah was "a preacher of righteousness" (2 Pet 2:5)

    Conclusion¶

    The Greek vocabulary supports the interpretation that Christ, BY HIS SPIRIT, preached THROUGH NOAH to those who are now "spirits in prison." The word study reveals:

    1. The preaching (kerusso) was public proclamation - what Noah did
    2. The "Spirit" (pneuma) is the agent of the action
    3. The "prison" (phylake) is where the disobedient are NOW confined
    4. The "going" (poreuomai) was by the Spirit, during Noah's time
    5. "By which" (en hō) grammatically links back to the Spirit
