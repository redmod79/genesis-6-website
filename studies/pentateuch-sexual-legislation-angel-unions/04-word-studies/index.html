    Word Studies: Pentateuch Sexual Legislation and Angel-Human Unions¶

    1. Zanah (H2181) -- Sexual Immorality / Whoredom¶

    Lexicon Entry¶

    H2181: zanah (zaw-naw) - Part of speech: Verb - Definition: A primitive root; to commit adultery (usually of the female, and less often of simple fornication); figuratively, to commit idolatry (the Jewish people being regarded as the spouse of Jehovah) - KJV translations: commit fornication, harlot, whore, whoredom, go a-whoring, whorish - Total occurrences: 107 (93 unique verse references)

    Pentateuch Usage¶

    Reference Translation Context
    Gen 34:31 "as with an harlot" Dinah's brothers' response
    Gen 38:15 "an harlot" Judah mistook Tamar
    Gen 38:24 "played the harlot" / "by whoredom" Tamar accused
    Exo 34:15 "go a whoring after their gods" Idolatry warning
    Exo 34:16 "go a whoring after their gods" (x2) Intermarriage danger
    Lev 17:7 "gone a whoring" Sacrificing to devils
    Lev 19:29 "to cause her to be a whore" / "fall to whoredom" Prohibition
    Lev 20:5 "go a whoring" After Molech
    Lev 20:6 "go a whoring" After familiar spirits
    Lev 21:7 "a whore" Priest must not marry
    Lev 21:9 "playing the whore" Priest's daughter
    Lev 21:14 "an harlot" High priest must not marry
    Num 15:39 "go a whoring" After own heart/eyes
    Num 25:1 "commit whoredom" With daughters of Moab
    Deu 22:21 "play the whore" In father's house
    Deu 23:18 "a whore" Revenue not to enter temple
    Deu 31:16 "go a whoring" After strange gods

    Key Observations¶

    1. In the Pentateuch, zanah is used for human sexual sin and figurative idolatry -- never for angel-human contact.
    2. The figurative use (idolatry as spiritual whoredom) is prominent in the intermarriage warnings (Exo 34:15-16; Deu 31:16). This confirms that Moses saw intermarriage with pagans as a spiritual/religious danger.
    3. Numbers 25:1 uses zanah for Israel's whoredom "with the daughters of Moab" -- human-to-human sexual immorality linked to pagan worship.
    4. The word is never applied to any interaction between humans and celestial beings.

    2. Toebah (H8441) -- Abomination¶

    Lexicon Entry¶

    H8441: toebah (to-ay-baw) - Part of speech: Feminine noun - Definition: Something disgusting (morally); an abhorrence; especially idolatry or (concretely) an idol - KJV translations: abomination, abominable (custom/thing) - Total occurrences: 127 (117 unique verse references)

    Leviticus 18-20 Usage¶

    Reference Context
    Lev 18:22 "Thou shalt not lie with mankind, as with womankind: it [is] abomination"
    Lev 18:26 "shall not commit [any] of these abominations"
    Lev 18:27 "all these abominations have the men of the land done"
    Lev 18:29 "whosoever shall commit any of these abominations"
    Lev 18:30 "these abominable customs, which were committed before you"
    Lev 20:13 "both of them have committed an abomination"

    Deuteronomy Usage in Sexual/Marriage Context¶

    Reference Context
    Deu 7:25 "it [is] an abomination to the LORD" (pagan idols)
    Deu 7:26 "bring an abomination into thine house"
    Deu 22:5 "all that do so [are] abomination unto the LORD" (cross-dressing)
    Deu 23:18 "both these [are] abomination unto the LORD" (whore's hire)
    Deu 24:4 "that [is] abomination before the LORD" (remarriage after divorce)
    Deu 25:16 "all that do unrighteously, [are] an abomination unto the LORD"
    Deu 27:15 "an abomination unto the LORD" (graven images)

    Key Observations¶

    1. Toebah is Moses's strongest term of moral condemnation. It is applied to homosexuality (Lev 18:22), idolatry, and the sum of all sexual violations listed in Leviticus 18.
    2. The term covers the entire sexual legislation. Leviticus 18:26-30 applies toebah to ALL the prohibited acts collectively.
    3. If angel-human unions had occurred and caused the flood, they would be the supreme toebah -- yet the word is never applied to angel-human contact.
    4. The absence of toebah applied to angel-human contact is significant because Moses applies it to less catastrophic sexual sins (under the angel view's logic).

    3. Tebel (H8397) -- Confusion / Perversion (Bestiality Term)¶

    Lexicon Entry¶

    H8397: tebel (teh-bel) - Part of speech: Masculine noun - Definition: Mixture; unnatural bestiality; confusion - KJV translations: confusion - Total occurrences: 2 (only in Leviticus)

    Usage¶

    Reference Text Context
    Lev 18:23 "it [is] confusion" Bestiality prohibition
    Lev 20:12 "they have wrought confusion" Man lying with daughter-in-law

    Key Observations¶

    1. Tebel specifically denotes unnatural mixing -- crossing a category boundary that should not be crossed.
    2. Moses coined a specialized term for cross-species sexual violation (bestiality). This shows he had the linguistic capacity to address cross-category sexual sins.
    3. If angel-human unions crossed the human-celestial boundary, Moses had the conceptual framework (tebel = unnatural mixture) and could have extended it or created an equivalent term. He did not.
    4. The Hebrew parser confirms Leviticus 18:23 uses tebel in a construct with behemah (beast/cattle), indicating Moses specifically identified the non-human party. Had he wanted to address angel-human contact, he could have similarly specified malak (angel) or any celestial term.

    4. Yabam (H2992) / Yebemeth (H2994) -- Levirate Marriage Terms¶

    Lexicon Entries¶

    H2992: yabam (yaw-bam) - Part of speech: Verb - Definition: To marry a (deceased) brother's widow; perform the duty of a husband's brother - Total occurrences: 3 (Gen 38:8; Deu 25:5,7)

    H2994: yebemeth (yeb-ay-meth) - Part of speech: Feminine noun - Definition: A sister-in-law; brother's wife - Total occurrences: 5 (Gen 38:8; Deu 25:7,9; Ruth 1:15)

    Why This Matters¶

    1. Moses created specialized terminology for a very specific marriage category (levirate marriage).
    2. He legislated detailed procedures for what happens when a man refuses levirate duty (Deu 25:7-10: public shaming, shoe removal, spitting).
    3. This demonstrates Moses's legislative thoroughness. He did not just address broad categories -- he addressed very specific subcategories of marriage and created vocabulary for them.
    4. The contrast is stark: Moses legislated detailed procedures for a brother-in-law who refuses to marry his deceased brother's wife, but said nothing about the supposedly most catastrophic sexual sin in history (angel-human unions).

    5. Laqach (H3947) -- "To Take" (Marriage Verb)¶

    Significance¶

    Both Genesis 6:2 and Deuteronomy 7:3 use laqach (to take) in the context of marriage:

    • Genesis 6:2: "wayyiqhu lahem nashim" -- "and they took for themselves wives"
    • Deuteronomy 7:3: "lo-tiqqach livneka" -- "thou shalt not take [his daughter] for thy son"

    This is standard human marriage vocabulary. The same verb is used for: - Abraham sending for a wife for Isaac (Gen 24:3-4) - Esau taking wives (Gen 26:34; 28:9) - Jacob's marriages (Gen 28:1-2)

    The linguistic parallel between Genesis 6:2 and Deuteronomy 7:3 suggests Moses understood both passages as describing human marriages -- one describing what happened before the flood, the other legislating to prevent the same pattern.

    6. Chathan (H2859) -- "Make Marriage Alliance" (Deuteronomy 7:3)¶

    Hebrew Parser Output¶

    Deuteronomy 7:3 uses tithchatten (Hithpael imperfect of chathan): - Root: chathan (H2859) -- to become a son-in-law; make a marriage alliance - Form: Hithpael = reflexive/reciprocal -- "make yourselves marriage-related"

    This is specifically a term for intermarriage between families/peoples. It is used in the context of Israel's relationship with pagan nations. The Hithpael form emphasizes the mutual nature of the marriage alliance -- two groups joining together.

    This is the same social dynamic described in Genesis 6:2: one group (sons of God) taking wives from another group (daughters of men). The vocabulary and social pattern match the human intermarriage reading.
