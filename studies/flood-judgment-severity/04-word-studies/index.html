    Word Studies: Severity of the Flood Judgment¶

    Primary Hebrew Terms¶

    שָׁחַת (shachath) - "Corrupt/Destroy"¶

    Strong's H7843

    • Transliteration: shachath
    • Pronunciation: shaw-khath'
    • Part of Speech: Verb (primitive root)

    Definition: To decay; causatively to ruin (literally or figuratively). The lexicon defines it as encompassing destruction, corruption, and deterioration across physical and moral dimensions.

    Key Usage Forms: - Niphal: "be marred, spoiled, corrupted" - Piel: "spoil, ruin; pervert, corrupt morally" - Hiphil: "spoil, destroy; corrupt morally"

    KJV Translation Frequency: - "destroy" - 96 occurrences - "corrupt" - 22 occurrences - "mar" - 7 occurrences - "spoil" - 3 occurrences - Total: 147 times across 136 verses

    Genesis 6 Usage:

    Verse Hebrew Translation
    6:11 נִשְׁחֲתָה (nishchatah) "was corrupt"
    6:12 הִשְׁחִית (hishchith) "had corrupted"

    Parallel Uses (Moral Context):

    Exodus 32:7 - "thy people... have corrupted [shachath] themselves" (golden calf - moral)

    Deuteronomy 4:16 - "Lest ye corrupt [shachath] yourselves, and make you a graven image" (moral/spiritual)

    Deuteronomy 31:29 - "ye will utterly corrupt [shachath] yourselves" (moral)

    Judges 2:19 - "they corrupted [shachath] themselves more than their fathers" (moral)

    Finding: Shachath consistently refers to moral/spiritual corruption in Scripture. Never used for genetic or biological corruption.

    תָּמִים (tamim) - "Perfect"¶

    Strong's H8549

    • Transliteration: tamim
    • Pronunciation: taw-meem'
    • Part of Speech: Adjective
    • Root Etymology: From H8552 (tamam)

    Definition: "Entire" (literally, figuratively, or morally); also (as noun) integrity, truth. Conveys completeness, wholeness, and moral integrity.

    Key Usage Categories: 1. Physical completeness: whole, entire, sound 2. Health/quality: wholesome, unimpaired (especially sacrificial animals) 3. Temporal: complete spans of time 4. Moral/spiritual: unimpaired integrity, innocence, sincerity

    KJV Translation Frequency: - "without blemish" - 44 occurrences - "perfect" - 18 occurrences - "upright" - 8 occurrences - Total: 91 times

    Genesis 6:9 Usage:

    "Noah was a just man [and] perfect [tamim] in his generations"

    Parallel Uses (Moral Context):

    Genesis 17:1 - "walk before me, and be thou perfect [tamim]" (to Abraham - moral command)

    Deuteronomy 18:13 - "Thou shalt be perfect [tamim] with the LORD thy God" (moral/spiritual)

    Psalm 18:23 - "I was also upright before him... I kept myself from mine iniquity" (Parallel to tamim = moral)

    Psalm 37:37 - "Mark the perfect [tamim] man, and behold the upright" (moral character)

    Finding: Tamim describes moral/spiritual integrity, not genetic purity. In every parallel usage, it refers to righteous character, not biological qualities.

    חָמָס (chamas) - "Violence"¶

    Strong's H2555

    • Transliteration: chamas
    • Pronunciation: khaw-mawce'
    • Part of Speech: Masculine noun
    • Root: From H2554 (chamas)

    Definition: Violence; by implication, wrong; by metonymy, unjust gain. Encompasses physical brutality, wrongful conduct, harsh treatment, and ill-gotten gains.

    KJV Translation Frequency: - "violence" - 39 occurrences - "violent" - 7 occurrences - "cruelty" - 4 occurrences - "wrong" - 3 occurrences - Total: 60 times

    Genesis 6 Usage:

    Verse Hebrew Translation
    6:11 חָמָס (chamas) "violence"
    6:13 חָמָס (chamas) "violence"

    Genesis 6:11 - "the earth was filled with violence [chamas]" Genesis 6:13 - "the earth is filled with violence [chamas] through them"

    Finding: Chamas is clearly behavioral/moral - referring to violent actions between humans, not genetic states.

    רַע (ra) / רָעָה (ra'ah) - "Wickedness/Evil"¶

    Strong's H7451

    • Transliteration: ra / ra'ah
    • Pronunciation: rah / raw-aw'
    • Part of Speech: Noun/Adjective

    Definition: Bad or evil (natural and moral); adversity, affliction, calamity; evil, mischief, wickedness.

    Genesis 6:5 Usage:

    "GOD saw that the wickedness [ra'ah] of man was great in the earth"

    Finding: This is quintessential moral vocabulary - the standard Hebrew term for moral evil.

    The Phrase "In His Generations"¶

    בְּדֹרֹתָיו (bedorotav)¶

    Components: - בְּ (be) - "in/among" - דֹּר (dor) - "generation, period, age" - תָיו (-tav) - "his" (suffix)

    Meaning: "Among his contemporaries" or "in his time/period"

    Compare Genesis 7:1:

    "thee have I seen righteous before me in this generation"

    Finding: The phrase means Noah was righteous compared to his contemporaries, not genetically distinct from them.

    Greek Terms (New Testament References)¶

    ἀσεβής (asebes) - "Ungodly"¶

    Strong's G765

    Used in 2 Peter 2:5:

    "bringing in the flood upon the world of the ungodly [asebeis]"

    Definition: Irreverent, impious, wicked

    Finding: Peter uses moral vocabulary to describe those destroyed by the flood.

    Summary: Word Studies and Their Implications¶

    All Key Terms Are Moral, Not Genetic¶

    Term Strong's Meaning Category
    shachath H7843 corrupt, ruin Moral
    tamim H8549 complete, having integrity Moral
    chamas H2555 violence, wrong Moral
    ra'ah H7451 wickedness, evil Moral
    asebes G765 ungodly, impious Moral

    The "Genetic Corruption" Claim¶

    Claim: "Corrupt" (shachath) refers to genetic contamination.

    Evidence: - Shachath is used for moral corruption in Exodus 32:7, Deuteronomy 4:16, Judges 2:19 - Never used for genetic or biological processes - The context of Genesis 6 pairs it with "violence" and "wickedness" - moral terms

    Claim: "Perfect in his generations" (tamim bedorotav) means genetically pure.

    Evidence: - Tamim means moral integrity in Genesis 17:1, Deuteronomy 18:13, Psalm 37:37 - Used for sacrificial animals ("without blemish") but referring to physical soundness, not genetics - "In his generations" means "among his contemporaries" (cf. Gen 7:1)

    Conclusion from Word Studies¶

    Every Hebrew word used to describe the pre-flood condition points to moral corruption, not genetic contamination:

    1. Wickedness (ra'ah) - moral evil
    2. Corrupt (shachath) - moral ruin
    3. Violence (chamas) - behavioral wickedness
    4. Perfect (tamim) - moral integrity

    The "genetic corruption" interpretation requires reading modern concepts into ancient Hebrew vocabulary that never carried such meaning.
