    Word Study: "Strange Flesh" (σαρκὸς ἑτέρας) - Jude 1:7¶

    The Phrase¶

    Greek: σαρκὸς ἑτέρας (sarkos heteras) KJV: "strange flesh" Literal: "flesh of a different kind"

    Context (Jude 1:7): "Even as Sodom and Gomorrha, and the cities about them in like manner, giving themselves over to fornication, and going after strange flesh, are set forth for an example, suffering the vengeance of eternal fire."

    Word Study: ἕτερος (heteros) - G2087¶

    Definition¶

    • Strong's: "of uncertain affinity; (an-, the) other or different"
    • Part of Speech: Adjective
    • NT Occurrences: 99

    Critical Distinction: ἕτερος vs ἄλλος¶

    Word Strong's Meaning Nuance
    ἕτερος (heteros) G2087 other, different Different in KIND or nature
    ἄλλος (allos) G243 another, other Another of the SAME kind

    This distinction is significant. When Greek writers wanted to emphasize qualitative difference, they used ἕτερος. When referring to another of the same type, they used ἄλλος.

    KJV Translation of ἕτερος (G2087)¶

    • "other" (43x)
    • "another" (42x)
    • "else" (4x)
    • "next day" (3x)
    • "strange" (1x) - only in Jude 1:7

    Key Observation: The KJV translates ἕτερος as "strange" only once in the entire NT - in Jude 1:7. This suggests the translators recognized something unusual about the "otherness" here.

    Word Study: σάρξ (sarx) - G4561¶

    Definition¶

    • Strong's: "flesh (as stripped of the skin)"
    • Part of Speech: Noun, Feminine
    • NT Occurrences: 147

    Semantic Range¶

    1. Physical body/flesh - the material of the body
    2. Human nature - humanity in general
    3. Sinful nature - the flesh as opposed to the Spirit
    4. Kinship - "my flesh" = my relatives

    Comparative Analysis: 1 Corinthians 15:39¶

    1 Corinthians 15:39 "All flesh [is] not the same flesh: but [there is] one [kind of] flesh of men, another [ἄλλη] flesh of beasts, another [ἄλλη] of fishes, [and] another [ἄλλη] of birds."

    Greek Words Used¶

    English Greek Strong's Meaning
    "another" (3x) ἄλλη (alle) G243 another of same category

    Key Observation: When Paul discusses different categories of flesh (human, animal, fish, bird), he uses ἄλλος (G243) - "another of the same kind." All are flesh; they differ within the category of "flesh."

    Contrast with Jude: Jude uses ἕτερος (G2087) - "different in kind." This suggests flesh that is not merely a different type within the same category, but flesh of an entirely different nature or order.

    Romans 1:26-27 - Different Terminology¶

    Romans 1:26-27 "For this cause God gave them up unto vile affections: for even their women did change the natural use into that which is against nature [παρὰ φύσιν]: And likewise also the men, leaving the natural use of the woman, burned in their lust one toward another; men with men working that which is unseemly..."

    Key Phrase¶

    • Greek: παρὰ φύσιν (para physin)
    • Meaning: "against nature" or "contrary to nature"

    Observation: When Paul describes homosexual relations, he does NOT use ἕτερος or σαρκὸς ἑτέρας. He uses παρὰ φύσιν ("against nature"). This is different terminology from Jude's "strange flesh."

    Passage Greek Phrase Literal Concept
    Jude 1:7 σαρκὸς ἑτέρας "flesh of different kind" Different category of flesh
    Rom 1:26 παρὰ φύσιν "against nature" Contrary to natural function

    OT Background: Genesis 19:1-11¶

    The Visitors Were Angels¶

    Genesis 19:1 "And there came two angels to Sodom at even..."

    Genesis 19:5 "And they called unto Lot, and said unto him, Where are the men which came in to thee this night? bring them out unto us, that we may know them."

    Genesis 19:10-11 "But the men put forth their hand, and pulled Lot into the house... And they smote the men that were at the door of the house with blindness..."

    Observations¶

    1. The visitors are explicitly called angels (מַלְאָכִים / ἄγγελοι) in v1 and v15
    2. The men of Sodom sought to "know" them (sexual intent, cf. Gen 4:1)
    3. The angels displayed supernatural power (blindness)

    If Reading A ("angels" interpretation): The "strange flesh" Sodom pursued was angelic flesh - flesh of a completely different order than human flesh. This would make ἕτερος ("different in kind") highly appropriate.

    Note on Genesis 6:1-4¶

    Some interpreters connect Jude 1:6-7 to Genesis 6:1-4 ("sons of God" taking human wives), arguing: - v6 = angels pursuing human flesh - v7 = humans pursuing angelic flesh (a "mirror" of the same sin)

    However, this connection is NOT established by Jude's text:

    1. Jude 6 describes rebellion, not sexual sin - "first estate" (arche) = position/rank; "habitation" (oiketerion) = heaven. The related study (jude-6-7-angels-sin) concludes this refers to the angelic rebellion (Rev 12 parallel), not Genesis 6.

    2. The Genesis 6 interpretation is a separate question - Whether "sons of God" in Genesis 6 are angels is debated and must be established from Genesis 6 itself, not imported from Jude.

    3. Jude does not mention Genesis 6 - He does not reference wives, offspring, or Nephilim.

    This word study does not assume the Genesis 6 connection. The angelic dimension in Jude 1:7 comes from Genesis 19:1 (the visitors were angels), not from Genesis 6.

    OT Background: Leviticus 18:22-23¶

    Leviticus 18:22 "Thou shalt not lie with mankind, as with womankind: it is abomination [תּוֹעֵבָה - toebah]."

    Leviticus 18:23 "Neither shalt thou lie with any beast to defile thyself therewith: neither shall any woman stand before a beast to lie down thereto: it is confusion [תֶּבֶל - tebel]."

    Hebrew Word Study: תֶּבֶל (tebel) - H8397¶

    • Definition: "mixture, i.e. unnatural bestiality; confusion"
    • Occurrences: 2 (Lev 18:23; 20:12)
    • Concept: Mixing of categories that should not be mixed

    Observation: The OT uses different terms for homosexuality (תּוֹעֵבָה - "abomination") versus bestiality (תֶּבֶל - "confusion/mixture"). The concept of תֶּבֶל involves crossing species boundaries - a mixing of different kinds of flesh.

    This OT concept of "mixture/confusion" (crossing flesh categories) aligns with ἕτερος ("different in kind") better than with homosexuality.

    Summary: Two Viable Readings¶

    Reading 1: Homosexuality Only¶

    "Strange flesh" = flesh "different" from the natural counterpart (men pursuing men instead of women).

    Strengths: - Simpler reading - Judges 19 parallel shows same sin pattern without angels - Ezekiel 16:50 confirms Sodom committed "abomination" (toebah)

    Challenge: - Why use ἕτερος ("different kind") rather than παρὰ φύσιν ("against nature") like Paul?

    Reading 2: Both/And¶

    "Strange flesh" encompasses both: - Subjective: Homosexual intent (they wanted males, refused females) - Objective: Angelic flesh (Jude knows the visitors were angels)

    Strengths: - Explains ἕτερος - the flesh WAS categorically different (angelic) - Explains why Jude chose Sodom over Gibeah - Maintains the homosexual element

    Challenge: - More complex interpretation

    Linguistic Conclusion¶

    The word ἕτερος (G2087) specifically denotes qualitative difference - something of a different category or nature, not merely "another" of the same type.

    • ἄλλος (G243): another apple in the basket (same kind)
    • ἕτερος (G2087): an orange instead of an apple (different kind)

    Jude's choice of σαρκὸς ἑτέρας ("flesh of a different kind") could mean: - Reading 1: Flesh different from the natural counterpart (same-sex) - Reading 2: Flesh that was objectively of a different order (angelic)

    What is certain: The homosexual element is present (Gen 19:4-9; Ezek 16:50).

    What is debated: Whether Jude is also highlighting the angelic dimension.
