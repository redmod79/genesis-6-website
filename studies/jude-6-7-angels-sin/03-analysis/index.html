    Analysis of Jude 6-7¶

    Methodology¶

    This analysis will: 1. Examine what Jude actually states 2. Use Revelation 12 as the primary interpretive parallel (per 2 Peter 2:4 study) 3. Carefully analyze the Greek grammar of "in like manner" 4. Address the "strange flesh" question 5. Evaluate the Genesis 6 connection

    Jude's Purpose and Structure¶

    The Purpose¶

    Jude writes to warn about false teachers who have "crept in unawares" (v.4). His argument: God has judged such rebels before, and will again.

    Three Examples of Judgment (vv. 5-7)¶

    Example Sin Judgment
    Israel in wilderness Unbelief "destroyed them that believed not"
    Angels "kept not... left" "reserved in everlasting chains"
    Sodom & Gomorrah Fornication, strange flesh "suffering the vengeance of eternal fire"

    The Application (v.8)¶

    "Likewise also these [filthy] dreamers defile the flesh, despise dominion, and speak evil of dignities."

    The false teachers follow the pattern of all three examples.

    Analysis of Jude 6: The Angels' Sin¶

    The Text¶

    "And the angels which kept not their first estate, but left their own habitation, he hath reserved in everlasting chains under darkness unto the judgment of the great day."

    Key Phrase 1: "Kept not their first estate"¶

    Greek: τὴν ἑαυτῶν ἀρχὴν (ten heauton archen) - arche (G746) = beginning, origin, principality, rule, domain

    Meaning options: 1. Did not keep their original position/rank 2. Did not keep their proper domain/sphere of authority 3. Did not keep their original nature

    Revelation 12 parallel:

    "And prevailed not; neither was their place found any more in heaven" (Rev 12:8)

    The angels lost their POSITION in heaven through rebellion. This matches "kept not their first estate" - they abandoned their proper rank/domain by following Satan.

    Key Phrase 2: "Left their own habitation"¶

    Greek: ἀπολιπόντας τὸ ἴδιον οἰκητήριον (apolipontas to idion oiketerion) - oiketerion (G3613) = dwelling place, habitation

    Usage: Only appears twice in NT: 1. Jude 6 - angels left their "habitation" 2. 2 Cor 5:2 - believers groan for our heavenly "habitation" (body)

    Common interpretation: Because oiketerion refers to a body in 2 Cor 5:2, angels left their "angelic body" to take human form.

    Problem with this interpretation: 1. Context determines meaning - the contexts are very different 2. 2 Cor 5:2 is about resurrection bodies; Jude 6 is about rebellion 3. This requires importing 2 Cor 5:2's meaning into Jude 6

    Revelation 12 parallel:

    "he was cast out into the earth, and his angels were cast out with him" (Rev 12:9)

    The angels left heaven - their proper dwelling place. They were cast out to earth. This matches "left their own habitation" without requiring a change of bodily form.

    Key Phrase 3: "Reserved in everlasting chains under darkness"¶

    From 2 Peter 2:4 study: - "Chains of darkness" describes spiritual state, not physical prison - Revelation 12 shows "cast down" angels who remain ACTIVE - "Reserved unto judgment" = "knoweth he hath but a short time"

    The angels are spiritually condemned (in darkness, bound to judgment) but not necessarily inactive.

    Summary of Jude 6¶

    Jude 6, interpreted through Revelation 12, describes: 1. Angels who rebelled with Satan 2. They abandoned their proper position/rank in heaven 3. They were cast out of their heavenly dwelling 4. They are spiritually condemned, awaiting judgment 5. No reference to Genesis 6 or taking human wives

    Analysis of Jude 7: "In Like Manner" and "Strange Flesh"¶

    The Text¶

    "Even as Sodom and Gomorrha, and the cities about them in like manner, giving themselves over to fornication, and going after strange flesh, are set forth for an example, suffering the vengeance of eternal fire."

    The Critical Question: What Does "In Like Manner" Connect?¶

    Greek: τὸν ὅμοιον τρόπον τούτοις (ton homoion tropon toutois) - "in like manner to these"

    Three possible readings:

    Option A: Sodom sinned like the angels (sexual sin connection)¶

    Reading: "Sodom, in like manner [as the angels], giving themselves to fornication..."

    This would mean: - The angels committed sexual sin - Sodom committed similar sexual sin - Both went after "strange flesh"

    Problems: 1. The grammar doesn't clearly support this 2. "toutois" (these) is ambiguous - what does it refer to? 3. This requires assuming the angels' sin was sexual (not stated in Jude 6)

    Option B: The surrounding cities sinned like Sodom¶

    Reading: "Sodom and Gomorrah, and the cities about them in like manner..."

    This would mean: - The surrounding cities sinned in the same manner as Sodom - All engaged in fornication and strange flesh

    Support: 1. Grammatically natural - "the cities about them in like manner" 2. "toutois" refers to Sodom and Gomorrah 3. Doesn't require assuming the angels' sin

    Option C: All three examples illustrate judgment "in like manner"¶

    Reading: All three examples (Israel, angels, Sodom) show God judging sin in a similar pattern.

    Support: 1. Jude's point is about God's judgment pattern 2. V.8 says "Likewise also these dreamers" - connecting to all three

    Grammatical Analysis¶

    The phrase τὸν ὅμοιον τρόπον τούτοις most naturally modifies "the cities about them":

    "Sodom and Gomorrah, and the cities about them [sinning] in like manner [to them]..."

    The surrounding cities (Admah, Zeboim - Deut 29:23) committed the same sins as Sodom and Gomorrah. This is the most straightforward grammatical reading.

    What Is "Strange Flesh" (heteras sarkos)?¶

    Greek: σαρκὸς ἑτέρας (sarkos heteras) - heteros (G2087) = other, different, another of a different kind - sarx (G4561) = flesh

    The interpretive question: What makes the flesh "different/strange"?

    Option A: Humans pursuing angelic flesh (cross-species)¶

    If Sodom's sin was pursuing the angels (Gen 19), "different flesh" = angelic vs human.

    Problems: 1. Genesis 19 shows the men of Sodom didn't know the visitors were angels 2. They wanted to "know" what appeared to be MEN 3. Lot offered his daughters as substitutes - suggesting the desire was sexual, not angel-specific 4. The men refused the daughters - their sin was homosexuality, not angel-pursuit

    Option B: Men pursuing male flesh (homosexuality)¶

    "Strange/different flesh" = pursuing the same sex instead of the opposite sex.

    Support: 1. Genesis 19:4-5 - men wanted the male visitors 2. Genesis 19:8 - Lot offered daughters, men refused 3. Romans 1:26-27 - describes homosexuality as "against nature" 4. The word "heteros" means "different" - men pursuing men is pursuing flesh "different" from the natural (female)

    Key insight: From the perspective of natural sexuality, men pursuing men IS pursuing "different/other" flesh - flesh that is not the natural counterpart (female).

    Option C: Connection to Genesis 6 angels taking wives¶

    If the angels in Jude 6 = angels of Genesis 6, and if "in like manner" connects to them, then "strange flesh" might mean angels pursuing human flesh.

    Problems: 1. Requires multiple assumptions 2. Jude 6 doesn't mention Genesis 6 3. The "in like manner" grammar doesn't clearly support this 4. Reverses the direction - in Gen 19, humans pursued angels, not angels pursuing humans

    The Question of Jude Quoting 1 Enoch¶

    The Fact¶

    Jude 14-15 quotes material that appears in 1 Enoch 1:9.

    Does This Endorse 1 Enoch's Theology?¶

    No, for these reasons:

    1. Quoting doesn't equal endorsing the entire source
    2. Paul quoted pagan poets (Acts 17:28; Titus 1:12)

    3. This doesn't mean Paul endorsed Greek paganism

    4. Jude may be quoting oral tradition, not the book

    5. Enoch was a historical person (Gen 5:21-24)

    6. A genuine prophecy could exist independently of the book

    7. Scripture is the authority, not 1 Enoch

    8. 1 Enoch elaborates extensively on angel-human marriages
    9. But Jude does NOT include these details

    10. If Jude wanted to teach the 1 Enoch angel view, why not include those details?

    11. Jude's description fits Revelation 12

    12. "kept not their first estate" = lost their position
    13. "left their own habitation" = cast out of heaven
    14. No need to import 1 Enoch's interpretation

    Does Jude Support the Genesis 6 Angel Interpretation?¶

    The Argument For¶

    1. Jude mentions angels (v.6) then Sodom (v.7)
    2. "In like manner" connects them
    3. "Strange flesh" implies cross-species sin
    4. Jude quotes 1 Enoch (vv.14-15)

    The Problems¶

    1. Jude 6 doesn't describe sexual sin
    2. "Kept not their first estate, left their own habitation" describes rebellion/departure
    3. Revelation 12 explains this as the war in heaven

    4. No mention of taking wives or producing offspring

    5. "In like manner" grammar

    6. Most naturally connects to "the cities about them"

    7. Not clearly connecting Sodom's sin to the angels' sin

    8. "Strange flesh" in context

    9. Genesis 19 shows homosexual assault, not angel-pursuit
    10. The men didn't know the visitors were angels

    11. "Different flesh" = same-sex pursuit

    12. Peter's parallel doesn't support it

    13. 2 Peter 2:4 simply says "angels that sinned"
    14. No description of sexual sin
    15. Peter adds Noah/flood BETWEEN angels and Sodom
    16. Jude adds Israel BEFORE angels

    17. Neither author structures the list to connect angels directly to Genesis 6

    18. Jesus's teaching about angels

    19. "Angels in heaven neither marry nor are given in marriage" (Matt 22:30)
    20. This suggests angels don't have sexual function

    Honest Assessment: What Jude Actually Says¶

    Jude 6 States:¶

    1. Angels did not keep their proper position
    2. Angels left their proper dwelling
    3. They are reserved for judgment

    Jude 6 Does NOT State:¶

    1. WHEN they sinned
    2. That their sin was sexual
    3. That they took human wives
    4. That this is the Genesis 6 event
    5. Any connection to Nephilim

    Jude 7 States:¶

    1. Sodom and the surrounding cities committed fornication
    2. They went after "strange flesh"
    3. They suffered eternal fire as an example

    The "In Like Manner" Question:¶

    The grammar most naturally reads: "Sodom and Gomorrah, and the cities about them [sinning] in like manner [to Sodom]..."

    Even if "in like manner" connects to the angels, it would only mean Sodom's judgment was similar to the angels' judgment - not that their sins were identical.

    Conclusion of Analysis¶

    Jude 6-7, properly analyzed:

    1. The angels' sin (v.6) is rebellion - abandoning their position and being cast out of heaven. This matches Revelation 12, not Genesis 6.

    2. "In like manner" (v.7) most naturally connects "the cities about them" to Sodom, not Sodom to the angels.

    3. "Strange flesh" (v.7) refers to Sodom's homosexuality - men pursuing men instead of the natural counterpart. The men of Sodom didn't know the visitors were angels.

    4. Jude's purpose is to warn about judgment, not to teach angel chronology. He gives three examples of God judging sin, not a connected narrative about angel-human interbreeding.

    5. The 1 Enoch quotation (vv.14-15) doesn't endorse 1 Enoch's entire theology, just as Paul's quotation of pagan poets didn't endorse paganism.

    The Genesis 6 connection is not required by Jude 6-7 and introduces assumptions not present in the text.
