    Word Studies: Jude 6-7¶

    Key Term 1: Arche (G746) - "First Estate"¶

    Definition¶

    Greek: ἀρχή (arche) Strong's G746: beginning, origin, first place, principality, rule, magistracy

    Meanings¶

    1. Beginning/Origin - the starting point (John 1:1 "In the beginning")
    2. Leadership/Rule - authority, principality, dominion
    3. Proper Position/Rank - one's assigned place in an order

    Usage in Scripture (58 occurrences)¶

    Most commonly translated: - "beginning" (40x) - "principality" (8x) - "rule" (2x)

    Key passages about spiritual powers: - Eph 1:21 - "Far above all principality (arche), and power..." - Col 1:16 - "...whether they be thrones, or dominions, or principalities (arche)..." - Rom 8:38 - "nor principalities (arche), nor powers..."

    Meaning in Jude 6¶

    "The angels which kept not their first estate (arche)..."

    The angels did not maintain their proper position, rank, or domain in the cosmic order. They abandoned their assigned place.

    Revelation 12 parallel: "neither was their place found any more in heaven" (Rev 12:8)

    The term refers to their position/rank, not their physical nature or form.

    Key Term 2: Oiketerion (G3613) - "Habitation"¶

    Definition¶

    Greek: οἰκητήριον (oiketerion) Strong's G3613: a residence, dwelling place, habitation

    Etymology¶

    From oikeo (G3611) = "to dwell"

    All NT Occurrences (Only 2)¶

    1. 2 Corinthians 5:2: "For in this we groan, earnestly desiring to be clothed upon with our house (oiketerion) which is from heaven."

    2. Context: The resurrection body believers will receive

    3. Jude 6: "the angels which... left their own habitation (oiketerion)"

    4. Context: Angels who rebelled

    Interpretive Question¶

    Some argue: Since oiketerion means "body" in 2 Cor 5:2, it means "body" in Jude 6 too. Therefore, angels left their "angelic bodies" to take human form.

    Problems with this reasoning:

    1. Context determines meaning - 2 Cor 5 is about resurrection bodies; Jude 6 is about rebellion
    2. The word means "dwelling place" - in 2 Cor 5:2, the body IS the dwelling place of the spirit
    3. In Jude 6, the dwelling could be heaven itself - where angels properly dwell

    Better Interpretation¶

    "Left their own habitation" = left their proper dwelling place (heaven)

    Revelation 12 parallel: "he was cast out into the earth, and his angels were cast out with him" (Rev 12:9)

    The angels left heaven - they were cast out. This is their "habitation" that they left.

    Key Term 3: Heteros (G2087) - "Strange" (in "Strange Flesh")¶

    Definition¶

    Greek: ἕτερος (heteros) Strong's G2087: the other, another, different

    Nuance¶

    Heteros indicates something "other" or "different" - often with a sense of qualitative difference, not just numerical distinction.

    Compare with allos (G243) which means "another" without emphasizing difference.

    Usage (99 occurrences)¶

    • "another" (43x)
    • "other" (42x)
    • "strange" (2x)

    In Jude 7: "Strange Flesh" (sarkos heteras)¶

    "going after strange flesh (sarkos heteras)"

    Literally: "different flesh" or "other flesh"

    What Makes It "Different"?¶

    Option A: Angelic flesh (different species) If Sodom pursued angels, the flesh would be "different" = non-human.

    Problems: - Genesis 19 shows the men didn't know visitors were angels - They appeared as men - Lot offered daughters as substitute - suggesting sexual desire, not angel-pursuit

    Option B: Same-sex flesh (different from natural) Men pursuing men = pursuing flesh "different" from the natural female counterpart.

    Support: - Romans 1:26-27 describes homosexuality as "against nature" - The natural counterpart for a man is a woman - Pursuing men is pursuing "heteros" (different/other) flesh from God's design - Genesis 19:4-5 clearly shows men demanding male visitors - Genesis 19:8 - men refused female substitutes

    Conclusion¶

    "Strange flesh" (heteras sarkos) = "different/other flesh" = pursuing the same sex instead of the natural opposite sex.

    The men of Sodom pursued flesh that was "different" from God's design - male instead of female.

    Key Phrase: "In Like Manner" (ton homoion tropon)¶

    Greek¶

    τὸν ὅμοιον τρόπον τούτοις (ton homoion tropon toutois) - "in like/similar manner to these"

    Grammatical Question¶

    What does "toutois" (these) refer to?

    Option A: The angels (v.6) - Sodom sinned like the angels Option B: Sodom and Gomorrah - the surrounding cities sinned like Sodom Option C: All the examples - the manner of judgment is similar

    Most Natural Reading¶

    "Sodom and Gomorrah, and the cities about them in like manner [to them]..."

    The phrase "the cities about them" is immediately followed by "in like manner." This most naturally means the surrounding cities (Admah, Zeboim - Deut 29:23) committed the same sins as Sodom.

    Why This Matters¶

    If "in like manner" connects to the angels, it would suggest Sodom's sin was similar to the angels' sin - supporting the sexual interpretation.

    But if "in like manner" connects to "the cities about them," it simply means the whole region committed the same sins - no connection to the angels' sin is implied.

    The grammar favors Option B.

    Summary of Word Studies¶

    Term Greek Meaning Application
    First estate arche (G746) Position, rank, domain Angels lost their proper position in heaven
    Habitation oiketerion (G3613) Dwelling place Angels left heaven (their proper dwelling)
    Strange heteros (G2087) Other, different Sodom pursued "different" flesh (same-sex)
    In like manner ton homoion tropon In similar fashion The surrounding cities sinned like Sodom

    What Word Studies Do NOT Support¶

    1. Angels taking on physical bodies (oiketerion doesn't require this)
    2. Angels committing sexual sin (arche refers to position, not nature)
    3. "In like manner" connecting Sodom's sin to the angels' sin (grammar favors connecting to surrounding cities)
    4. Sodom pursuing angels as angels (heteros more naturally refers to same-sex pursuit)
