    Word Studies: Genesis 4-5 Narrative Context for Genesis 6:1-4¶

    Study 1: H7121 -- qara (call) + H8034 -- shem (name) + H3068 -- YHWH¶

    The Phrase: "qara b'shem YHWH" (call upon the name of the LORD)¶

    H7121: qara (call)¶

    • Hebrew: קָרָא
    • Transliteration: qara
    • Part of Speech: verb
    • BLB Count: 735 occurrences
    • Definition: A primitive root; to call out to (properly, address by name); used widely for: call, cry unto, proclaim, name, read, pronounce
    • Key semantic range: To name, to proclaim, to invoke, to summon

    H8034: shem (name)¶

    • Hebrew: שֵׁם
    • Transliteration: shem
    • Part of Speech: masculine noun
    • BLB Count: 864 occurrences
    • Definition: An appellation, as a mark or memorial of individuality; by implication: honor, authority, character
    • Key semantic range: Name, reputation, renown, fame, memorial

    The Phrase in Genesis 4:26 (Hebrew Parser Output)¶

    אָ֣ז הוּחַ֔ל לִקְרֹ֖א בְּשֵׁ֥ם יְהוָֽה
az  huchal  liqro  b'shem  YHWH
then began  to-call  in-name-of  YHWH

    Critical grammatical note: The verb הוּחַ֔ל (huchal) is Hophal perfect 3ms from the root חלל (chalal). The Hophal is the passive of the Hiphil. The primary meaning of chalal in Hiphil is "to begin" (as in Gen 6:1, same root: הֵחֵ֣ל, hechel -- "began"). However, chalal also carries the meaning "to profane" in other stems (Niphal, Piel). The KJV translates this as "then began [men]" -- taking it as "to begin."

    The infinitive construct לִקְרֹ֖א (liqro) = "to call" The prepositional phrase בְּשֵׁ֥ם יְהוָֽה (b'shem YHWH) = "in/upon the name of YHWH"

    Every Occurrence of "Calling on the Name of the LORD" in Genesis¶

    Reference Text Context Actor
    Gen 4:26 "then began men to call upon the name of the LORD" Birth of Enos in Seth's line Seth's line collectively
    Gen 12:8 "called upon the name of the LORD" Abram builds altar near Bethel Abram
    Gen 13:4 "Abram called on the name of the LORD" Return to Bethel altar Abram
    Gen 21:33 "called there on the name of the LORD, the everlasting God" Beersheba covenant with Abimelech Abraham
    Gen 26:25 "called upon the name of the LORD" Beer-sheba, after God's promise Isaac

    Pattern observed: Every person who "calls on the name of the LORD" in Genesis is: 1. In the godly line (Seth's line, then Abraham's line) 2. Associated with altar-building and worship 3. Identified by their relationship with YHWH

    This phrase functions as a spiritual identity marker -- it distinguishes those who belong to God from those who do not.

    Post-Genesis Usage (Key Examples)¶

    Reference Context
    1 Kings 18:24 Elijah's contest: "I will call on the name of the LORD" vs. Baal worshippers
    Psalm 116:4,13,17 Worship pattern: calling on YHWH in distress and thanksgiving
    Joel 2:32 "Whosoever shall call on the name of the LORD shall be delivered"
    Zeph 3:9 Eschatological: nations will "call upon the name of the LORD"
    Acts 2:21 Peter quotes Joel: "whosoever shall call on the name of the Lord shall be saved"
    Romans 10:13 Paul quotes Joel: "whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved"

    Theological significance: The phrase moves from Genesis (identity marker of the godly) through the prophets (promise of salvation) to the NT (the universal offer of salvation). Those who "call on the name of the LORD" are God's people -- the "sons of God" in spiritual terms.

    Study 2: H1121 -- ben (son/children)¶

    H1121: ben¶

    • Hebrew: בֵּן
    • Transliteration: ben
    • Part of Speech: masculine noun
    • BLB Count: 4,906+ occurrences (one of the most common Hebrew words)
    • Definition: From banah (to build); a son (as a builder of the family name), in the widest sense of literal and figurative relationship, including grandson, subject, nation, quality or condition
    • Key semantic range: son, child, descendant, member of a class/group

    Usage in Genesis 5-6¶

    Reference Hebrew Translation Context
    Gen 5:4 בָּנִים (banim) "sons" Adam begat sons and daughters
    Gen 5:7 בָּנִים "sons" Seth begat sons and daughters
    Gen 5:10 בָּנִים "sons" Enos begat sons and daughters
    Gen 5:28 בֵּן (ben) "a son" Lamech begat a son (Noah)
    Gen 5:32 -- "sons" Noah begat Shem, Ham, Japheth
    Gen 6:2 בְנֵי־הָֽאֱלֹהִים֙ (bene ha-elohim) "sons of God" saw daughters of men
    Gen 6:4 בְנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים "sons of God" came in unto daughters of men

    Key observation: The word "ben" (son) runs as a thread through Genesis 5 into Genesis 6. In Genesis 5, every patriarch begets "sons" (banim). Then in Genesis 6:2, the "sons of God" (bene ha-elohim) appears. The reader has just been told about the sons of the godly line (Seth's genealogy) -- and now encounters "sons of God." The literary connection is deliberate.

    Figurative Uses of "ben" for Quality/Character¶

    "Ben" frequently means "belonging to the class/category of": - "Sons of Belial" (bene beliyyaal) = worthless men (Deut 13:13; Judges 19:22) - "Sons of the prophets" (bene ha-neviim) = belonging to the prophetic order (2 Kings 2:3) - "Children of the LORD your God" (bene YHWH elohekem) = those belonging to God (Deut 14:1) - "Sons of God" (bene elohim) = those belonging to God / the divine council

    The phrase "bene elohim" in Genesis 6:2 could therefore mean "those who belong to God" -- the godly line who called upon the name of the LORD.

    Study 3: H120 -- adam (man/mankind)¶

    H120: adam¶

    • Hebrew: אָדָם
    • Transliteration: adam
    • Part of Speech: masculine noun
    • BLB Count: 552 occurrences
    • Definition: From H119 (to be red); a human being (an individual or the species, mankind)
    • Key semantic range: man, mankind, human being, person

    Usage in Genesis 6:1-7 (Hebrew Parser Analysis)¶

    Genesis 6:1: הָֽאָדָ֔ם (ha-adam) -- "the man" / "mankind" with the definite article - "when ha-adam (mankind) began to multiply on the face of the earth"

    Genesis 6:2: הָֽאָדָ֔ם (ha-adam) -- "the man" / "mankind" - "the daughters of ha-adam (mankind)"

    Genesis 6:3: בָֽאָדָם֙ (ba-adam) -- "in man" / "with man" - "My spirit shall not always strive with adam (man)"

    Genesis 6:5: הָֽאָדָ֔ם (ha-adam) -- "the man" / "mankind" - "the wickedness of ha-adam was great"

    Genesis 6:6: הָֽאָדָ֔ם (ha-adam) - "that he had made ha-adam on the earth"

    Genesis 6:7: הָֽאָדָ֔ם (ha-adam) - "I will destroy ha-adam whom I have created"

    Critical observation for Genesis 6:2: The phrase is "benot ha-adam" (daughters of the adam/mankind). The article "ha-" makes it generic -- "daughters of mankind." This is NOT "daughters of Cain" specifically; it is "daughters of human beings." This means:

    1. The "sons of God" are distinguished FROM generic humanity
    2. The "daughters of men" = the general population (which includes Cain's line)
    3. The contrast is between a specific group ("sons of God") and the general human population ("daughters of ha-adam")

    This fits perfectly if "sons of God" = the godly line (those who call on the name of YHWH), and "daughters of men" = the broader human population that does not.

    Study 4: H1323 -- bath (daughter)¶

    H1323: bath¶

    • Hebrew: בַּת
    • Transliteration: bath
    • Part of Speech: feminine noun
    • BLB Count: 588 occurrences
    • Definition: Feminine of ben; a daughter (used in the same wide sense: literal and figurative)
    • Key semantic range: daughter, girl, town, branch

    Usage Pattern: Genesis 5 to Genesis 6¶

    Genesis 5 -- "banot" (daughters, plural of bath) appears 9 times in the refrain: - 5:4, 5:7, 5:10, 5:13, 5:16, 5:19, 5:22, 5:26, 5:30 - Each time: "[patriarch] begat sons and daughters (banot)"

    Genesis 6:1 -- "u-banot yulledu lahem" = "and daughters were born unto them" - Same word: banot - Direct vocabulary link from Genesis 5's refrain

    Genesis 6:2 -- "benot ha-adam" = "daughters of men" - Same word: benot (construct form of banot) - The "daughters" that were born (6:1) are now specified as "daughters of ha-adam" (6:2)

    Vocabulary chain: Genesis 5 creates a rhythm of "sons and daughters, sons and daughters" for 9 generations. Then Genesis 6:1 picks up "daughters" and Genesis 6:2 identifies them as "daughters of men." The vocabulary is continuous.

    Study 5: H2555 -- chamas (violence)¶

    H2555: chamas¶

    • Hebrew: חָמָס
    • Transliteration: chamas
    • Part of Speech: masculine noun
    • BLB Count: 60 occurrences
    • Definition: From H2554; violence; by implication, wrong; by metonymy unjust gain
    • Key semantic range: violence, cruelty, wrong, injustice, oppression

    Usage in Genesis¶

    Reference Context Note
    Gen 6:11 "the earth was filled with chamas (violence)" First use of the word
    Gen 6:13 "the earth is filled with chamas through them" God's stated reason for the flood
    Gen 16:5 Sarah says: "My chamas [wrong] be upon thee" Used for interpersonal wrong
    Gen 49:5 "instruments of chamas are in their habitations" Simeon and Levi's violence

    The Violence Chain: Genesis 4 to Genesis 6¶

    While the word chamas does not appear in Genesis 4, the concept of violence escalates through the chapter:

    Stage Reference Description
    1. First murder Gen 4:8 Cain slew (harag) his brother Abel
    2. Exile from God's presence Gen 4:16 Cain went out from the presence of the LORD
    3. Polygamy Gen 4:19 Lamech took two wives (first polygamy in Scripture)
    4. Boasted violence Gen 4:23 Lamech: "I have slain (harag) a man to my wounding"
    5. Defiance of God Gen 4:24 Lamech: "If Cain sevenfold, truly Lamech seventy and sevenfold"
    6. Universal violence Gen 6:11 "The earth was filled with violence (chamas)"

    The progression is clear: - Cain murders out of anger (4:8) - Lamech murders with boasting and poetic celebration (4:23-24) - By Genesis 6:11, violence has filled the entire earth

    Hebrew Parser: Genesis 6:11¶

    וַתִּשָּׁחֵ֥ת הָאָ֖רֶץ לִפְנֵ֣י הָֽאֱלֹהִ֑ים וַתִּמָּלֵ֥א הָאָ֖רֶץ חָמָֽס
wa-tishshacheth  ha-aretz  lifne  ha-elohim  wa-timmale  ha-aretz  chamas
and-was-corrupted  the-earth  before  God  and-was-filled  the-earth  violence
    • תִּשָּׁחֵ֥ת (tishshacheth) -- Niphal wayyiqtol 3fs of שׁחת (shachath) = "was corrupted"
    • תִּמָּלֵ֥א (timmale) -- Niphal wayyiqtol 3fs of מלא (male) = "was filled"
    • Both verbs are Niphal (passive) -- the earth RECEIVED corruption and violence from its inhabitants

    Study 6: The Hophal of chalal (begin/profane) -- Genesis 4:26 and 6:1¶

    Critical connection: Same root in Gen 4:26 and Gen 6:1¶

    Genesis 4:26: הוּחַ֔ל (huchal) -- Hophal perfect 3ms of חלל (chalal) -- "then began [men to call upon the name of the LORD]"

    Genesis 6:1: הֵחֵ֣ל (hechel) -- Hiphil perfect 3ms of חלל (chalal) -- "when [men] began to multiply on the face of the earth"

    The same verb root (chalal = begin) frames both statements: - 4:26: Men began to call upon the name of the LORD (godly worship begins) - 6:1: Men began to multiply on the face of the earth (population grows, setting up the crisis)

    This is a deliberate literary connection: what began as worship (4:26) leads into a context where multiplication (6:1) creates the conditions for intermarriage between the worshippers and the non-worshippers.

    Study 7: H430 -- elohim (God/gods) in "bene ha-elohim"¶

    The phrase "bene ha-elohim" in Genesis 6:2¶

    From the Hebrew parser: 

    בְנֵי־הָֽאֱלֹהִים֙
bene-ha-elohim
sons of-the-god(s)

    • בְנֵי (bene) = construct plural of ben = "sons of"
    • הָֽ (ha) = definite article = "the"
    • אֱלֹהִים (elohim) = God/gods (morphologically plural)

    The article matters: The phrase is "sons of THE elohim" -- with the definite article. This is the same construction used in Job 1:6; 2:1; 38:7.

    However, the meaning depends on context: - In Job (throne-room scenes with Satan): heavenly beings - In Genesis 6 (earthly scene of marriage): those who belong to God

    The Sethite view reads "bene ha-elohim" as "those who are characterized by their relationship with God" -- the line that calls upon the name of YHWH.

    Word Strong's Key Finding

    shem (name) H8034 "Name" connects to identity and character; calling on God's name = identifying with God
    ben (son) H1121 "Sons" threads from Gen 5 genealogy into Gen 6:2; "ben" frequently means "belonging to a class"
    adam (man) H120 "ha-adam" in Gen 6:1-2 is generic "mankind" -- not specifically Cain's line
    bath (daughter) H1323 "banot" links Gen 5's refrain directly to Gen 6:1-2
    chamas (violence) H2555 Violence escalates from Cain (4:8) to Lamech (4:23) to universal (6:11)
    chalal (begin) H2490 Same root in 4:26 and 6:1 creates literary frame: worship began, then multiplication began
