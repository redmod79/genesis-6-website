    Word Studies: Explicit vs. Implied Evidence for Genesis 6 "Sons of God"¶

    Note on Scope¶

    This is a methodological study that categorizes arguments as explicit or implied. The word studies below focus on the key terms that distinguish EXPLICIT from IMPLIED evidence -- terms where the text either directly states something or where inference is required.

    1. "Man" / adam (H120)¶

    Genesis 6:3: "My spirit shall not always strive with man (adam)"¶

    Feature Data
    Hebrew אָדָם (adam)
    Strong's H120
    Meaning Man, mankind, human being
    Genesis 6:3 usage God identifies the "sons of God" as adam -- human
    Category EXPLICIT -- God's own identification

    Why this matters for the methodology: God's response to the sons of God/daughters of men situation uses "adam" (man/mankind). If the "sons of God" were angels, God's response addresses the wrong party. The term adam is never applied to angels anywhere in Scripture.

    Occurrences in Genesis 6: Genesis 6:1 ("men began to multiply"), 6:2 ("daughters of men"), 6:3 ("strive with man"), 6:5 ("wickedness of man"), 6:6 ("made man"), 6:7 ("destroy man"). Every use of adam in Genesis 6 refers to humans. The chapter is about human beings from start to finish.

    2. "Flesh" / basar (H1320)¶

    Genesis 6:3: "for that he also [is] flesh (basar)"¶

    Feature Data
    Hebrew בָּשָׂר (basar)
    Strong's H1320
    Meaning Flesh, body, human nature
    Genesis 6:3 usage God calls the "sons of God" flesh
    Category EXPLICIT -- never applied to angels

    Significance: basar (flesh) is used 270 times in the OT. It refers to human flesh, animal flesh, or the physical body. It is NEVER used to describe angels. Hebrews 1:7, 14 explicitly identifies angels as "spirits" (pneumata), not flesh. God calling the "sons of God" basar is an explicit identification as human, not angelic.

    Genesis 6 usage: 6:3 ("he also is flesh"), 6:12 ("all flesh had corrupted"), 6:13 ("end of all flesh"), 6:17 ("all flesh"), 6:19 ("all flesh"). The term consistently means "all living human beings" in this context.

    3. "Sons of God" / bene elohim (H1121 + H430)¶

    The Term at the Center of the Debate¶

    Feature Data
    Hebrew בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים (bene ha-elohim)
    Strong's H1121 (ben = son) + H430 (elohim = God/gods/mighty ones)
    OT occurrences Genesis 6:2, 6:4; Job 1:6, 2:1, 38:7
    Job meaning Heavenly beings -- EXPLICIT in context
    Genesis 6 meaning DISPUTED -- this is the question

    What is EXPLICIT:¶

    • In Job, the context is a heavenly throne room with Satan present -- heavenly beings are clearly meant
    • In Genesis 6, the context is earthly marriage and population growth
    • God calls the Genesis 6 "sons of God" adam and basar (man and flesh)

    What is IMPLIED:¶

    • Transferring Job's referent to Genesis 6 requires inference across different genres and contexts (the authorship of Job is debated -- Moses is a traditional candidate but uncertain; even if the same author, the context difference remains)
    • The Hebrew phrase itself does not dictate a single referent

    Elohim semantic range (H430):¶

    • God (the true God): ~87.5% of OT occurrences
    • False gods: ~5.2%
    • Human judges/authorities: Exodus 21:6; 22:8-9 (5 occurrences)
    • Mighty/great (superlative): Genesis 23:6; Exodus 9:28

    Jesus identifies "gods" (elohim) in Psalm 82 as HUMANS "unto whom the word of God came" (John 10:34-35). "Bene elohim" can legitimately mean "sons of the mighty/nobles/judges."

    4. "Angel" / malak (H4397)¶

    Moses's Consistent Term for Celestial Beings¶

    Feature Data
    Hebrew מַלְאָךְ (malak)
    Strong's H4397
    Meaning Messenger, angel
    Pentateuch usage 28+ times for celestial beings
    Category EXPLICIT verifiable vocabulary fact

    Key data: - "Angel of the LORD" (malak YHWH): 14 Pentateuch occurrences - "Angel of God" (malak elohim): 3 occurrences - "Angels" plural (malakim): 4 occurrences - "An angel / his angel" (malak): 7 occurrences

    Moses NEVER uses "bene elohim" for angels. In Genesis 19:1, describing celestial beings at Sodom, Moses writes "two angels" (malakim) -- not "sons of God." This vocabulary distinction is directly verifiable and constitutes explicit evidence.

    5. "Spirits" / pneumata (G4151)¶

    The Nature of Angels¶

    Feature Data
    Greek πνεύματα (pneumata)
    Strong's G4151
    Hebrews 1:7 "Who maketh his angels spirits"
    Hebrews 1:14 "Are they not all ministering spirits"
    Category EXPLICIT -- direct statement about angelic nature

    Why this matters: Angels are explicitly identified as spirits. Genesis 6:3 calls the subjects "flesh." Flesh and spirit are contrasted categories in Scripture (John 3:6: "That which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit"). The explicit identification of angels as spirits and the Genesis 6 subjects as flesh places them in opposing categories.

    6. "Marry" / gameo (G1060) and gamizo (G1061)¶

    Jesus's Marriage Vocabulary¶

    Feature Data
    Greek γαμέω (gameo) = to marry; γαμίζω (gamizo) = to give in marriage
    Strong's G1060, G1061
    Matthew 22:30 "neither marry (gamousin) nor are given in marriage (gamizontai)"
    NT usage 29 occurrences of gameo -- always literal marriage
    Category EXPLICIT -- Jesus's direct teaching

    Significance: Jesus uses BOTH terms, covering the entire marriage institution: the groom's action (gameo) and the father's act of giving the bride (gamizo). The double formulation is comprehensive. Jesus's statement about angelic nature with respect to marriage is explicit and categorical.

    7. "Isangeloi" / equal to the angels (G2465)¶

    Luke's Unique Term¶

    Feature Data
    Greek ἰσάγγελοι (isangeloi)
    Strong's G2465
    Meaning Angel-equal, equal to the angels
    Occurrence Luke 20:36 only (hapax legomenon)
    Category EXPLICIT -- removes any "in heaven" limitation

    Why this matters for the methodology: The angel view claims "in heaven" (Matthew/Mark) limits Jesus's statement to faithful angels. Luke's isangeloi has NO "in heaven" qualifier. It describes what the resurrected will BE -- equal to angels -- as the REASON they cannot die or marry. This eliminates the "fallen angels are different" inference.

    The term is a compound: isos (equal) + angelos (angel). It predicates a quality about nature, not location. If any angels could marry, the comparison breaks -- "you'll be like the class of beings that mostly don't marry" is not a meaningful comparison.

    8. "Corrupt" / shachath (H7843) + "Way" / derek (H1870)¶

    The "Corrupt His Way" Phrase¶

    Feature Data
    Hebrew שָׁחַת (shachath) + דֶּרֶךְ (derek)
    Strong's H7843 + H1870
    Genesis 6:12 "All flesh had corrupted his way"
    Category EXPLICIT that this means moral corruption

    Every parallel: - Deuteronomy 9:12: Golden calf idolatry -- MORAL - Deuteronomy 31:29: Future apostasy -- MORAL - Judges 2:19: Following false gods -- MORAL - Ezekiel 16:47: Jerusalem's abominations -- MORAL

    Zero instances of "corrupt + way" meaning genetic or biological corruption. The genetic reading is implied (inferred against the textual evidence); the moral reading is explicit (confirmed by every parallel).

    9. "Nephilim" / nephilim (H5303)¶

    The Giants¶

    Feature Data
    Hebrew נְפִלִים (nephilim)
    Strong's H5303
    Meaning Giants, tyrants ("a feller, i.e. a bully")
    Root naphal (H5307) = to fall
    Category The word itself is EXPLICIT in meaning; the angel-hybrid origin is IMPLIED

    Key finding: The word "nephilim" describes what they were (giants/tyrants), not how they originated. Numbers 13:33 applies "nephilim" to the Anakim, whose human genealogy is fully traceable (Joshua 15:13-14: Arba > Anak > three sons). If "nephilim" required angel parentage, it could not apply to the Anakim. The application proves it means "giants" -- a description, not an origin story.

    Summary: Explicit vs. Implied at the Word Level¶

    Term What is EXPLICIT What is IMPLIED (by angel view)
    adam (man) God calls sons of God "man" That He meant something else
    basar (flesh) God calls them "flesh" That this applies to angels too
    bene elohim = angels in Job; disputed in Gen 6 = angels in Gen 6 (cross-context transfer)
    malak (angel) Moses's 28+ times for angels That he used a different term in Gen 6 for variety
    pneumata (spirits) Angels are spirits That spirits can reproduce biologically
    gameo/gamizo Angels do not marry That "in heaven" limits this
    isangeloi Equal to angels (no qualifier) (No counter -- this removes the "in heaven" limit)
    shachath + derek Always moral corruption That Gen 6:12 uniquely means genetic corruption
    nephilim = giants/tyrants (description) = angel-human hybrids (origin theory)

    In every case, the explicit meaning of the term supports the human view. The angel view requires implied reinterpretation of these terms against their established usage.
