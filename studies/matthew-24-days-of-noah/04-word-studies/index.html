    Word Studies¶

    Key Greek Terms in Jesus's Teaching¶

    γάρ (gar) - G1063¶

    Location: Matthew 24:38 - "For as in the days that were before the flood..."

    Definition: A primary particle; properly, assigning a reason (used in argument, explanation or intensification)

    Significance: - This conjunction introduces Jesus's explanation of what "days of Noah" means - It signals: "What I mean by 'days of Noah' is this..." - The activities Jesus lists (eating, drinking, marrying) are His authoritative definition

    Translation: "for," "because," "indeed"

    ἐσθίω (esthio) - G2068¶

    Location: Matthew 24:38 - "they were eating" (τρώγοντες) Location: Luke 17:27 - "They did eat" (ἤσθιον)

    Definition: To eat (usually literal)

    Usage in Context: - Basic human activity of consuming food - Not a metaphor or symbol - Represents ordinary daily life

    Significance: Jesus chose the most basic human activity to characterize Noah's days - sustenance. This is universal and timeless.

    πίνω (pino) - G4095¶

    Location: Matthew 24:38 - "drinking" (πίνοντες) Location: Luke 17:27 - "they drank" (ἔπινον)

    Definition: To imbibe (literally or figuratively)

    Usage in Context: - Basic human activity paired with eating - Not specifically referring to drunkenness - Simply normal consumption

    Significance: Like eating, drinking is universal and ordinary. Jesus did not emphasize excess or sin but normalcy.

    γαμέω (gameo) - G1060¶

    Location: Matthew 24:38 - "marrying" (γαμοῦντες) Location: Luke 17:27 - "they married wives" (ἐγάμουν)

    Definition: To wed (of either sex)

    Usage Pattern in NT: - Matthew 19:10 - disciples ask about marrying - Matthew 22:30 - angels don't marry (ἐν γὰρ τῇ ἀναστάσει οὔτε γαμοῦσιν) - 1 Corinthians 7:28 - "if thou marry, thou hast not sinned"

    Significance: - Standard word for marriage throughout NT - In Matthew 24:38, paired with eating/drinking as normal activity - NOT a technical term for Genesis 6 "took wives" - Jesus's point: ordinary social arrangements continue until judgment

    ἐκγαμίζω (ekgamizo) - G1547¶

    Location: Matthew 24:38 - "giving in marriage" (ἐκγαμίζοντες) Location: Luke 17:27 - "they were given in marriage" (ἐξεγαμίζοντο)

    Definition: To marry off a daughter

    Usage Pattern: - Matthew 22:30 - "nor are given in marriage" (οὔτε ἐκγαμίζονται) - 1 Corinthians 7:38 - "he that giveth her in marriage doeth well"

    Significance: - Refers to the family arrangement of giving daughters in marriage - Represents normal social/family activity - Paired with γαμέω to cover both sides of wedding arrangements

    οἶδα (oida) - G1492¶

    Location: Matthew 24:39 - "And knew not" (οὐκ ἔγνωσαν)

    Note: Matthew uses γινώσκω (ginosko, G1097) here, a synonym.

    Definition: To know, perceive, understand

    Usage in Context: - "Knew not until the flood came" - Emphasizes unawareness or lack of perception - Not intellectual ignorance but spiritual unconcern

    Significance: This is central to Jesus's point. The people were: - Not watching for judgment - Not spiritually alert - Living as if judgment would never come - Caught completely unaware

    ὁμοίως (homoios) - G3668¶

    Location: Luke 17:28 - "Likewise also as it was in the days of Lot"

    Definition: In like manner, similarly, likewise

    Significance: - Jesus draws a direct parallel between Noah and Lot - Whatever characterized Noah's days FOR THIS TEACHING also characterized Lot's days - Since Lot's days had no "sons of God" element, neither does Jesus's point about Noah's days

    This word is interpretively crucial - it proves Jesus's point is about common elements (ordinary life, sudden judgment), not unique elements (supernatural activity).

    γρηγορέω (gregoreo) - G1127¶

    Location: Matthew 24:42 - "Watch therefore"

    Definition: To keep awake, be vigilant, watchful

    Usage Pattern: - Matthew 26:38 - "tarry ye here, and watch with me" - Mark 13:35 - "Watch ye therefore" - 1 Thessalonians 5:6 - "let us watch and be sober"

    Significance: This is Jesus's application. The response to "days of Noah" is: - Stay spiritually awake - Be vigilant - Don't be caught unaware like those in Noah's day

    This application makes sense ONLY if Jesus's point was about unawareness. If His point was about supernatural activity, "watch" would be an odd response.

    ἕτοιμος (hetoimos) - G2092¶

    Location: Matthew 24:44 - "be ye also ready"

    Definition: Adjusted, prepared, ready

    Usage Pattern: - Matthew 25:10 - "they that were ready went in" - Luke 12:40 - "be ye therefore ready also" - 1 Peter 3:15 - "be ready always to give an answer"

    Significance: Second application from Jesus. Be: - Prepared - In a state of readiness - Not caught off-guard

    Again, this application makes sense only if the danger is unexpected timing, not supernatural phenomena.

    Word Study Summary¶

    Greek Word English What It Shows
    γάρ (gar) "for" Jesus is explaining what "days of Noah" means
    ἐσθίω (esthio) "eating" Ordinary, universal activity
    πίνω (pino) "drinking" Ordinary, universal activity
    γαμέω (gameo) "marrying" Normal word for marriage, not technical
    ἐκγαμίζω (ekgamizo) "giving in marriage" Normal family arrangements
    γινώσκω (ginosko) "knew" Unawareness is the problem
    ὁμοίως (homoios) "likewise" Noah and Lot parallel - same point
    γρηγορέω (gregoreo) "watch" Application: stay alert
    ἕτοιμος (hetoimos) "ready" Application: be prepared

    Conclusion from Word Studies¶

    The Greek vocabulary confirms Jesus's point was about: 1. Ordinary activities (eating, drinking, marrying) - not supernatural events 2. Spiritual unawareness (knew not) - the problem being addressed 3. Parallel with Lot (likewise) - proving the point is common, not unique to Noah 4. Watchfulness and readiness (watch, ready) - the practical response

    None of the vocabulary suggests Jesus was referencing Genesis 6's "sons of God" or any supernatural element.
