    Verse Analysis: Deuteronomy 32:8 - "Sons of God" Textual Variant¶

    The Argument Examined¶

    The Angel View Claim¶

    "The Dead Sea Scrolls preserve the original reading 'sons of God' (bene elohim) in Deuteronomy 32:8. This proves that 'sons of God' refers to divine beings, not humans. Therefore, the 'sons of God' in Genesis 6 must also be divine beings (angels) who mated with humans."

    The Question¶

    Does the textual variant in Deuteronomy 32:8 prove the angel view of Genesis 6?

    Part 1: Understanding the Textual Variant¶

    The Three Readings¶

    Text Reading Date
    Masoretic Text (MT) "children of Israel" (בני ישראל) Medieval manuscripts
    Dead Sea Scrolls (4QDeutj) "sons of God" (בני אלהים) ~2nd-1st century BC
    Septuagint (LXX) "angels of God" (ἀγγέλων θεοῦ) ~3rd-2nd century BC

    Scholarly Opinions¶

    Some scholars favor DSS/LXX: - Argue MT reflects theological alteration to avoid polytheistic implications - DSS is older manuscript evidence - "Sons of God" is the more difficult reading (lectio difficilior)

    Some scholars favor MT: - MT is the received text with unbroken transmission - The 70 nations / 70 Israelites connection makes contextual sense - DSS/LXX may reflect influence from pagan divine council mythology - Minimal letter change: ישראל (Israel) → אלהים (elohim)

    For This Study's Purpose¶

    We will examine BOTH possibilities: 1. What if MT is original? 2. What if DSS is original?

    Critical question: Does EITHER reading support the angel view of Genesis 6?

    Part 2: If the Masoretic Text Is Original¶

    The Reading¶

    "he set the bounds of the people according to the number of the children of Israel"

    The Meaning¶

    When God divided nations at Babel (Genesis 10-11), He set their boundaries with Israel in mind. The number of nations (70) corresponds to the number of Israelites who would later go to Egypt (70).

    The Evidence¶

    70 Nations 70 Israelites
    Genesis 10 - Table of Nations Genesis 46:27 - "threescore and ten"
    Traditional Jewish count = 70 Exodus 1:5 - "seventy souls"

    Theological Significance¶

    God's plan for Israel was embedded in His arrangement of the nations from the beginning. When He divided humanity at Babel, He already knew: - Israel would number 70 going to Egypt - Israel would be His special portion (v.9) - The nations would correspond to Israel's founding families

    This Reading and Genesis 6¶

    The MT reading says nothing about angels or divine beings. It cannot support the angel view.

    Part 3: If the Dead Sea Scrolls Reading Is Original¶

    The Reading¶

    "he set the bounds of the peoples according to the number of the sons of God"

    What This Actually Says¶

    1. God (the Most High) divided the nations
    2. God separated the sons of Adam
    3. God set the boundaries
    4. The number corresponds to "sons of God"

    What This Does NOT Say¶

    • It does NOT say angels married humans
    • It does NOT say angels produced Nephilim
    • It does NOT say angels left their proper habitation
    • It does NOT connect to Genesis 6

    The "Divine Council" Interpretation¶

    Some scholars interpret this as: - 70 divine beings assigned to oversee 70 nations - YHWH took Israel as His direct portion - Each nation has a patron deity/angel

    Even if this interpretation is correct: - It describes administrative assignment, not sexual reproduction - The "sons of God" are passive recipients of assignment by God - Nothing here says they can or did mate with humans

    Part 4: The Logical Gap¶

    The Angel View's Reasoning¶

    1. DSS says nations were divided according to "sons of God"
    2. Therefore "sons of God" = divine beings
    3. Therefore "sons of God" in Genesis 6 = angels
    4. Therefore angels mated with humans

    The Problem¶

    The leap from step 2 to step 4 is unsupported:

    What Deut 32:8 (DSS) Says What Genesis 6 Claims
    Divine beings assigned to nations Divine beings took wives
    Administrative/territorial role Sexual/reproductive role
    God divided; beings received Beings acted on their own
    No offspring mentioned Offspring (Nephilim?)

    These are completely different activities.

    Analogy¶

    This is like arguing: 1. Angels are called "ministers" (Hebrews 1:7) 2. Therefore angels can serve in human churches 3. Therefore angels wrote the epistles

    The premise doesn't support the conclusion.

    Part 5: Job's "Sons of God" - The Better Parallel¶

    Job 1:6; 2:1¶

    "the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD"

    What They Do¶

    • Present themselves before God
    • Report to God (Job 1:7)
    • Make accusations (Job 1:9-11)
    • Receive permission for action (Job 1:12)

    What They Don't Do¶

    • Marry
    • Reproduce
    • Leave their proper habitation
    • Take on permanent physical form

    Even in Job, "sons of God" are heavenly beings who serve in God's court - not beings who mate with humans.

    Part 6: Deuteronomy 4:19-20 - The Parallel Passage¶

    Deut 4:19 - "the sun, and the moon, and the stars...the host of heaven...which the LORD thy God hath divided unto all nations" Deut 4:20 - "But the LORD hath taken you...to be unto him a people of inheritance"

    Key Points¶

    1. GOD divided the heavenly hosts to nations
    2. This is about what nations worship, not what rules them
    3. Israel is contrasted as God's direct inheritance
    4. Same theme as Deuteronomy 32:8-9

    The Implication¶

    Both passages say: - Other nations have their assigned worship objects - Israel has the LORD directly - GOD made these assignments

    Neither passage says angels mated with humans.

    Part 7: Acts 17:26 - New Testament Confirmation¶

    "And hath made of one blood all nations of men for to dwell on all the face of the earth, and hath determined the times before appointed, and the bounds of their habitation"

    Paul's Teaching¶

    • God made all nations from one blood (Adam)
    • God determined their times and boundaries
    • God did this so they might seek Him (v.27)

    The Significance¶

    Paul attributes national boundaries to GOD, not to angels or divine council members. This confirms the theological point of Deuteronomy 32:8 - whether MT or DSS reading.

    Part 8: Connection to Genesis 6¶

    The Angel View's Use of Deuteronomy 32:8¶

    The angel view argues: 1. DSS proves "sons of God" = divine beings 2. Therefore Genesis 6 "sons of God" = angels 3. Therefore angels mated with humans

    Why This Fails¶

    Deuteronomy 32:8 Genesis 6:1-4
    National boundaries Marriage and reproduction
    God assigns territories Beings "take" wives
    Administrative function Sexual function
    No offspring mentioned Children born
    Heavenly council context Earthly domestic context

    The contexts are completely different.

    The Key Distinction¶

    Even if "sons of God" in Deuteronomy 32:8 means divine beings: - They are assigned to oversee nations - They don't marry humans - They don't produce offspring

    Deuteronomy 32:8 proves nothing about whether angels can or did mate with humans.

    Part 9: What Deuteronomy 32:8 Actually Teaches¶

    Regardless of Textual Variant¶

    The verse teaches: 1. The Most High (God) divided nations 2. God separated the sons of Adam (humanity) 3. God set national boundaries 4. Israel is God's special portion (v.9) 5. The LORD alone led Israel (v.12)

    The Point of the Passage¶

    Moses recounts God's faithfulness to Israel: - God planned Israel's place among the nations - Israel is uniquely God's inheritance - Despite this privilege, Israel rebelled (v.5, 15-18)

    The textual variant affects HOW God made the divisions, not WHETHER He made them or what it means for Genesis 6.

    Summary of Analysis¶

    If MT Is Original¶

    "Children of Israel" connects 70 nations to 70 Israelites, showing God's foreknowledge. No relevance to Genesis 6.

    If DSS Is Original¶

    "Sons of God" describes divine beings assigned to nations - an administrative role, not a reproductive one. Still no relevance to Genesis 6.

    The Logical Gap¶

    What DSS Deut 32:8 Proves What Angel View Claims
    Divine beings exist ✓ Agreed
    Divine beings can be called "sons of God" ✓ Agreed
    Divine beings assigned to nations ✓ Agreed (if DSS original)
    Divine beings can marry humans ✗ NOT proven
    Divine beings produced Nephilim ✗ NOT proven
    Genesis 6 describes angel-human marriage ✗ NOT proven

    Deuteronomy 32:8 cannot bridge the gap from "divine beings exist" to "divine beings reproduced with humans."
