# Word Studies: Jude 1:6-7 "In Like Manner"

## The Key Phrase

**Greek:** τὸν ὅμοιον τρόπον τούτοις
**Transliteration:** ton homoion tropon toutois
**Literal:** "the similar manner to-these"
**KJV:** "in like manner"

---

## Word Study 1: ὅμοιος (homoios) - G3664

**Parsing in Jude 1:7:** A-ASM (Adjective, Accusative Singular Masculine)

### Definition
- **Strong's:** "Similar (in appearance or character)" - rendered as "like" or "+ manner"
- **Thayer's:** "Like, similar, resembling"
  - Resembling in form, appearance, nature, or condition
  - Corresponding or equivalent to; the same as

### Etymology
From the base of G3674 (ὁμοῦ - "together")

### NT Usage (47 occurrences)
- Frequently used in parables: "The kingdom of heaven is **like**..." (Matt 13:31, 33, 44-45, 47)
- Used for comparison: "like unto" (Luke 6:47-49; 7:31-32)
- In Revelation: describing appearances (Rev 1:13, 15; 4:3, 6-7)

### Key Observation
ὅμοιος establishes a comparison of **similarity** - the things compared share characteristics.

---

## Word Study 2: τρόπος (tropos) - G5158

**Parsing in Jude 1:7:** N-ASM (Noun, Accusative Singular Masculine)

### Definition
- **Strong's:** "A turn, i.e. (by implication) mode or style; figuratively, deportment or character"
- **Thayer's:** "A manner, way, or fashion; manner of life, character, and deportment"

### NT Usage (13 occurrences)

| Reference | KJV Translation | Greek Construction |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Matt 23:37 | "even as" | ὃν τρόπον |
| Luke 13:34 | "as" | ὃν τρόπον |
| Acts 1:11 | "in like manner as" | ὃν τρόπον |
| Acts 7:28 | "as" | ὃν τρόπον |
| Acts 15:11 | "even as" | καθ' ὃν τρόπον |
| Acts 27:25 | "even as" | καθ' ὃν τρόπον |
| 2 Tim 3:8 | "as" | ὃν τρόπον |
| **Jude 1:7** | "in like manner" | **τὸν ὅμοιον τρόπον** |

### Key Observation
τρόπος with ὅμοιον creates an **adverbial accusative** phrase meaning "in the similar manner" - describing HOW the action was performed, not just that it was similar.

## Word Study 3: τούτοις (toutois) - from οὗτος (houtos) G3778

**Parsing in Jude 1:7:** D-DPM (Demonstrative Pronoun, Dative Plural **Masculine**)

### Definition
- **Strong's:** "The he (she or it), i.e. this or that" - referring to someone or something present or previously mentioned

### Usage
- "this" (157x), "these" (59x), "he" (31x), "the same" (28x)

### Critical Grammatical Point
τούτοις is **Dative Plural MASCULINE**

The dative case indicates the object of comparison: "in like manner **to these**"

The **masculine gender** is significant because:
- Greek nouns must agree in gender with their referents
- Sodom (Σόδομα) = Feminine
- Gomorrah (Γόμορρα) = Feminine
- Cities (πόλεις) = Feminine
- Angels (ἀγγέλους) in v6 = **Masculine**

| Γόμορρα | N-NSF* | Gomorrah (Nom Sg **Fem/Neut**) |
| πόλεις | N-NPF | cities (Nom Pl **Fem**) |
| τὸν | T-ASM | the (Acc Sg Masc) |

| **τούτοις** | D-DPM | to these (Dat Pl **Masc**) |
| ἐκπορνεύσασαι | V-AAP-NPF | having fornicated (Nom Pl **Fem**) |
| ἀπελθοῦσαι | V-2AAP-NPF | having gone after (Nom Pl **Fem**) |
| σαρκὸς | N-GSF | flesh (Gen Sg Fem) |

*Note: N1904 parses Σόδομα/Γόμορρα as feminine. Strong's (G4670, G1116) classifies Σόδομα as neuter and Γόμορρα as neuter or feminine. Either way, they are not masculine.

### Grammatical Tension
- The subject of v7 (Sodom, Gomorrah, cities) is **feminine**
- The participles describing their actions (ἐκπορνεύσασαι, ἀπελθοῦσαι) are **feminine**
- But τούτοις is **masculine**

## Word Study 4: σαρκὸς ἑτέρας - "Strange Flesh"

**Greek:** σαρκὸς ἑτέρας (sarkos heteras)

### σάρξ (sarx) G4561
- **Meaning:** flesh, body, human nature
- **Parsing:** N-GSF (Genitive Singular Feminine)

### ἕτερος (heteros) G2087
- **Meaning:** other, another, different (of a different kind)
- **Parsing:** A-GSF (Adjective, Genitive Singular Feminine)
- **Note:** Distinct from ἄλλος (allos) which means "another of the same kind"

### Significance
ἕτερος implies **qualitative difference** - not just "other flesh" but "flesh of a different kind/nature"

This could mean:
1. Different from their own (homosexuality - human with human of same sex)
2. Different category entirely (human with non-human)

The interpretation affects how we understand the "in like manner" comparison.

## Textual Note

Both Nestle 1904 and Textus Receptus contain τὸν ὅμοιον τρόπον τούτοις with only minor word order variation:
- N1904: τὸν ὅμοιον τρόπον τούτοις
- TR: τὸν ὅμοιον τούτοις τρόπον

The grammatical question is consistent across textual traditions.
