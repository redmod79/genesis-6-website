# Analysis: Identity of "Sons of God" in Genesis 6

## Framework: Two Major Views

| View | "Sons of God" = | "Daughters of Men" = | Result |
|------|-----------------|---------------------|--------|
| **Angel View** | Fallen angels | Human women | Nephilim = angel-human hybrids |
| **Sethite View** | Godly line of Seth | Ungodly line of Cain | Nephilim = mighty men from mixed marriages |

---

## Analysis of Evidence

### Evidence Point 1: Old Testament Usage of "bene elohim"

**Job 1:6; 2:1; 38:7** - The phrase "bene elohim" (sons of God) clearly refers to angels:
- They present themselves before the LORD
- Satan is "among them"
- In Job 38:7, they existed at earth's creation (before humans)

**Observation:** In Job, "bene elohim" unambiguously refers to heavenly beings.

**Question:** Does this require the same meaning in Genesis 6?

**Answer:** Not necessarily. Context determines meaning. Job is wisdom literature describing heavenly scenes. Genesis 6 is historical narrative in an earthly setting about human multiplication and wickedness.

---

### Evidence Point 2: The Immediate Context (Genesis 4-6)

The literary structure of Genesis 4-5 establishes a contrast:

| Genesis 4:16-24 | Genesis 4:25-5:32 |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| Cain's line | Seth's line |
| "Went out from the presence of the LORD" | "Began to call upon the name of the LORD" |
| Lamech: polygamy, murder, boasting | Enoch: "walked with God" |
| No mention of God | Godly heritage traced |
| Ends with arrogance | Ends with Noah |

**Genesis 6:1-2 follows immediately after this contrast:**
- "Men began to multiply" (both lines increasing)
- "Daughters were born unto them" (both lines having daughters)
- "Sons of God saw the daughters of men" (intermarriage begins)

**Observation:** The context strongly suggests Genesis 6 is describing the mixing of the two lines just established in chapters 4-5.

---

### Evidence Point 3: Moses's Vocabulary (from prerequisite studies)

**Moses-angel-terminology study found:**
- Moses uses "malak" (angel/messenger) 28+ times for celestial beings
- Moses NEVER uses "bene elohim" (sons of God) for angels anywhere in the Pentateuch

**Moses-human-god-relationship-terms study found:**
- Moses uses "children of the LORD your God" (bene YHWH elohekem) for Israel (Deu 14:1)
- Moses uses various terms for godly humans: "my servant," "my people," "holy nation"
- "Sons of God" appears ONLY in Genesis 6:2,4 in all of Moses's writings

**Observation:** If Moses meant angels, why didn't he use his normal vocabulary ("malak")? The unique usage suggests something other than Moses's typical angel references.

---

### Evidence Point 4: God's Response (Genesis 6:3)

After the "sons of God" take wives:

> "And the LORD said, **My spirit shall not always strive with man**, for that **he also is flesh**: yet **his days** shall be an hundred and twenty years."

**Key observations:**
1. God speaks of **man** (not angels)
2. God says "**he also is flesh**" (the sons of God are flesh?)
3. God limits **his days** to 120 years (human lifespan)

**Question:** If the "sons of God" were angels, why does God:
- Speak only of man?
- Call them "flesh"?
- Limit human days as the consequence?

**The angel view requires:** The punishment falls on humans even though angels sinned. This is possible but requires explanation.

**The Sethite view explains:** The godly line (also flesh, also human) corrupted themselves, so God limits human lifespan.

---

### Evidence Point 5: The Wickedness Described (Genesis 6:5-7)

> "GOD saw that the wickedness of **man** was great in the earth... And the LORD said, I will destroy **man** whom I have created..."

**Observation:** The entire passage (6:1-7) focuses on HUMAN wickedness:
- v.1: men multiplying
- v.3: God striving with man, man is flesh
- v.5: wickedness of man
- v.6: repented making man
- v.7: will destroy man

**Question:** If fallen angels were the primary actors, why is the focus entirely on human sin?

---

### Evidence Point 6: Jesus on Angels and Marriage

> "For in the resurrection they neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as **the angels of God in heaven**." (Matt 22:30)

**Key phrase:** "angels of God **in heaven**"

**Angel view argues:**
- This refers to angels in heaven, not fallen angels
- Fallen angels may have different capabilities
- The text doesn't say angels "cannot" marry, just that they don't

**Sethite view argues:**
- Jesus teaches angels don't marry as a category
- No biblical evidence angels CAN marry or reproduce
- This directly contradicts the angel interpretation

**Analysis:** Jesus's statement is about the nature of angels generally ("as the angels"). He doesn't distinguish between faithful and fallen angels. The natural reading is that angels, by nature, do not marry.

---

### Evidence Point 7: 2 Peter 2:4 and Jude 6 (from prerequisite studies)

**Previous studies concluded:**

**2 Peter 2:4:**
- Describes the angelic rebellion (cast down, chains of darkness)
- Revelation 12 is the interpretive key
- "Cast down" angels remain active (Rev 12:12)
- Genesis 6 connection is NOT required by the text

**Jude 6-7:**
- Angels "kept not their first estate" = abandoned their position
- "Left their own habitation" = cast out of heaven
- "In like manner" connects surrounding cities to Sodom, not angels to Sodom
- "Strange flesh" = homosexuality (men pursuing men)
- Genesis 6 connection is NOT established

**Observation:** Neither passage explicitly mentions Genesis 6, "sons of God," Nephilim, or angels marrying humans. The connection is assumed, not stated.

---

### Evidence Point 8: The Nephilim

> "There were **giants** [nephilim] in the earth in those days; **and also after that**..." (Gen 6:4)

**Hebrew:** נְפִלִים (nephilim) - possibly from נָפַל (naphal) = "to fall"

**Key observation:** "and also after that" - Nephilim existed BEFORE and AFTER the sons of God incident.

**Numbers 13:33:** "And there we saw the giants [nephilim], the sons of Anak, which come of the giants [nephilim]..."

**Question:** If Nephilim were angel-human hybrids destroyed in the flood, how did they exist "after that" and appear in Canaan?

**Angel view must explain:**
- Either some survived the flood
- Or the same sin repeated after the flood
- Or "after that" means something else

**Sethite view explains:**
- Nephilim = mighty men, men of renown (as the verse says)
- They existed before and after the intermarriage
- They were human warriors/rulers, not hybrids

---

### Evidence Point 9: "Daughters of Men" (benoth adam)

If "sons of God" = angels (non-human), then "daughters of men" = human women.

But if this is a contrast of species, why use "adam" (man)?

**Alternative reading:**
- "Sons of God" (bene elohim) = men who belonged to God (godly line)
- "Daughters of men" (benoth adam) = daughters of mankind generally, or specifically of ungodly humanity

**Genesis 6:1** says daughters were born "unto them" - unto whom? The "men" (adam) who were multiplying. This suggests "daughters of men" simply means human women, making "sons of God" also human.

## Unresolved Questions

### For the Angel View:
1. Why does Moses never use "bene elohim" for angels elsewhere?
2. Why does God respond by judging "man" who is "flesh"?
3. How do angels marry if they don't marry (Matt 22:30)?
4. How did Nephilim exist "after that" (post-flood)?
5. Where does Scripture explicitly connect 2 Pet 2:4/Jude 6 to Genesis 6?

### For the Sethite View:
1. Why does Job use "bene elohim" for angels?
2. How does intermarriage produce "giants"?
3. Why would mixed marriage warrant such severe judgment?
4. Why doesn't Moses use "bene YHWH" as in Deuteronomy 14:1?
