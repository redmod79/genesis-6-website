# Word Studies: Second Temple Literature and Genesis 6

## H430 -- elohim (אֱלֹהִים)

### Lexicon Entry
- **Transliteration:** 'elohiym
- **Pronunciation:** el-o-heem
- **Part of Speech:** masculine noun (plural form)
- **Total OT occurrences:** 2,685
- **Definition:** Plural of eloah; gods in the ordinary sense; but specifically used (especially with the article) of the supreme God; occasionally applied by way of deference to magistrates; and sometimes as a superlative -- angels, exceeding, God (gods)(-dess, -ly), (very) great, judges, mighty.

| Translation | Count | Percentage |

| God (the true God) | ~2,350 | ~87.5% |
| gods (false deities) | ~140 | ~5.2% |
| the judges | 4 | 0.15% |
| the judge | 1 | 0.04% |
| mighty | 2 | 0.07% |
| than the angels | 1 | 0.04% |

### Elohim as "Judges" -- Key Passages

The KJV translates elohim as "the judges" in:
- **Exodus 21:6** -- "his master shall bring him unto **the judges** [elohim]"
- **Exodus 22:8** -- "the master of the house shall be brought unto **the judges** [elohim]"
- **Exodus 22:9** -- "the cause of both parties shall come before **the judges** [elohim]" (twice)

And as "the judge" in:
- **1 Samuel 2:25** -- "**the judge** [elohim] shall judge him"

### Elohim as "Mighty" -- Key Passages
- **Genesis 23:6** -- "a **mighty** [elohim] prince among us"
- **Exodus 9:28** -- "**mighty** [elohim] thunderings"

### Significance for Genesis 6

The wide semantic range of elohim demonstrates that "sons of elohim" (bene ha'elohim) is NOT linguistically limited to "sons of angels" or "sons of divine beings." The phrase could legitimately mean:
1. **Sons of God** -- godly descendants (Sethite view)
2. **Sons of the judges/mighty ones** -- sons of rulers or nobles (Targum Onkelos: "sons of nobles")
3. **Sons of the gods** -- divine beings (angel view)

The Targum Onkelos rendering "sons of nobles/great ones" reflects the elohim = "mighty ones/judges" semantic range. Symmachus translated "sons of kings" -- also reflecting the ruler/judge meaning. Both are linguistically legitimate based on the documented usage of elohim.

## G4396 -- prophetes (προφήτης)

### Lexicon Entry
- **Transliteration:** prophetes
- **Pronunciation:** prof-ay-tace
- **Part of Speech:** masculine noun
- **Total NT occurrences:** 134
- **Definition:** From a compound of pro (before/forth) and phemi (to speak); a foreteller, prophet; by analogy, an inspired speaker

### Translation Distribution

| Translation | Count | Percentage |
|-------------|-------|------------|
| prophets | 69 | 51.5% |
| prophet | 34 | 25.4% |
| a prophet | 12 | 9.0% |
| A prophet | 6 | 4.5% |
| the prophets | 4 | 3.0% |
| other forms | 9 | 6.7% |

### Key Usage: Titus 1:12

Paul calls Epimenides **ἴδιος αὐτῶν προφήτης** ("their own prophet") using the identical Greek word (prophetes) used for:
- Moses (Acts 3:22: "A **prophet** shall the Lord your God raise up")
- Jesus (Luke 24:19: "a **prophet** mighty in deed and word")
- OT prophets generally (Hebrews 1:1: "God, who at sundry times spake by the **prophets**")

**This is critical for the Jude/1 Enoch question:** If Paul can call a pagan Cretan poet "a prophet" (G4396) without endorsing Epimenides' theology, religion, or other writings, then Jude can say Enoch "prophesied" (G4395) without endorsing 1 Enoch's entire theological framework.

### The Range of Prophetes

The NT use of prophetes extends beyond "inspired canonical author" to include:
- **Biblical prophets:** Isaiah, Jeremiah, Daniel, etc.
- **NT prophets:** Agabus, church prophets (1 Cor 12:28; Eph 4:11)
- **Pagan figures who spoke truth:** Epimenides (Titus 1:12)
- **Historical figures who predicted:** Caiaphas unwittingly prophesied (John 11:51)

The word describes the ACT of speaking forth truth, not necessarily the canonical authority of the speaker's entire body of work.

## G4395 -- propheteuo (προφητεύω)

### Lexicon Entry
- **Transliteration:** propheteuo
- **Pronunciation:** prof-ate-yoo-o
- **Part of Speech:** verb
- **Total NT occurrences:** 29
- **Definition:** From prophetes; to foretell events, divine, speak under inspiration, exercise the prophetic office

### Translation Distribution

| Translation | Count | Percentage |
|-------------|-------|------------|
| prophesied | 6 | 20.7% |
| prophesy | 4 | 13.8% |
| prophesieth | 4 | 13.8% |
| Prophesy (imperative) | 3 | 10.3% |
| they shall prophesy | 2 | 6.9% |
| other forms | 10 | 34.5% |

### Key Usage: Jude 1:14

**Ἐπροφήτευσεν** (Epropheteusen) -- Aorist Active Indicative 3rd Singular
- "And Enoch also, the seventh from Adam, **prophesied** of these..."

**Grammatical Analysis:**
- The aorist tense indicates a completed past action -- Enoch did prophesy
- The subject is the MAN Enoch ("the seventh from Adam"), not the BOOK of 1 Enoch
- Jude attributes the prophetic act to the historical person, using his genealogical identification
- Compare Matthew 7:22 where false prophets claim "have we not **prophesied** in thy name?" -- the word propheteuo can describe the act of speaking forth without validating the speaker's authority

### The Jude/Paul Parallel in Word Study

| Feature | Jude 1:14 | Titus 1:12 |
|---------|-----------|------------|
| Source cited | 1 Enoch (attributed to Enoch) | Epimenides of Crete |
| Verb/Title used | ἐπροφήτευσεν (propheteuo, G4395) | προφήτης (prophetes, G4396) |
| Word family | Same root: prophet- | Same root: prophet- |
| Speaker identified as | "Enoch, seventh from Adam" | "one of themselves, a prophet of their own" |
| Content affirmed | Truth about coming judgment | Truth about Cretan character |
| Source endorsed? | Not the book, but the statement | Not Epimenides' religion, but the statement |
| Paul's verdict | -- | "This witness is TRUE" (v.13) |

**Conclusion from word study:** The propheteuo/prophetes word family is used for speaking truth, not for endorsing an entire corpus. Both Jude and Paul cite a true statement from a non-canonical source using the "prophet" word family, confirming that quoting does not equal endorsing.

## Summary of Word Study Findings

1. **H430 (elohim):** The semantic range includes God, gods, judges, mighty ones. "Sons of elohim" is not linguistically limited to "sons of angels." The Targum Onkelos and Symmachus readings reflect legitimate semantic options.

2. **G4396 (prophetes):** The word means "one who speaks forth." Paul applies it to a pagan poet (Titus 1:12). This establishes that calling someone a "prophet" or saying they "prophesied" does not canonize their entire body of work.

3. **G4395 (propheteuo):** Jude says Enoch "prophesied" using the same word family Paul uses for Epimenides. The act of prophesying affirms the truth of the specific statement, not the theological framework of the source.
