# Analysis: Second Temple Literature and Genesis 6

## Overview

This analysis addresses four interconnected questions about the relationship between Second Temple Jewish literature (particularly 1 Enoch) and the biblical text of Genesis 6:1-4, examining whether 1 Enoch preserves original meaning or represents later expansion, whether the angel interpretation was unanimous, whether Jude's quotation endorses 1 Enoch's theology, and whether the Pentateuch naturally supports the angel interpretation.

---

## Part 1: The Development Problem -- Genesis 6 to 1 Enoch

### The Scale of Expansion

Genesis 6:1-4 consists of exactly **four verses** containing approximately 100 Hebrew words. This text mentions:
- Men multiplying, daughters born (v.1)
- "Sons of God" seeing and taking wives (v.2)
- God limiting human days to 120 years, calling them "flesh" (v.3)
- Giants (nephilim) in the earth, mighty men, men of renown (v.4)

1 Enoch 6-16 (the Book of the Watchers) expands this into **eleven chapters** containing elaborate narrative with:
- **Named angels:** Semjaza (the leader), Azazel, and 18 other named "Watchers"
- **An oath-bound conspiracy:** The angels swear an oath on Mount Hermon to carry out their plan
- **Forbidden knowledge teachings:** Angels teach metallurgy, cosmetics, enchantments, astrology, weather signs
- **Giant mythology:** Giants 3,000 ells tall who consume all produce, then turn to cannibalism
- **An intermediary petition:** Enoch serves as a go-between, carrying the angels' petition to God
- **Detailed punishments:** Specific punishments assigned to specific named angels
- **Azazel as scapegoat figure:** Azazel bound and cast into darkness

### The Critical Question: Preservation or Elaboration?

**The claim:** 1 Enoch preserves original traditions about Genesis 6 that the biblical text abbreviated.

**The evidence against:**

1. **The expansion pattern matches midrashic elaboration, not historical preservation.** Jewish midrash characteristically takes brief biblical texts and expands them with named characters, dialogues, narrative details, and theological commentary. The Dead Sea Scroll commentary tradition (pesharim) does the same thing. 1 Enoch follows this exact pattern.

2. **None of 1 Enoch's distinctive details appear in Scripture.** The Bible never mentions:
   - Named fallen angels (Semjaza, Azazel)
   - An oath on Mount Hermon
   - Angels teaching metallurgy or cosmetics
   - Giants 3,000 ells tall
   - Enoch as intermediary for angels
   - Any detail from 1 Enoch's elaborate narrative

3. **The elaboration direction is always one way.** Brief text becomes elaborate narrative; elaborate narrative never becomes brief text. If 1 Enoch contained the "original tradition," it would be inexplicable why Moses would strip ALL of it down to four generic verses with no distinctive details preserved.

4. **Other Second Temple texts elaborate differently.** Jubilees 5:1-11 tells the story differently from 1 Enoch. The Book of Giants (from Qumran) adds different details still. The Targum Pseudo-Jonathan adds its own elaborations. If these were "preserving original tradition," they would agree with each other. Instead, they diverge -- exactly as independent midrashic expansions would.

5. **The composition dates reveal dependency.** 1 Enoch 6-36 is typically dated to the 3rd century BC -- over a millennium after Moses. The Book of Giants is even later. These are Second Temple compositions, not pre-Mosaic traditions.

### Parallel: Other Midrashic Expansions

The same expansion pattern occurs with other biblical texts:
- Genesis 22 (the binding of Isaac) -- The Targums and midrashim add dialogues between Abraham and Isaac, Satan's role, angels weeping, etc. No one argues these "preserve original details."
- Exodus 14 (the Red Sea crossing) -- Midrashic literature adds named Egyptian generals, specific numbers, tribal formations. These are recognized as elaborations.
- Genesis 3 (the fall) -- Later literature adds extensive dialogues between the serpent and Eve, details about the tree, etc.

The pattern is consistent: brief biblical text generates elaborate midrashic expansion. 1 Enoch's relationship to Genesis 6 fits this pattern exactly.

## Part 2: Was the Angel Interpretation Unanimous?

### The Claim

The angel view is often presented as the unanimous understanding of ancient Judaism and early Christianity, supposedly overturned only in modern times.

### The Evidence: It Was NOT Unanimous

**Readings supporting HUMAN identity:**

| Source | Date | Genesis 6:2 Rendering | Nature |
|--------|------|----------------------|--------|
| Targum Onkelos | 2nd cent. AD | "sons of nobles/great ones" | **Human** |
| Symmachus (Greek) | 2nd cent. AD | "sons of kings" | **Human** |
| Standard LXX (Rahlfs) | 3rd cent. BC | "sons of God" (υἱοί, NOT ἄγγελοι) | **Neutral -- used for humans in NT** |
| Peshitta (Syriac) | 2nd cent. AD | "sons of God" | **Neutral** |
| Vulgate (Latin) | 4th cent. AD | "filii Dei" ("sons of God") | **Neutral** |

**Church fathers supporting HUMAN identity:**

| Father | Date | Position |
|--------|------|----------|
| Julius Africanus | c. 160-240 AD | Sethite view |
| John Chrysostom | 347-407 AD | Sethite view |
| Augustine | 354-430 AD | Sethite view (City of God XV.23) |
| Cyril of Alexandria | 376-444 AD | Sethite view |
| Theodoret | 393-458 AD | Sethite view |

**Readings supporting ANGEL identity:**

| Source | Date | Genesis 6:2 Rendering | Nature |
|--------|------|----------------------|--------|
| 1 Enoch 6-16 | 3rd cent. BC | Angels (named "Watchers") | **Angelic** |
| Jubilees 5:1-11 | 2nd cent. BC | Angels | **Angelic** |
| Book of Giants (Qumran) | 2nd-1st cent. BC | Giants from angel unions | **Angelic** |
| Codex Alexandrinus | 5th cent. AD | "angels of God" (ἄγγελοι) | **Angelic** (variant) |
| Philo of Alexandria | 1st cent. AD | Angels | **Angelic** |
| Justin Martyr | c. 100-165 AD | Angel view | **Angelic** |

### Critical Observations from Prior Studies

**From [septuagint-genesis-6-translation](../septuagint-genesis-6-translation/CONCLUSION.md) study:**
- The standard critical text of the LXX (Rahlfs-Hanhart) reads "sons of God" (υἱοί), NOT "angels of God" (ἄγγελοι) in Genesis 6:2
- The same LXX translators who used "angels" in Job 1:6, 2:1, and 38:7 deliberately chose "sons" for Genesis 6
- The "angels" reading in Codex Alexandrinus (5th century) is a later scribal variant
- This distinction shows the translators SAW a contextual difference

**From [genesis-6-lxx-nt-comparison](../genesis-6-lxx-nt-comparison/CONCLUSION.md) study:**
- The LXX Genesis 6:2 uses identical Greek terminology (υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ) to the NT's phrase for human believers (Rom 8:14, 19; Gal 3:26)
- Linguistically, the LXX Genesis 6 phrase is FULLY compatible with human reference

### Conclusion on Unanimity

The angel interpretation was widespread but **not unanimous**. Ancient Jewish sources like Targum Onkelos explicitly chose human readings. The standard LXX translators distinguished Genesis 6 from Job. Major church fathers held the Sethite view. The claim of unanimity is historically false.

## Part 3: Does Jude's Quotation of 1 Enoch Endorse Its Theology?

### The Argument

"Jude quotes 1 Enoch 1:9 in Jude 14-15, calling Enoch's words a 'prophecy.' This validates 1 Enoch and, by extension, its angel interpretation of Genesis 6."

### The Biblical Pattern: Quoting a Source Does Not Endorse Its Entire Theology

**Paul quotes three pagan sources:**

| Passage | Source Quoted | Pagan Author | What Paul Calls Him |
|---------|-------------|--------------|---------------------|
| Acts 17:28a | "In him we live and move and have our being" | Epimenides of Crete | "your own poets" (ποιητῶν) |
| Acts 17:28b | "For we are also his offspring" | Aratus of Soli (Phaenomena) | "your own poets" |
| Titus 1:12 | "Cretians are always liars, evil beasts, slow bellies" | Epimenides of Crete | "a prophet of their own" (προφήτης) |
| 1 Cor 15:33 | "Evil communications corrupt good manners" | Menander (Thais) | (no title given) |

**Critical parallels between Paul and Jude:**

1. **Paul calls Epimenides "a prophet" (prophetes, G4396).** This is the same Greek word used for Isaiah, Jeremiah, and Jesus. Yet no one argues that Paul thereby endorses Epimenides' theology, his other writings, or his Cretan religious system.

2. **Jude says Enoch "prophesied" (propheteuo, G4395).** This is the verb form of the same word Paul uses for Epimenides. Jude attributes the prophecy to the MAN Enoch ("the seventh from Adam"), identifying him historically, not validating the BOOK attributed to him.

3. **Paul affirms the TRUTH of the specific statement** (Titus 1:13: "This witness is true"), not the truth of everything Epimenides wrote or believed.

4. **Jude affirms the TRUTH of the specific prophecy** (God coming in judgment against the ungodly), not the truth of everything in 1 Enoch.

### What Jude 14-15 Actually Quotes

The content of Jude's quotation from 1 Enoch 1:9 is:
- The Lord comes with ten thousands of His saints
- To execute judgment upon all
- To convict the ungodly of their ungodly deeds and hard speeches

**What this content is about:** Universal eschatological judgment against the ungodly.

**What this content is NOT about:** Angels, Genesis 6, Watchers, named fallen angels, giant offspring, forbidden knowledge, or any distinctive 1 Enoch theology.

Jude quotes a statement about final judgment -- a truth thoroughly established throughout Scripture (Deut 33:2; Dan 7:10; Zech 14:5; Matt 25:31; 2 Thess 1:7-8; Rev 19:14). The specific statement Jude quotes would be TRUE regardless of whether 1 Enoch existed.

### The Logical Error

The argument "Jude quotes 1 Enoch, therefore 1 Enoch's angel interpretation of Genesis 6 is validated" commits a logical fallacy:

1. Jude quotes statement X from 1 Enoch
2. 1 Enoch also contains interpretation Y of Genesis 6
3. Therefore Jude endorses interpretation Y

This is a non sequitur. Quoting one true statement from a source does not validate every other statement in that source. Paul's quotation of Epimenides does not validate Epimenides' claim that Zeus was born in Crete. Paul's quotation of Aratus does not validate Stoic pantheism. Jude's quotation of 1 Enoch's judgment prophecy does not validate 1 Enoch's Watcher narrative.

### Additional Evidence: Jude's Context

**From [jude-6-7-angels-sin](../jude-6-7-angels-sin/CONCLUSION.md) study:**
- Jude 6 describes angelic rebellion (leaving their position, cast from heaven -- parallel to Rev 12)
- Jude 6 does NOT describe angels taking human wives
- Jude 7 describes Sodom's sin as homosexuality ("strange flesh")
- "In like manner" connects surrounding cities to Sodom, not angels to sexual sin
- Jude gives THREE examples of judgment (Israel, angels, Sodom) -- three SEPARATE illustrations, not one connected narrative

Even in the immediate context of Jude's letter, the angel reference (v.6) does not match 1 Enoch's narrative of angel-human intermarriage.

## Part 4: The Pentateuch Without Second Temple Imports

### Reading Genesis 1-6 on Its Own Terms

If a reader approached Genesis 1-6 with no knowledge of 1 Enoch, Jubilees, or Second Temple angel traditions, what would the text naturally communicate?

**Genesis 4:** Establishes Cain's ungodly line (murder, arrogance, polygamy -- Lamech)
**Genesis 4:26:** Marks Seth's line: "then began men to call upon the name of the LORD"
**Genesis 5:** Traces Seth's godly line through Enoch ("walked with God") and Noah
**Genesis 6:1-2:** "Sons of God" take "daughters of men" as wives
**Genesis 6:3:** God says: "My spirit shall not always strive with **man**, for that **he also is flesh**"
**Genesis 6:5-7:** God sees human wickedness and resolves to judge

The natural reading in context: The godly line (sons of God, identified by calling on the LORD's name) intermarried with the ungodly line (daughters of men, Cain's descendants). This mixed marriage led to spiritual decline. God's response addresses HUMAN flesh, not angelic transgression.

### The Critical Verse: Genesis 6:3

"My spirit shall not always strive with **man**, for that **he also is flesh**."

God identifies the "sons of God" as:
1. **"Man"** (adam) -- a human term
2. **"Flesh"** (basar) -- a human description
3. Subject to limited "days" -- human mortality

If angels were the problem, why would God:
- Address His response to "man"?
- Call the offenders "flesh"?
- Limit human lifespan as the consequence?
- Focus judgment entirely on the human race (flood)?

**From [genesis-6-sons-of-god_2](../genesis-6-sons-of-god_2/CONCLUSION.md) study:** God's own words in Gen 6:3 identify the "sons of God" as "man" and "flesh." This is the single most decisive textual indicator.

### Moses's Vocabulary Patterns

**From [moses-angel-terminology](../moses-angel-terminology/CONCLUSION.md) study (existing research):**
- Moses uses "malak" (H4397, angel/messenger) for celestial beings 28+ times throughout the Pentateuch
- Moses NEVER uses "bene elohim" (sons of God) for angels
- In Genesis 19:1, when Moses means angels, he writes "angels" (malakhim)
- If Moses intended angels in Genesis 6:2, why did he use completely different terminology?

**From [moses-human-god-relationship-terms](../moses-human-god-relationship-terms/CONCLUSION.md) study:**
- Moses calls Israel "children of the LORD your God" (Deut 14:1) -- humans as "children of God"
- Moses uses "my people," "holy nation," "sons" for godly humans

## Part 5: Moses Strips Pagan Mythology from Genesis

### The Pattern: Moses Systematically Strips Divine Status from Pagan Mythological Entities

The Pentateuch was written in a world saturated with pagan mythology. Surrounding cultures had rich mythologies about divine beings interacting with the physical world. Moses's response to this cultural context is consistent and systematic: he strips divine status from the elements that pagan cultures worshipped.

| Pagan Myth | Moses's Text | What Moses Does |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| Sun = Shamash (deity) | Gen 1:16: "the greater **light**" | Strips divine identity; it is a "light" (maor), not a god |
| Moon = Sin (deity) | Gen 1:16: "the lesser **light**" | Strips divine identity; it is a "light," not a god |
| Stars = divine beings | Gen 1:16: "the stars also" | Dismissive aside: "also" -- not divine, just made |
| Sea monsters = Tiamat (divine rival) | Gen 1:21: "God **created** great whales" | Created things (bara), not divine rivals |
| Man = from divine blood | Gen 2:7: "formed man of **the dust**" | From dust, not divine blood (contra Enuma Elish) |
| Celestial bodies = worthy of worship | Deut 4:19: "should be driven to **worship** them" | Explicitly forbids celestial worship |
| Helel/Day Star = deity | Isa 14:12: "fallen from heaven" | Applied to a mortal king who will "die like men" |

### The Consistency of the Pattern

This pattern is not occasional -- it is **systematic and comprehensive**:

1. **Creation Day 1:** Light is created by God's word, not born from divine union
2. **Creation Day 4:** Sun and moon are functional "lights," deliberately unnamed (in pagan cultures, naming conferred divine status)
3. **Creation Day 5:** Sea creatures (tanninim) are created by God, not primordial rivals
4. **Creation Day 6:** Man from dust, breath from God -- no divine blood component
5. **Post-flood:** Rainbow as covenant sign (Gen 9:13-16), not divine weapon
6. **Deuteronomy:** Explicit prohibition against celestial worship (Deut 4:19)

### The Implication for Genesis 6

If the Pentateuch SYSTEMATICALLY strips pagan mythology -- removing divine status from sun, moon, stars, sea monsters, and human origins -- then reading Genesis 6 as "divine beings mating with humans" would be:

1. **A reversal of the established pattern.** Every other pagan mythological concept is stripped of divine character. Why would Moses suddenly PRESERVE the most sensational myth of all -- divine beings procreating with humans?

2. **A contradiction of Moses's own theological program.** Moses's consistent message is that these "divine" beings of pagan myth are merely created things. Introducing actual divine-human hybridization would undermine his entire program.

3. **An insertion of precisely what Moses elsewhere removes.** Pagan cultures (Mesopotamian, Greek, Canaanite) had myths of divine beings mating with humans. Moses removes such concepts everywhere else but supposedly preserves it in Genesis 6?

**The human interpretation CONTINUES the pattern.** If "sons of God" = godly human line, then Genesis 6 describes human intermarriage and moral decline -- no mythological divine beings involved. This is consistent with everything else Moses writes.

**The angel interpretation REVERSES the pattern.** If "sons of God" = angels/divine beings who mate with humans, then Moses has introduced the very pagan mythology he systematically strips everywhere else.

## Part 6: The Elohim Semantic Range

### From the Word Study (04-word-studies.md)

H430 (elohim) is translated in the KJV as:
- God (the true God): ~87.5% of occurrences
- gods (false deities): ~5.2%
- **the judges: 5 occurrences** (Exo 21:6; 22:8, 9; 1 Sam 2:25)
- **mighty: 2 occurrences** (Gen 23:6; Exo 9:28)

### What This Means for "Sons of God" (bene ha'elohim)

The phrase "sons of God" is built on elohim. Because elohim has a documented range including "judges" and "mighty ones," the phrase "sons of elohim" can mean:
- Sons of God (theological/spiritual relationship)
- Sons of the mighty/powerful (human rulers)
- Sons of the judges (human authorities)
- Sons of the gods (divine council -- as in Job)

**From [psalm-82-gods](../psalm-82-gods/CONCLUSION.md) study:**
- Psalm 82:6 calls human judges "elohim" and "children of the most High"
- Jesus interprets this as referring to humans "unto whom the word of God came" (John 10:34-35)
- The parallel Psalm 58 calls the same group "sons of men"
- Elohim applied to humans is BIBLICALLY ESTABLISHED

The Targum Onkelos translation "sons of nobles" and Symmachus's "sons of kings" both reflect this legitimate semantic range. These are not distortions of the Hebrew but valid renderings based on documented usage of elohim.

## Synthesis: The Six Lines of Evidence Converge

| Question | Finding |
|----------|---------|
| 1. Does 1 Enoch preserve original meaning? | No -- the expansion pattern matches midrashic elaboration; none of 1 Enoch's distinctive details appear in Scripture |
| 2. Was the angel view unanimous? | No -- Targum Onkelos, Symmachus, standard LXX translators, and major church fathers held the human view |
| 3. Does Jude's quotation endorse 1 Enoch? | No -- quoting a true statement does not endorse the source's entire theology (parallel: Paul quoting pagan poets) |
| 4. Does the Pentateuch naturally support the angel view? | No -- the immediate context (Gen 4-5), God's words in Gen 6:3, and Moses's vocabulary all favor the human reading |
| 5. Does Moses's treatment of pagan mythology support the angel view? | No -- the angel view reverses Moses's pattern of stripping pagan mythology from Genesis |
| 6. Does elohim require "divine beings"? | No -- elohim means judges, mighty ones, rulers in documented usage |
