# Word Studies: Origin of the Nephilim

## Key Term 1: Nephilim (נְפִלִים)

### Strong's H5303

**Hebrew:** נָפִיל (naphil), plural: נְפִלִים (nephilim)

**Transliteration:** naphil / nephilim

**Pronunciation:** nef-eel'

**Part of Speech:** Masculine noun

**Root/Etymology:** From נָפַל (naphal, H5307) = "to fall"

**Definition:** According to Strong's: "a feller, i.e. a bully or tyrant" - translated as "giant"

### All Biblical Occurrences

| Reference | Hebrew | KJV Translation | Context |
|-----------|--------|-----------------|---------|
| Genesis 6:4 | הַנְּפִלִים | giants | Pre-flood era |
| Numbers 13:33 (2x) | הַנְּפִילִם | giants | Spies' report of Canaan |

**Total occurrences: 3** (in 2 verses)

### Interpretation Options

1. **"Fallen ones"** - from root naphal (to fall)
   - Could mean: those who fell morally
   - Or: those who cause others to fall (tyrants)
   - Or: the offspring of those who fell (angel view interpretation)

2. **"Giants"** - physical stature
   - Septuagint (LXX) translates as γίγαντες (gigantes)
   - Numbers 13:33 clearly associates with large stature
   - Context in both passages involves size/power

3. **"Mighty men / Tyrants"** - Strong's primary definition
   - "A feller, i.e. a bully or tyrant"
   - Genesis 6:4 describes them as "mighty men... men of renown"

### Critical Observation

The root naphal means "to fall" - but the angel view assumes this refers to "fallen angels." However:

1. The word is used of the Anakim in Numbers 13:33
2. The Anakim have a known human genealogy (Arba > Anak > sons)
3. If "nephilim" required angel parentage, it couldn't apply to the Anakim

**Conclusion:** "Nephilim" describes a TYPE of person (giants/tyrants), not an ORIGIN (angel-human hybrids).

## Key Term 2: Rephaim (רְפָאִים)

### Strong's H7497

**Hebrew:** רְפָאִים (rephaim)

**Transliteration:** repha'im

**Pronunciation:** raw-faw'

**Definition:** "Giants, Rephaim" - an old tribe of giants, ancient inhabitants of Canaan

**Root:** From H7495 רָפָא (rapha) = "to heal, to invigorate"

### KJV Usage

| Translation | Occurrences |
|-------------|-------------|
| "giant(s)" | 17 times |
| "Rephaim" | 8 times |
| **Total** | 25 times |

### Key Occurrences

| Reference | Context |
|-----------|---------|
| Genesis 14:5 | Rephaims smitten by Chedorlaomer |
| Genesis 15:20 | Listed among Canaanite peoples |
| Deuteronomy 2:11 | Compared to Anakim |
| Deuteronomy 2:20 | Zamzummims "accounted" Rephaim |
| Deuteronomy 3:11 | Og, last of the Rephaim |
| Joshua 12:4; 13:12 | Og's territory |
| Joshua 17:15 | Land of the Rephaim |

### Significance

1. **Listed among Canaanite tribes:** Genesis 15:20 lists Rephaim alongside Hittites, Perizzites - all human peoples
2. **Geographic entity:** "Valley of Rephaim" is a place name (Josh 15:8; 2 Sam 5:18)
3. **Human king:** Og is called "remnant of the Rephaim" but rules a human kingdom
4. **No angel connection:** Never attributed to angel parentage

## Key Term 3: Anak / Anakim

### Strong's H6061 / H6062

**Hebrew:** עֲנָק (Anak) / עֲנָקִים (Anakim)

**Transliteration:** Anak / Anaqim

**Pronunciation:** aw-nawk'

**Meaning:** "Long-necked" (suggesting great stature)

### Genealogy in Scripture

```
Arba (H704) - "the father of Anak" (Josh 15:13)
  │         - "a great man among the Anakims" (Josh 14:15)
  │
  └── Anak (H6061) - eponymous ancestor
        │
        ├── Sheshai (H8344)
        ├── Ahiman (H289)
        └── Talmai (H8526)
```

### Key Verses

> **Joshua 14:15:** "Arba was **a great man** among the Anakims"

> **Joshua 15:13:** "Arba **the father of Anak**"

> **Joshua 15:14:** "The three sons of Anak, Sheshai, and Ahiman, and Talmai, **the children of Anak**"

### Significance

1. **Arba explicitly called "a man"** - not angel, not hybrid
2. **Complete human genealogy** - father to sons traceable
3. **Called "nephilim"** in Numbers 13:33 - proves nephilim = giants, not hybrids
4. **Defeated by humans** - Caleb drove out the three sons (Josh 15:14; Judg 1:20)

## Key Term 4: Giant (in 2 Samuel 21)

### Strong's H7498

**Hebrew:** רָפָה (Raphah)

**Transliteration:** raphah

**Pronunciation:** raw-faw'

**Definition:** A Philistine giant, ancestor of several giants in David's time

### 2 Samuel 21 Usage

> **v. 16:** "Ishbibenob, which [was] of **the sons of the giant [H7497]**"
> **v. 18:** "Saph, which [was] of **the sons of the giant [H7497]**"
> **v. 20:** "he also was **born to the giant [H7498]**"
> **v. 22:** "These four were **born to the giant [H7498]** in Gath"

### Significance

1. **"Born to"** - human birth language
2. **"Sons of the giant"** - family lineage, not supernatural origin
3. **"The giant" (H7498)** - a specific person, their ancestor
4. **From Gath** - same location where Anakim remnant remained (Josh 11:22)

**Conclusion:** The giants of Gath were descendants of a human giant ancestor, not angel-human hybrids. The language is consistently human family terminology.

## Key Term 5: Cubit (אַמָּה)

### Strong's H520

**Hebrew:** אַמָּה (ammah)

**Transliteration:** ammah

**Pronunciation:** am-maw'

**Definition:** A cubit (a unit of measure), the forearm (below the elbow)

**Standard measurement:** ~18 inches (1.5 feet)

### Application to Giants

| Giant | Measurement | Estimated Size |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| Og's bed | 9 cubits x 4 cubits | ~13.5 ft x 6 ft |
| Goliath | 6 cubits and a span | ~9.5 ft tall |

**Note on Goliath:** Some manuscripts (LXX, Dead Sea Scrolls) record "4 cubits and a span" (~6.5 ft), which is still very tall but more within natural human range.

### Significance

**Deuteronomy 3:11:** "after the cubit **of a man**"

This phrase confirms:
1. Human measurement standards apply
2. Og was large but measured by human standards
3. No supernatural scale required

## Key Finding from Word Studies

### The Term "Nephilim" in Numbers 13:33

The spies called the Anakim "nephilim." But we know:
- The Anakim descended from Arba (human)
- Arba fathered Anak (human genealogy)
- Anak fathered Sheshai, Ahiman, Talmai (human family)

**If "nephilim" required angel parentage:**
- The term could NOT apply to the Anakim
- But the inspired text DOES apply it to them

**Therefore:** "Nephilim" describes physical/social characteristics (giants, tyrants), NOT origin (angel-human hybrids).
