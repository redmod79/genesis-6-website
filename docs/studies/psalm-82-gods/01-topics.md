# Topics Research: Psalm 82:6 - "Ye Are Gods"

## Question

Psalm 82:6 says "I have said, Ye are gods; and all of you are children of the most High. But ye shall die like men."

The angel view argues:
- "Gods" and "children of the Most High" refer to divine beings who sinned
- "Die like men" implies they weren't originally men
- This supports divine beings interacting with/becoming like humans (parallel to Genesis 6)

Does Psalm 82 describe divine beings or human judges?

## The Key Evidence: "Elohim" for Human Judges

### Strong's H430 (elohim)

The Hebrew word אֱלֹהִים (elohim) is translated in the KJV as:
- **God** - 2,346 times
- **gods** (false deities) - 244 times
- **judge/judges** - 5 times
- **angels** - once

**Critical finding:** The KJV translates elohim as "judges" in:

| Passage | KJV Translation | Context |
|---------|-----------------|---------|
| Exodus 21:6 | "unto the **judges**" | Master brings servant to judges |
| Exodus 22:8 | "unto the **judges**" | Theft cases |
| Exodus 22:9 | "before the **judges**" | Property disputes |
| Exodus 22:28 | "the **gods**" (margin: "judges") | Don't revile authorities |

This proves that "elohim" can refer to **human judges** in Scripture.

## Jesus's Interpretation: John 10:34-36

> **John 10:34-35** - "Jesus answered them, Is it not written in your law, I said, Ye are gods? If he called them **gods, unto whom the word of God came**, and the scripture cannot be broken..."

### What Jesus Says

1. The "gods" were those "**unto whom the word of God came**"
2. This refers to Israel receiving the Law at Sinai
3. Jesus uses this to argue from lesser to greater (a fortiori)

### The Logic of Jesus's Argument

- If Scripture calls **humans** who received God's word "gods"
- How much more can the Son of God claim deity?
- This only works if the "gods" in Psalm 82 are **HUMAN**

If they were already divine beings, Jesus's argument collapses.

## Selected Topics for This Study

### Topic 1: The Context of Psalm 82

| Verse | Content | Type of Activity |
|-------|---------|------------------|
| v.1 | "God...judgeth among the gods" | Divine oversight of **judges** |
| v.2 | "How long will ye judge unjustly?" | **Judicial** failure |
| v.3 | "Defend the poor and fatherless" | **Judicial** duty |
| v.4 | "Deliver the poor and needy" | **Judicial** duty |
| v.5 | "They know not, neither will they understand" | Human ignorance |
| v.6 | "I have said, Ye are gods" | Honorific title for judges |
| v.7 | "But ye shall die like men" | Mortality despite title |
| v.8 | "Arise, O God, judge the earth" | Appeal to true Judge |

Every element describes **judicial activity** and **human failure**.

### Topic 2: "Die Like Men" - What It Means

The phrase כְּאָדָם (ke'adam) = "like/as men"

This doesn't necessarily mean "you weren't originally men." Compare:

> **Hosea 6:7** - "But they **like men** have transgressed the covenant"

Israel transgressed "like men" - but they WERE men. The phrase emphasizes **mortality** and **common human fate** despite their honored position.

### Topic 3: Parallel Psalm - Psalm 58

> **Psalm 58:1** - "Do ye indeed speak righteousness, O congregation? do ye judge uprightly, **O ye sons of men**?"

Same theme (corrupt judges), but explicitly calls them "**sons of men**" - humans.
