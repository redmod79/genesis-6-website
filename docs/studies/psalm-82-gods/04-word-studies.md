# Word Studies: Psalm 82:6 - "Ye Are Gods"

## Hebrew Terms

### אֱלֹהִים (elohim) - "Gods/God/Judges"

**Strong's H430**

- **Transliteration:** elohim
- **Pronunciation:** el-o-heem'
- **Part of Speech:** Masculine noun (plural)
- **Etymology:** Plural of H433 (eloah)

**Definition:** Gods in the ordinary sense; but specifically used of the supreme God; occasionally applied by way of deference to **magistrates/judges**.

**KJV Translation Frequency:**
- "God" - 2,346 occurrences
- "god/gods" - 244 occurrences
- "**judge/judges**" - 5 occurrences
- "angels" - 1 occurrence
- Other: goddess, mighty, great

**Critical Finding:** The KJV translators understood that elohim can mean "judges" - human authorities exercising divine prerogative.

**Passages Where Elohim = Judges:**

| Passage | Hebrew | KJV Translation |
|---------|--------|-----------------|
| Exodus 21:6 | הָאֱלֹהִים | "the judges" |
| Exodus 22:8 | הָאֱלֹהִים | "the judges" |
| Exodus 22:9 (2x) | הָאֱלֹהִים... אֱלֹהִים | "the judges...the judges" |
| Exodus 22:28 | אֱלֹהִים | "the gods" (margin: judges) |

**Psalm 82:1, 6 Usage:**

> v.1: "God [אֱלֹהִים] standeth in the congregation of the mighty; he judgeth among the **gods** [אֱלֹהִים]"
> v.6: "I have said, Ye [are] **gods** [אֱלֹהִים]"

**Significance:** The same word (elohim) is used for both the true God and the human judges. Context determines meaning. In Psalm 82, the context (judging unjustly, defending the poor) indicates human judges.

### עֶלְיוֹן (elyon) - "Most High"

**Strong's H5945**

- **Transliteration:** elyon
- **Pronunciation:** el-yone'
- **Part of Speech:** Adjective/Noun
- **Etymology:** From H5927 (alah - to ascend)

**Definition:** An elevation; as adjective: lofty; as title: the Supreme (God)

**KJV Translation Frequency:**
- "high" - 18 occurrences
- "most high" - 9 occurrences
- "upper" - 8 occurrences
- "higher" - 4 occurrences
- "highest" - 2 occurrences
- Other: above, uppermost

**Psalm 82:6 Usage:**

> "all of you [are] children of the **most High** [עֶלְיוֹן, elyon]"

**Significance:** "Most High" (elyon) is a title for God. Being called "children of the Most High" indicates a special relationship to God - but this is used of humans throughout Scripture (see Matt 5:9; Luke 6:35; Rom 8:14).

---

### בֵּן (ben) - "Son/Child"

**Strong's H1121**

- **Transliteration:** ben
- **Pronunciation:** bane
- **Part of Speech:** Masculine noun
- **Etymology:** From H1129 (banah - to build)

**Definition:** A son; broadly: children, people, subjects; used in many relational compounds.

**Common Compounds:**

| Hebrew Phrase | Translation | Meaning |
|---------------|-------------|---------|
| בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל | bene Yisrael | "children of Israel" |
| בְּנֵי אָדָם | bene adam | "sons of men" (humans) |
| בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים | bene ha'elohim | "sons of God" |
| בְּנֵי עֶלְיוֹן | bene elyon | "children of the Most High" |

**Psalm 82:6 Usage:**

> "all of you [are] **children** [בְּנֵי, bene] of the most High"

**Significance:** "Children of" expresses relationship, not necessarily biological offspring. Judges are "children of the Most High" because they represent His authority.

---

### אָדָם (adam) - "Man/Men"

**Strong's H120**

- **Transliteration:** adam
- **Pronunciation:** aw-dawm'
- **Part of Speech:** Masculine noun
- **Etymology:** From H119 (adam - to be red)

**Definition:** Man, mankind, human being; used generically for humanity.

**KJV Translation Frequency:**
- "man" - 408 occurrences
- "men" - 121 occurrences
- Other: person, mankind, Adam

**Psalm 82:7 Usage:**

> "But ye shall die **like men** [כְּאָדָם, ke'adam]"

**Key Parallel - Hosea 6:7:**

> "But they **like men** [כְּאָדָם, ke'adam] have transgressed the covenant"

**Significance:** The identical phrase (כְּאָדָם) is used in Hosea 6:7 of ISRAEL - who were obviously men. The phrase means "in the manner of men" or "like ordinary mortals," not "become men from a non-human state."

---

### שָׁפַט (shaphat) - "Judge"

**Strong's H8199**

- **Transliteration:** shaphat
- **Pronunciation:** shaw-fat'
- **Part of Speech:** Verb
- **Etymology:** A primitive root

**Definition:** To judge, govern, vindicate, punish; to act as judge, execute judgment.

**KJV Translation Frequency:**
- "judge" - 119 occurrences
- "judge" (noun) - 60 occurrences
- Other: plead, defend, deliver

**Psalm 82 Usage:**

> v.1: "he **judgeth** [יִשְׁפֹּט, yishpot] among the gods"
> v.2: "How long will ye **judge** [תִּשְׁפְּטוּ, tishpetu] unjustly"
> v.8: "Arise, O God, **judge** [שָׁפְטָה, shophtah] the earth"

**Significance:** The verb "judge" (shaphat) appears 3 times in this psalm. The entire context is **judicial** - exactly what human judges do.

---

### מוּת (muth) - "Die"

**Strong's H4191**

- **Transliteration:** muth
- **Pronunciation:** mooth
- **Part of Speech:** Verb
- **Etymology:** A primitive root

**Definition:** To die, be dead; causatively: to kill, execute.

**Psalm 82:7 Usage:**

> "But ye shall **die** [תְּמוּתוּן, temutun] like men"

**Significance:** The verb is in the imperfect tense with the subject "you" (plural). These beings WILL die - indicating they are mortal beings who can die, not immortal beings receiving a new punishment.

---

### שַׂר (sar) - "Prince"

**Strong's H8269**

- **Transliteration:** sar
- **Pronunciation:** sar
- **Part of Speech:** Masculine noun
- **Etymology:** From H8323 (sarar - to have dominion)

**Definition:** A head person, chief, captain, ruler, prince.

**Psalm 82:7 Usage:**

> "and fall like one of the **princes** [הַשָּׂרִים, hasarim]"

**Significance:** Princes (sarim) are human rulers. The comparison is to other human rulers who have fallen (died). This parallel confirms the "die like men" refers to ordinary human mortality.

## Greek Terms (New Testament)

### θεός (theos) - "God/gods"

**Strong's G2316**

- **Transliteration:** theos
- **Pronunciation:** theh'-os
- **Definition:** A deity; the supreme Divinity; figuratively: a magistrate

**John 10:34-35 Usage:**

> "Is it not written in your law, I said, Ye are **gods** [θεοί, theoi]?"
> "If he called them **gods** [θεούς, theous], unto whom the word of God came..."

**Significance:** Jesus uses the Greek theos (gods) to translate the Hebrew elohim of Psalm 82:6. He identifies the "gods" as those "unto whom the word of God came" - humans who received divine revelation.

## Summary: Word Studies and Their Implications

### The Key Terms in Psalm 82:6-7

| Hebrew | Transliteration | Meaning in Context |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|
| אֱלֹהִים | elohim | Judges (honorific title) |
| בְּנֵי עֶלְיוֹן | bene elyon | Children of the Most High (relational) |
| כְּאָדָם | ke'adam | Like men (manner of death) |
| הַשָּׂרִים | hasarim | The princes (parallel comparison) |

### Critical Findings

| Term | Evidence | Conclusion |
|------|----------|------------|
| אֱלֹהִים (elohim) | Translated "judges" in Exodus 21-22 | Can mean human authorities |
| בְּנֵי עֶלְיוֹן (bene elyon) | "Children of God" used for believers throughout Scripture | Relational, not ontological |
| כְּאָדָם (ke'adam) | Used of Israel in Hosea 6:7 who WERE men | Manner, not transformation |
| שָׁפַט (shaphat) | Used 3x in psalm | Judicial context throughout |

### Jesus's Identification (John 10:35)

Jesus identifies the "gods" as those "unto whom the word of God came" - **HUMANS** who received God's revelation at Sinai.
