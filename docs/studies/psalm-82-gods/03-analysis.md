# Verse Analysis: Psalm 82:6 - "Ye Are Gods"

## The Argument Examined

### The Angel View Claim

"Psalm 82:6 refers to divine beings ('gods,' 'children of the Most High') who sinned. The phrase 'die like men' implies they weren't originally men. This supports divine beings interacting with/becoming like humans, parallel to Genesis 6."

### The Question

Does Psalm 82 describe divine beings who will "die like men," or human judges who will die despite their honored title?

---

## Part 1: Jesus's Definitive Interpretation

### John 10:34-36

> **John 10:34-35** - "Jesus answered them, Is it not written in your law, I said, Ye are gods? If he called them **gods, unto whom the word of God came**, and the scripture cannot be broken..."

### Who Were the "Gods"?

Jesus identifies them as those "**unto whom the word of God came**."

This refers to:
- Israel at Sinai receiving the Law
- Those who received God's revelation
- **HUMANS** who received divine communication

### Jesus's Argument Requires Human Referent

Jesus argues from lesser to greater:
1. Scripture calls **mere humans** who received God's word "gods"
2. Therefore, how can you accuse the Son of God of blasphemy for claiming deity?

**This argument only works if the "gods" in Psalm 82 are HUMANS.**

If they were already divine beings:
- There's no "lesser to greater" logic
- The argument collapses
- Jesus would be comparing himself to fallen angels (problematic)

### The Verdict

Jesus himself interprets the "gods" of Psalm 82 as **humans who received God's word**.

---

## Part 2: The Context of Psalm 82

### The Activity Described

| Verse | Activity | Human or Divine? |
|-------|----------|------------------|
| v.2 | "How long will ye **judge unjustly**?" | **Judicial** - human judges |
| v.2 | "accept the persons of the wicked" | **Partiality** - human corruption |
| v.3 | "**Defend** the poor and fatherless" | **Judicial duty** |
| v.3 | "do **justice** to the afflicted and needy" | **Judicial duty** |
| v.4 | "**Deliver** the poor and needy" | **Judicial duty** |
| v.4 | "rid them out of the hand of the wicked" | **Judicial protection** |
| v.5 | "They **know not**, neither will they **understand**" | **Human ignorance** |

Every activity is **judicial** - the work of human judges, not heavenly council meetings.

### The Charges

1. Judging unjustly (v.2)
2. Showing partiality to the wicked (v.2)
3. Failing to defend the poor (v.3)
4. Failing to deliver the needy (v.4)

These are charges against **corrupt human judges** - the same charges made against Israel's judges throughout the OT (see Isaiah 1:23; 10:1-2; Amos 5:12).

---

## Part 3: "Elohim" as a Title for Human Judges

### The Hebrew Evidence

The word אֱלֹהִים (elohim) in Psalm 82:1, 6 is the same word translated:
- "God" (the true God)
- "gods" (false deities)
- "**judges**" (human authorities)

### KJV Translations of Elohim as "Judges"

| Passage | Hebrew | KJV Translation | Context |
|---------|--------|-----------------|---------|
| Exodus 21:6 | הָאֱלֹהִים | "the judges" | Servant brought before authorities |
| Exodus 22:8 | הָאֱלֹהִים | "the judges" | Theft case adjudication |
| Exodus 22:9 | הָאֱלֹהִים | "the judges" | Property dispute resolution |
| Exodus 22:28 | אֱלֹהִים | "the gods" (margin: judges) | Command not to revile authorities |

### Why "Elohim" for Human Judges?

Judges represent God's authority on earth. They are called "elohim" because:
1. They exercise divine prerogative (judgment)
2. They represent God in civil matters
3. They are God's deputies in the administration of justice

This is an **honorific title**, not an ontological claim about their nature.

---

## Part 4: "Die Like Men" - What It Actually Means

### The Hebrew Phrase

**כְּאָדָם תְּמוּתוּן** (ke'adam temutun) = "like men you shall die"

### The Angel View's Interpretation

"'Die like men' implies they weren't originally men."

### Problems with This Interpretation

**1. Parallel usage in Hosea 6:7**

> **Hosea 6:7** - "But they **like men** [כְּאָדָם, ke'adam] have transgressed the covenant"

Israel transgressed "like men" - but Israel WAS composed of men. The phrase emphasizes the **manner** or **common human pattern**, not non-human origin.

**2. Parallel in Psalm 82:7b**

> "and **fall like one of the princes**"

"Fall like one of the princes" doesn't mean they weren't princes. It compares them to princes who fell (died). Similarly, "die like men" compares them to common mortals despite their exalted title.

**3. The Rhetorical Force**

The point is contrast:
- You are called "gods" and "children of the Most High" (v.6)
- **Nevertheless** you will die like any other mortal (v.7)

The contrast is between their **exalted title** and their **mortal reality** - not between original divine nature and punishment by mortality.

**4. The Logic**

If these were divine beings, why would "dying like men" be a punishment? Angels don't die. The threat of mortality only makes sense if addressed to beings who could otherwise expect to be exempt - i.e., those with the honorific title "gods" who might think themselves above ordinary mortality.

---

## Part 5: Psalm 58 - The Parallel

### Same Theme, Explicit Identification

> **Psalm 58:1** - "Do ye indeed speak righteousness, O congregation? do ye judge uprightly, O ye **sons of men**?"

Same elements:
- Corrupt judges failing to judge righteously
- Addressed as "congregation" (cf. Psalm 82:1)
- Called "**sons of men**" (בְּנֵי אָדָם, bene adam) - explicitly HUMAN

### The Conclusion in Both Psalms

| Psalm 82:8 | Psalm 58:11 |
|------------|-------------|
| "Arise, O God, **judge the earth**" | "he is a **God that judgeth in the earth**" |

Both psalms conclude by appealing to God as the true Judge who will set things right - because human judges have failed.

---

## Part 6: "Children of the Most High"

### The Phrase

**בְּנֵי עֶלְיוֹן** (bene elyon) = "children/sons of the Most High"

### Does This Require Divine Origin?

No. Throughout Scripture, "children of..." is used for those who:
- Have a special relationship with someone
- Are characterized by a quality
- Represent someone's authority

### Examples of "Children of God" for Humans

| Passage | Reference | Who? |
|---------|-----------|------|
| Matthew 5:9 | "shall be called the children of God" | Peacemakers |
| John 1:12 | "power to become the sons of God" | Believers |
| Romans 8:14 | "they are the sons of God" | Those led by Spirit |
| Galatians 3:26 | "ye are all the children of God" | Believers |
| 1 John 3:1-2 | "we should be called the sons of God" | Believers |

Humans can be called "children of God" or "children of the Most High" without being ontologically divine.

---

## Part 7: Why This Matters for Genesis 6

### The Angel View's Use of Psalm 82

The angel view argues:
1. Psalm 82 shows divine beings called "gods" and "children of the Most High"
2. These beings sinned and will "die like men"
3. This parallels Genesis 6 where divine "sons of God" interacted with humans

### Why This Fails

1. **Jesus identifies the "gods" as humans** who received God's word (John 10:35)
2. **The context is judicial** - human judges failing in their duties
3. **"Elohim" means "judges"** in multiple OT passages (Exodus 21-22)
4. **Psalm 58 (parallel)** explicitly calls them "sons of men"
5. **"Die like men"** emphasizes mortality despite title, not change of nature

Psalm 82 provides **no support** for the angel view of Genesis 6.

---

## Part 8: Summary of Analysis

### What Psalm 82 Actually Describes

| Element | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| "Gods" (elohim) | Honorific title for human judges |
| "Children of the Most High" | Those with special relationship to God |
| "Judge unjustly" | Corrupt human judicial activity |
| "Defend the poor" | Judicial duty |
| "Die like men" | Mortality despite exalted title |
| "Fall like one of the princes" | Death like other fallen rulers |

### The Evidence Summary

| Evidence | Finding |
|----------|---------|
| Jesus's interpretation | "gods" = those who received God's word (humans) |
| Psalm context | Judicial duties - human judges |
| "Elohim" usage | Translated "judges" in Exodus 21-22 |
| Psalm 58 parallel | Same theme, explicitly "sons of men" |
| "Like men" phrase | Used of those who ARE men (Hosea 6:7) |
| "Children of God" | Used of human believers throughout Scripture |

**Conclusion:** Psalm 82 describes human judges who bear an honorific title but will die like any other mortal. It provides no evidence for divine beings becoming human or "dying like men."
