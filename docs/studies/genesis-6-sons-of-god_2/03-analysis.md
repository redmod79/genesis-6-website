# Comprehensive Analysis: Identity of the "Sons of God" in Genesis 6

## Introduction

This study synthesizes findings from 12 prerequisite studies to answer the question: Who are the "sons of God" in Genesis 6:2,4? Each section evaluates an argument used for the angel view and presents the biblical counter-evidence.

---

## Part 1: The Primary Text (Genesis 6:1-7)

### What the Text Actually Says

| Element | Description | Observation |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Setting | "when men began to multiply" | Earthly, population growth |
| Actors | "sons of God" | Identity disputed |
| Action | "saw... took them wives" | Marriage - normal human vocabulary |
| God's Response | "strive with man," "he also is flesh" | Addresses humans, not angels |
| Judgment | Destroy "man whom I have created" | Human-focused |

### The Critical Verse: Genesis 6:3

> "And the LORD said, My spirit shall not always strive with **man**, for that **he also is flesh**: yet his days shall be an hundred and twenty years."

**Key observations:**
1. God addresses "**man**" - not angels
2. God calls the "sons of God" "**flesh**" - a human term
3. The 120 years = time until judgment (Noah's preaching period, not a lifespan limit - post-flood patriarchs lived much longer)
4. The word "**also**" (גַּם, gam) emphasizes common nature with humanity

**If the sons of God were angels, why does God:**
- Call them "man"?
- Call them "flesh"?
- Focus judgment entirely on humanity?

The angel view has no satisfying answer.

## Part 2: Moses's Terminology

### From: [moses-angel-terminology](../moses-angel-terminology/CONCLUSION.md) Study

**Finding:** Moses uses "malak" (מַלְאָךְ) for angels 28+ times in the Pentateuch:
- "Angel of the LORD" - 14 occurrences
- "Angels" (plural) - 4 occurrences
- "Angel of God" - 3 occurrences
- Other "angel" references - 7 occurrences

**Moses NEVER uses "sons of God" (bene elohim) for angels anywhere else.**

### From: [moses-human-god-relationship-terms](../moses-human-god-relationship-terms/CONCLUSION.md) Study

**Finding:** Moses calls Israel "children of the LORD your God" (בְּנֵי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם) in Deuteronomy 14:1.

**The question:** If Moses wanted to indicate angels in Genesis 6, why didn't he use "malak" - his consistent term for celestial beings?

### From: [septuagint-genesis-6-translation](../septuagint-genesis-6-translation/CONCLUSION.md) Study

**Finding:** The standard Septuagint text translates Genesis 6:2 as "sons of God" (υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ), NOT "angels of God."

**Remarkable fact:** The same LXX translators who translated Job 1:6; 2:1; 38:7 as "angels of God" chose to translate Genesis 6:2 differently - as "sons of God." They saw a contextual distinction.

| Passage | Context | LXX Translation |
|---------|---------|-----------------|
| Genesis 6:2 | Earthly marriage | "sons of God" |
| Job 1:6 | Heavenly throne room | "angels of God" |
| Job 2:1 | Heavenly throne room | "angels of God" |
| Job 38:7 | Creation | "my angels" |

**The "angels" reading in some LXX manuscripts (e.g., Codex Alexandrinus) is a 5th-century variant - likely scribal harmonization.**

## Part 3: The Genesis 4-5 Context

### The Two Lines Established

Genesis 4-5 establishes a clear contrast:

**Cain's Line (Chapter 4):**
- Went out from the LORD's presence (4:16)
- Built cities, developed culture without God (4:17-22)
- Polygamy introduced - Lamech takes two wives (4:19)
- Violence celebrated - Lamech boasts of murder (4:23-24)

**Seth's Line (Chapter 5):**
- Replaced Abel (4:25)
- "Then began men to call upon the name of the LORD" (4:26)
- Enoch "walked with God" (5:24)
- Noah "found grace in the eyes of the LORD" (6:8)

### The Natural Reading

Genesis 6:1-2 describes the **merger of these lines**:
- "Daughters of men" (human daughters generally, including Cain's line)
- "Sons of God" (godly line of Seth)
- The intermarriage erased the distinction
- Result: universal moral corruption (6:5)

This fits the pattern Scripture repeats:
- Solomon's wives turned his heart (1 Kings 11)
- Israel intermarried and served foreign gods (Judges 3)
- Post-exilic community threatened by intermarriage (Ezra 10)

## Part 4: Jesus's Teaching on Angels and Marriage

### Matthew 22:30; Mark 12:25; Luke 20:35-36

> "For in the resurrection they neither marry, nor are given in marriage, but are as **the angels of God in heaven**."

### The Angel View's Response

"This only applies to 'angels in heaven' - not fallen angels who left heaven."

### Problems with This Response

**1. Jesus describes angelic NATURE, not just location**
- "Are AS the angels" - what they are like
- Luke 20:36: resurrected saints will be "**equal unto the angels**" - same nature
- The argument: angels don't marry → resurrected saints won't marry
- This only works if angels CATEGORICALLY don't marry

**2. No Scripture says fallen angels gain new abilities**
- Rebellion does not grant reproductive powers
- This is speculation, not biblical evidence

**3. The logic is backwards**
- The angel view: "Angels CAN marry, so Jesus must mean only heavenly angels"
- Jesus's actual teaching: "Angels DON'T marry, so resurrected saints won't either"

**4. Hebrews 1:7, 14 defines angelic nature**
- "Who maketh his angels **spirits**"
- "Are they not all **ministering spirits**"
- Spirits by nature, not biological beings

### From: [angels-physical-form](../angels-physical-form/CONCLUSION.md) Study

**The claim:** Angels ate food (Genesis 18), so they can reproduce.

**The refutation:** This is a non sequitur (logical fallacy). Temporary physical manifestation for a divine mission does not prove permanent biological reproductive capability.

| What Gen 18-19 demonstrates | What Gen 18-19 does NOT demonstrate |
|-----------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Angels can appear in human form | Angels have reproductive biology |
| Angels can eat food | Angels can produce offspring |
| Angels appeared as ordinary men | Fallen angels gain abilities |

**Critical observation:** The angels in Genesis 18-19 ate food but **produced no offspring**. If physical form included reproductive capability, where is the evidence?

## Part 5: 2 Peter 2:4 - "Angels That Sinned"

### From: [2-peter-2-4-angels-that-sinned](../2-peter-2-4-angels-that-sinned/CONCLUSION.md) Study

**The claim:** 2 Peter 2:4 describes angels who sinned in Genesis 6.

**The finding:** The Genesis 6 connection is NOT required by the text. Revelation 12 provides a clearer explanation.

### The Revelation 12 Connection

| 2 Peter 2:4 | Revelation 12 |
|-------------|---------------|
| "angels that sinned" | "the dragon... and his angels" (v.7) |
| "cast them down" | "cast out... cast down" (v.9-10) |
| "chains of darkness" | Active with "great wrath" (v.12) |
| "reserved unto judgment" | "knoweth he hath but a short time" (v.12) |

**Key insight:** Revelation 12 uses the same "cast down" language as 2 Peter 2:4, yet those angels remain ACTIVE. "Chains of darkness" describes their condemned spiritual state, not physical imprisonment.

### The "Two Classes" Problem

The angel view often claims two groups:
1. Imprisoned angels (Genesis 6 sinners)
2. Active demons (other fallen angels)

**Problem:** This distinction has no biblical basis. The same language ("cast down," "reserved for judgment") describes beings who are clearly active (Revelation 12:12-17).

## Part 6: Jude 6-7 - "Strange Flesh"

### From: [jude-6-7-angels-sin](../jude-6-7-angels-sin/CONCLUSION.md) Study

**The claim:** Jude 6-7 connects angels' sin to sexual sin with humans, and "strange flesh" means angelic flesh.

### What Jude 6 Actually Says

> "And the angels which **kept not their first estate**, but **left their own habitation**..."

**Analysis:**
- "First estate" (ἀρχή, arche) = position, rank, domain
- "Left their own habitation" (οἰκητήριον, oiketerion) = dwelling place (heaven)

**Meaning:** The angels abandoned their position and were cast from heaven. This describes the rebellion (Revelation 12), not taking human wives.

### What "In Like Manner" Connects

> "Even as Sodom and Gomorrha, **and the cities about them in like manner**..."

**The most natural reading:** "In like manner" connects the surrounding cities (Admah, Zeboim) to Sodom - they committed the same sins.

**Less natural:** Connecting to angels would require assuming the angels' sin was sexual.

### What "Strange Flesh" Means

> "...going after **strange flesh**" (σαρκὸς ἑτέρας, sarkos heteras)

**Analysis:**
- ἕτερος (heteros) = other, different (qualitatively)
- The men of Sodom pursued MEN, not the natural female counterpart
- Romans 1:26-27 describes homosexuality as "against nature"
- **From God's design perspective, men pursuing men IS "different flesh"**

**Critical observation:** The men of Sodom **didn't know** the visitors were angels - they appeared as ordinary men (Genesis 19:5). They weren't pursuing angels; they were pursuing what they thought were men.

## Part 7: 1 Peter 3:19 - "Spirits in Prison"

### From: [1-peter-3-spirits-in-prison](../1-peter-3-spirits-in-prison/CONCLUSION.md) Study

**The claim:** Christ preached to imprisoned fallen angels between His death and resurrection.

**The finding:** This passage describes Christ preaching BY the Spirit THROUGH Noah to those who were disobedient during Noah's time.

### The Key Phrase: "By Which"

> "quickened by the Spirit: **By which** also he went and preached..."

The Spirit by whom Christ was made alive is the same Spirit by whom He preached. 1 Peter 1:10-11 establishes that "the Spirit of Christ" was IN the Old Testament prophets.

### The Timing: "In the Days of Noah"

> "Which sometime were disobedient, **when once the longsuffering of God waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was a preparing**..."

The disobedience (and preaching) happened **during** Noah's time - not after Christ's death.

### 2 Peter 2:5 Confirms Noah Preached

> "saved Noah the eighth person, **a preacher of righteousness**"

Christ preached through Noah to those who are NOW "spirits in prison" (the dead awaiting judgment).

## Part 8: The Nephilim Problem

### From: [nephilim-origin](../nephilim-origin/CONCLUSION.md) Study

**The claim:** Nephilim were angel-human hybrids - explaining their giant size.

### The Timing Problem (Fatal to Hybrid Theory)

> **Genesis 6:4** - "There were giants in the earth **in those days; and also after that**, when the sons of God came in unto the daughters of men..."

The Nephilim existed:
- **BEFORE** the "sons of God" unions ("in those days")
- **AFTER** the unions ("and also after that")

**If Nephilim were offspring of these unions, they could only exist AFTER. The text explicitly states they pre-existed the unions.**

### Post-Flood "Nephilim" Have Human Lineage

> **Numbers 13:33** - "And there we saw the giants [nephilim], **the sons of Anak**..."

> **Joshua 15:13-14** - "...the city of **Arba the father of Anak**... the children of Anak."

**The Anakim genealogy is fully human:**
```
Arba ("a great man" - Josh 14:15)
  └── Anak
       ├── Sheshai
       ├── Ahiman
       └── Talmai
```

If "nephilim" required angel parentage, the term couldn't apply to the Anakim. But Scripture does apply it.

### Every Post-Flood Giant Is Human

| Giants | Description | Reference |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| Anakim | "Sons of Anak" - human genealogy | Josh 15:13-14 |
| Emim | "A people great, and many, and tall" | Deut 2:10-11 |
| Zamzummim | "A people great, and many, and tall" | Deut 2:20-21 |
| Og | "A man" - king of Bashan | Deut 3:11 |
| Goliath | "Philistine of Gath" | 1 Sam 17:4 |
| Giants of Gath | "Born to the giant" - human family | 2 Sam 21:16-22 |

**Not one post-flood giant is attributed to angel parentage.**

### Pre-Flood Longevity Explains Size

Genesis 5 records lifespans of 800-970 years. Longer life means:
- Extended growth periods
- More time for physical development
- Different environmental conditions

No supernatural hybridization is required.

## Part 10: Psalm 82 and "Gods"

### From: [psalm-82-gods](../psalm-82-gods/CONCLUSION.md) Study

**The claim:** Psalm 82:6 ("Ye are gods... die like men") describes divine beings punished with mortality, supporting the angel view.

### Jesus's Definitive Interpretation

> **John 10:34-35** - "If he called them **gods, unto whom the word of God came**, and the scripture cannot be broken..."

Jesus identifies "gods" as those who **received God's word** (humans at Sinai).

### Why Jesus's Interpretation Is Decisive

Jesus's argument:
- If Scripture calls **mere humans** "gods" (because they received God's word)
- Then the Son of God cannot be accused of blasphemy

**This argument ONLY WORKS if the "gods" in Psalm 82 are HUMANS.**

### "Elohim" = Judges in Exodus

| Passage | Context |
|---------|---------|
| Exodus 21:6 | Servant brought before "the judges" (elohim) |
| Exodus 22:8, 9 | Case before "the judges" (elohim) |
| Exodus 22:28 | "Revile not the gods" (elohim = judges) |

### "Die Like Men" Does Not Imply Non-Human Origin

Hosea 6:7 uses the same phrase of Israel - who WERE men:
> "But they **like men** have transgressed the covenant..."

## Part 11: Deuteronomy 32:8 Variant

### From: [deuteronomy-32-8-sons-of-god](../deuteronomy-32-8-sons-of-god/CONCLUSION.md) Study

**The claim:** The Dead Sea Scrolls reading "sons of God" proves the term refers to divine beings.

### Even If DSS Is Original, It Doesn't Help

| What Deut 32:8 Describes | What Genesis 6 Describes |
|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| God divides territories | "Sons of God" take wives |
| Administrative assignment | Sexual reproduction |
| National boundaries | Children born |
| Divine beings as overseers | Divine beings as husbands |
| God is the active agent | "Sons of God" act independently |

**The contexts are completely different.**

### The Logical Gap

1. DSS says nations correspond to "sons of God" ✓
2. Therefore "sons of God" = divine beings ✓ (possibly)
3. Therefore Genesis 6 "sons of God" = divine beings ✗
4. Therefore angels mated with humans ✗

**Step 3→4 is unsupported.** Even if divine beings exist and oversee nations, this proves nothing about reproductive capability.

## Part 12: The Severity of the Flood

### From: [flood-judgment-severity](../flood-judgment-severity/CONCLUSION.md) Study

**The claim:** Only genetic corruption explains why the flood was so severe.

### The Text's Stated Reasons

Every reason given in Genesis 6 is **MORAL**:
- Wickedness (moral)
- Evil thoughts (moral)
- Corruption before God (moral)
- Violence (moral)

**The text never mentions genetics.**

### Why This Flood Was More Severe

| Situation | Extent | Judgment |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| Genesis 6 | Universal - "all flesh," "every imagination" | Worldwide flood |
| Numbers 25 (Baal Peor) | Localized | 24,000 died |
| Judges 3 (Canaanites) | National | 8 years bondage |
| 1 Kings 11 (Solomon) | Individual | Kingdom divided |

**The difference is not TYPE of sin but EXTENT of corruption.**

The pre-flood corruption was TOTAL:
- **Every** imagination only evil
- **All** flesh corrupted
- Only Noah righteous

### Jesus's Description

> **Matthew 24:37-38** - "For as in the days that were before the flood they were **eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage**..."

Jesus emphasizes normal human activities and spiritual ignorance - NOT supernatural corruption.

## Summary: Why the Sethite View Is Better Supported

### Evidence Favoring Sethite View

| Factor | Evidence |
|--------|----------|
| Immediate context (Gen 4-5) | Godly/ungodly line contrast established |
| Moses's terminology | Uses "malak" for angels, never "bene elohim" |
| Genesis 6:3 | Calls them "man" and "flesh" |
| Jesus's teaching | Angels do not marry (categorical) |
| 2 Peter 2:4 | Genesis 6 connection NOT required |
| Jude 6-7 | "Strange flesh" = homosexuality; connection assumed |
| Nephilim timing | Existed BEFORE the unions (Gen 6:4) |
| Post-flood giants | All have human genealogies |
| "Corrupt way" | ALWAYS moral in parallels |
| LXX translation | Says "sons," not "angels" - translators saw difference |

### Evidence Favoring Angel View

| Factor | Evidence | Counter-Evidence |
|--------|----------|------------------|
| Job's "bene elohim" | Clearly heavenly beings | Different context (heavenly throne vs. earthly marriage) |
| 2 Peter 2:4 | "Angels that sinned" near flood | Rev 12 explains; Gen 6 not required |
| Jude 6 | "Left habitation" | Describes rebellion, not reproduction |
| 1 Enoch | Angel interpretation | Extra-biblical; not authoritative |

### The Decisive Factors

**1. Genesis 6:3** - God calls them "flesh" and addresses "man"
**2. Jesus's teaching** - Angels do not marry (categorical statement about nature)
**3. Moses's vocabulary** - Never uses "bene elohim" for angels
**4. Nephilim timing** - Existed BEFORE the unions (fatal to hybrid theory)
**5. Cross-reference failure** - 2 Peter 2:4 and Jude 6-7 don't require Genesis 6 connection
