# Topics Research: Septuagint Translation of Genesis 6:2

## Question

Does the Septuagint (LXX) translate "sons of God" in Genesis 6:2 as "angels of God"? And if so, what does this mean for interpretation?

---

## Important Principle

**The Septuagint is a translation, not the inspired original.**

The LXX is a Greek translation of the Hebrew Scriptures made by Jewish scholars, primarily in Alexandria, Egypt, beginning around the 3rd century BC. While valuable as an ancient witness to Jewish interpretation, it reflects the **interpretive choices of the translators**, not the authoritative meaning of the text.

The Hebrew Masoretic Text is the inspired original. Translations (including the LXX, Vulgate, and modern versions) reflect human understanding of that original.

## Selected Topics for This Study

### Topic 1: Translation Terminology

This study examines how ancient translations rendered the Hebrew phrase בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים (bene ha'elohim):

| Translation | Date | Genesis 6:2 Rendering |
|-------------|------|----------------------|
| Septuagint (LXX) | 3rd cent. BC | υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ = "sons of God" |
| Codex Alexandrinus | 5th cent. AD | ἄγγελοι τοῦ θεοῦ = "angels of God" (variant) |
| Peshitta (Syriac) | 2nd cent. AD | "sons of God" |
| Vulgate (Latin) | 4th cent. AD | "filii Dei" = "sons of God" |
| Targum Onkelos | 2nd cent. AD | "sons of nobles" (human) |
| Symmachus (Greek) | 2nd cent. AD | "sons of the kings" (human) |

### Topic 2: LXX Translator Distinctions

The LXX translators made a notable distinction between how they rendered the same Hebrew phrase in different books:

| Passage | Hebrew | LXX Greek | LXX Translation |
|---------|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| Genesis 6:2 | בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים | υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ | **sons of God** |
| Genesis 6:4 | בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים | υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ | **sons of God** |
| Job 1:6 | בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים | ἄγγελοι τοῦ θεοῦ | **angels of God** |
| Job 2:1 | בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים | ἄγγελοι τοῦ θεοῦ | **angels of God** |
| Job 38:7 | בְּנֵי אֱלֹהִים | ἄγγελοί μου | **my angels** |

**Critical finding:** The same Hebrew phrase was translated differently based on context:
- In Genesis 6 (earthly scene of marriage): "sons of God"
- In Job (heavenly throne-room with Satan): "angels of God"
