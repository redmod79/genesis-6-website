# Verse Analysis: Septuagint Translation of Genesis 6:2

## The LXX Translator Distinction

### Key Observation

The Septuagint translators made a **significant distinction** between Genesis and Job, even though the Hebrew phrase is identical:

| Book | Hebrew | Greek Translation | Context |
|------|--------|-------------------|---------|
| Genesis 6 | bene ha'elohim | υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ (huioi tou theou) | Earthly marriage scene |
| Job 1, 2, 38 | bene ha'elohim | ἄγγελοι τοῦ θεοῦ (angeloi tou theou) | Heavenly throne room |

**This is remarkable:** The LXX translators knew how to write "angels" (ἄγγελοι) in Greek. They used it in Job. But they chose NOT to use it in Genesis 6. Why?

### Analysis: Why the Distinction?

The LXX translators saw something in the **context** that led them to make different translation choices:

**Genesis 6 Context:**
- Earthly scene on "the face of the earth" (v. 1)
- Men multiplying; daughters born (human reproduction)
- "Took them wives" - normal marriage vocabulary
- God's response focuses on "man" who is "flesh" (v. 3)
- Human lifespan limited to 120 years (v. 3)

**Job 1-2 Context:**
- Heavenly throne-room scene
- Beings "present themselves before the LORD"
- Satan among them
- No marriage, no reproduction, no earthly activity

**Job 38:7 Context:**
- Creation account
- Before humans existed
- "Morning stars" and "sons of God" together
- Clearly celestial beings

### The Contextual Principle

The LXX translators applied a principle we should also apply: **context determines meaning**.

The same phrase can have different referents in different settings:
- In a heavenly throne room with Satan → angelic beings
- In an earthly scene of marriage → humans in relationship with God

## Manuscript Evidence

### The Standard Text (Rahlfs-Hanhart)

The critical edition of the Septuagint (Rahlfs-Hanhart) reads:

**Genesis 6:2:** οἱ **υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ** = "the sons of God"

This is the standard, scholarly text of the LXX.

**Source verification:**
- [Blue Letter Bible LXX Genesis 6:2](https://www.blueletterbible.org/lxx/gen/6/2/)
- Full Greek text: ἰδόντες δὲ οἱ **υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ** τὰς θυγατέρας τῶν ἀνθρώπων...
- Confirms: υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ (huioi tou theou) = "sons of God" (NOT angeloi)

### Variant Readings

Some manuscripts have different readings:

| Manuscript | Date | Genesis 6:2 Reading |
|------------|------|---------------------|
| **Codex Vaticanus (B)** | 4th century AD | υἱοί ("sons") |
| **Codex Alexandrinus (A)** | 5th century AD | ἄγγελοι τοῦ θεοῦ ("angels of God") |

### Analysis of Variants

**Codex Alexandrinus reading "angels":**
- This is a later manuscript (5th century)
- Likely a scribal harmonization with Job
- Not the original LXX reading
- Reflects what one scribe (or tradition) believed, not what the text says

**Manuscript variants tell us what scribes thought, not what is correct.** Later harmonization is common - scribes often "fixed" perceived inconsistencies.

## Other Ancient Translations

### Peshitta (Syriac) - 2nd century AD

Translates Genesis 6:2 as "**sons of God**" (bnay Alaha)

### Vulgate (Latin) - 4th century AD (Jerome)

Translates Genesis 6:2 as "**filii Dei**" = "sons of God"

### Targum Onkelos (Aramaic) - 2nd century AD

Translates Genesis 6:2 as "**sons of nobles**" (bene rabrevaya)

**Significance:** This explicitly interprets "sons of God" as humans - specifically, noble or powerful men.

### Symmachus (Greek) - 2nd century AD

Translates Genesis 6:2 as "**sons of the kings**" (huioi ton basileon)

**Significance:** Another explicit human interpretation - sons of rulers/kings.

## Summary of Ancient Translation Evidence

| Translation | Date | Genesis 6:2 Rendering | Interpretation |
|-------------|------|----------------------|----------------|
| LXX (standard) | 3rd cent. BC | "sons of God" | Literal |
| Peshitta | 2nd cent. AD | "sons of God" | Literal |
| Vulgate | 4th cent. AD | "sons of God" | Literal |
| Targum Onkelos | 2nd cent. AD | "sons of nobles" | **Human** |
| Symmachus | 2nd cent. AD | "sons of the kings" | **Human** |
| Codex Alexandrinus | 5th cent. AD | "angels of God" | **Angel** (variant) |

**Finding:** The majority of ancient translations render this as "sons of God" (literal). Two translations (Targum Onkelos, Symmachus) explicitly interpret it as humans. Only a later variant (Codex Alexandrinus) has "angels."

## What This Evidence Actually Shows

### The Claim

"The Septuagint translates Genesis 6:2 as 'angels of God,' proving the angel view."

### The Reality

1. **The standard LXX text says "sons of God," NOT "angels"**
   - The critical edition reads υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ (huioi tou theou)
   - This is not disputed among scholars

2. **The LXX translators distinguished Genesis 6 from Job**
   - Same Hebrew → different Greek based on context
   - They knew the word for "angels" and chose not to use it in Genesis 6

3. **The "angels" reading is a later variant**
   - Found in Codex Alexandrinus (5th century)
   - Likely scribal harmonization with Job
   - Not the original translation

4. **Other ancient translations support the human view**
   - Targum Onkelos: "sons of nobles"
   - Symmachus: "sons of the kings"

## Connection to Moses-Angel-Terminology Study

The prerequisite study found:

> "Moses consistently uses 'angel' (malak) when referring to celestial messengers."
> "Moses did NOT use 'sons of God' for angels anywhere in the Pentateuch."

If Moses meant "angels" in Genesis 6:2, he could have written:
- מַלְאֲכֵי הָאֱלֹהִים (mal'ake ha'elohim) = "angels of God"
- מַלְאָכִים (mal'akim) = "angels"

Instead, he wrote בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים (bene ha'elohim) = "sons of God."

The LXX translators recognized this distinction. They knew how to translate "malak" (as ἄγγελος). They knew Moses didn't use that word in Genesis 6, so they didn't add it.

## The Fundamental Issue

### Translations Are Not Authoritative

A translation is the work of human interpreters. It tells us what the translators thought the text meant, not what it actually means.

Even if the entire LXX tradition had "angels of God" in Genesis 6:2 (which it does not):
- This would only tell us what the translators believed
- It would not determine what the Hebrew text actually means
- The question must be answered from the Hebrew original and its context

### The Question Must Be Answered from Scripture

**Doctrinal questions are answered from Scripture, not from translations.**

The LXX is valuable as an ancient witness to Jewish interpretation. But it remains a translation - the opinions of translators, not the inspired original.

## Patterns Identified

1. **LXX context-sensitivity:** Translators used context to choose between "sons" and "angels"
2. **Genesis 6 = "sons":** Standard LXX consistently uses υἱοί in Genesis 6
3. **Job = "angels":** LXX translators saw Job's heavenly scenes as requiring ἄγγελοι
4. **"Angels" variant is late:** Codex Alexandrinus (5th century) - likely harmonization
5. **Human interpretations exist:** Targum Onkelos, Symmachus explicitly chose human readings
