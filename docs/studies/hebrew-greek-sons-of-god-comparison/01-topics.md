# Topics Research

## Question
Does the NT Greek usage of "sons of God" (υἱοὶ θεοῦ) match the Hebrew Genesis 6 usage of "sons of God" (בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים)? Specifically, does the NT use huios (G5207) in a similar manner to how the OT uses ben (H1121)?

## Research Framework

### The Hebrew Phrase (Genesis 6:2, 4)
**בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים** (bene ha'elohim)
- בְּנֵי (bene) = sons of (construct plural of בֵּן/ben)
- הָאֱלֹהִים (ha'elohim) = the God / God

### The Greek Phrase (NT)
**υἱοὶ θεοῦ** (huioi theou)
- υἱοὶ (huioi) = sons (nominative plural of υἱός/huios)
- θεοῦ (theou) = of God (genitive of θεός/theos)

### The Core Question
Is **huios** the semantic equivalent of **ben**?
- If yes, then NT usage of huios theou informs the semantic range of ben elohim
- If NT applies huios theou to humans, then ben elohim linguistically CAN apply to humans

## Key Observations from Word Studies

### Hebrew בֵּן (ben) - H1121
Appears **4,906 times** in OT with broad semantic range:
1. Literal son/offspring
2. Descendants/posterity
3. Members of a group ("sons of the prophets")
4. Those characterized by a quality ("sons of valor")
5. Age expressions ("son of X years")
6. Divine relationship ("sons of God")

### Greek υἱός (huios) - G5207
Appears **382 times** in NT with similar semantic range:
1. Literal son/offspring
2. Descendants/posterity ("sons of Abraham")
3. Those characterized by quality ("sons of disobedience")
4. Spiritual relationship ("sons of God")
5. Messianic titles ("Son of Man," "Son of God," "Son of David")

### Translation Relationship
The LXX consistently translates Hebrew בֵּן (ben) with Greek υἱός (huios), establishing them as translation equivalents.
