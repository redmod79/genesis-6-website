# Word Studies

## בֵּן (ben) - H1121

**Original:** בֵּן
**Transliteration:** ben
**Pronunciation:** bane
**Definition:** A son (as a builder of the family name), in the widest sense

### Etymology
From the root בָּנָה (banah, H1129) meaning "to build." A son is one who "builds" or continues the family.

### Usage Statistics
- **Occurrences:** 4,906 times in OT
- **Primary translations:** "son" (2,978×), "children" (1,568×), "old" (135×), plus many other renderings

### Semantic Categories

#### 1. Literal Offspring
> Genesis 4:25: "And Adam knew his wife again; and she bare a **son** (בֵּן), and called his name Seth"

#### 2. Descendants/Posterity
> Deuteronomy 23:3: "The **children** (בֵּן) of the third generation... shall enter into the congregation"

#### 3. Members of a Group
> 1 Kings 20:35: "A certain man of the **sons of the prophets** (בְּנֵי הַנְּבִיאִים)"
- Not literal offspring, but members of the prophetic guild

#### 4. Characterized by a Quality
> Judges 19:22: "**sons of Belial** (בְּנֵי בְלִיַּעַל)" = worthless men
> 1 Samuel 26:16: "**sons of death** (בְּנֵי מָוֶת)" = deserving death
> Deuteronomy 3:18: "**sons of valor** (בְּנֵי חַיִל)" = valiant warriors

#### 5. Age Designation
> Genesis 5:32: "Noah was five hundred years old (בֶּן חֲמֵשׁ מֵאוֹת שָׁנָה)"
- Literally: "son of five hundred years"

#### 6. Divine Relationship
> Genesis 6:2: "**sons of God** (בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים)"
> Deuteronomy 14:1: "Ye are **children** (בָּנִים) of the LORD your God"
> Hosea 1:10: "**sons of the living God** (בְּנֵי אֵל־חָי)"

### Key Finding for This Study
Hebrew בֵּן is used for humans as God's children:
- Deuteronomy 14:1 - Israel called "sons of the LORD"
- Hosea 1:10 - Israel called "sons of the living God"
- Isaiah 43:6 - God calls Israel "my sons...my daughters"
- Exodus 4:22 - Israel is God's "firstborn son"

**The Hebrew word בֵּן CAN and DOES refer to humans in divine relationship.**

## υἱός (huios) - G5207

**Original:** υἱός
**Transliteration:** huios
**Pronunciation:** hwee-os'
**Definition:** A son (sometimes of animals), used very widely of immediate, remote, or figuratively, kinship

### Etymology
Apparently a primary word in Greek.

### Usage Statistics
- **Occurrences:** 382 times in NT
- **Primary translations:** "son" (85×), "Son of man" (87×), "Son of God" (49×), "children" (49×), "Son" (42×), plus others

### Semantic Categories

#### 1. Literal Offspring
> Matthew 1:21: "she shall bring forth a **son** (υἱόν), and thou shalt call his name JESUS"

#### 2. Descendants/Posterity
> Galatians 3:7: "they which are of faith, the same are the **children** (υἱοί) of Abraham"
- Not literal descendants, but spiritual heirs

#### 3. Members of a Group
> Matthew 9:15: "**children of the bridechamber** (υἱοὶ τοῦ νυμφῶνος)" = wedding attendants
> Mark 3:17: "**sons of thunder** (υἱοὶ βροντῆς)" = James and John

#### 4. Characterized by a Quality
> Ephesians 2:2: "**sons of disobedience** (υἱοῖς τῆς ἀπειθείας)" = disobedient people
> 1 Thessalonians 5:5: "**sons of light** (υἱοὶ φωτός)" = enlightened believers
> John 17:12: "**son of perdition** (υἱὸς τῆς ἀπωλείας)" = Judas

#### 5. (Not prominent in Greek)
Age designation idiom is Hebraic, not native Greek.

#### 6. Divine Relationship
> Romans 8:14: "**sons of God** (υἱοὶ θεοῦ)" = believers
> Galatians 3:26: "**sons of God** (υἱοὶ θεοῦ) by faith"
> 1 John 3:1-2: "**children of God** (τέκνα θεοῦ)" = believers

### Key Finding for This Study
Greek υἱός is used for humans as God's children:
- Romans 8:14, 19 - believers called "sons of God"
- Galatians 3:26 - believers are "sons of God by faith"
- Romans 9:26 - quoting Hosea, "sons of the living God"

**The Greek word υἱός CAN and DOES refer to humans in divine relationship.**

## Comparative Analysis: בֵּן vs υἱός

### Semantic Range Comparison

| Category | Hebrew בֵּן | Greek υἱός | Equivalent? |
|----------|-------------|------------|-------------|
| Literal offspring | ✓ | ✓ | **YES** |
| Descendants/posterity | ✓ | ✓ | **YES** |
| Group membership | ✓ ("sons of prophets") | ✓ ("sons of bridechamber") | **YES** |
| Characterization | ✓ ("sons of Belial") | ✓ ("sons of disobedience") | **YES** |
| Age designation | ✓ ("son of X years") | Rare (Hebraism) | **Partial** |
| Divine relationship | ✓ ("sons of God") | ✓ ("sons of God") | **YES** |

### Translation Relationship

The LXX consistently uses υἱός to translate בֵּן:

| Hebrew | LXX Greek | Evidence |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| בֵּן (ben) | υἱός (huios) | Standard translation |
| בָּנִים (banim) | υἱοί (huioi) | Plural form |
| בְּנֵי (bene) | υἱοὶ (huioi) | Construct form |

### NT Authors Follow LXX Pattern

When NT authors quote OT passages with בֵּן, they use υἱός:

| OT Passage | Hebrew | NT Citation | Greek |
|------------|--------|-------------|-------|
| Hosea 1:10 | בְּנֵי אֵל־חָי | Romans 9:26 | υἱοὶ θεοῦ ζῶντος |
| 2 Samuel 7:14 | לְבֵן | Hebrews 1:5 | υἱόν |
| Psalm 2:7 | בְּנִי | Acts 13:33 | υἱός μου |

**This establishes υἱός as the recognized Greek equivalent of בֵּן.**

## The "Son of X" Idiom

### Hebrew Pattern: בֶּן + Noun = One Characterized by That Noun

| Hebrew | Literal | Actual Meaning |
|--------|---------|----------------|
| בֶּן־חַיִל | son of strength | mighty warrior |
| בְּנֵי בְלִיַּעַל | sons of worthlessness | worthless men |
| בֶּן־מָוֶת | son of death | one deserving death |
| בְּנֵי אָדָם | sons of Adam/man | human beings |
| בְּנֵי אֱלֹהִים | sons of God | beings in relationship with God |

### Greek Pattern: υἱός + Genitive = One Characterized by That Quality

| Greek | Literal | Actual Meaning |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| υἱὸς παρακλήσεως | son of consolation | encourager (Barnabas) |
| υἱοὶ τῆς ἀπειθείας | sons of disobedience | disobedient people |
| υἱοὶ τοῦ φωτός | sons of light | enlightened believers |
| υἱὸς ἀπωλείας | son of perdition | one destined for destruction |
| υἱοὶ θεοῦ | sons of God | beings in relationship with God |

### Analysis: Identical Idiom
The NT adopts the Hebrew "son of X" idiom directly. This is a Semitic construction that Greek inherited through the LXX and Jewish influence. The NT uses υἱός exactly as the OT uses בֵּן in these figurative constructions.

## Summary: Word Studies and the Question

### The Question
Does the NT use υἱός (huios) in a similar manner to the OT's use of בֵּן (ben)?

### The Answer

**YES** - The evidence is overwhelming:

1. **Same semantic range** - Both words cover literal offspring, descendants, group members, characterization, and divine relationship

2. **Translation equivalence** - LXX consistently translates בֵּן with υἱός; NT authors follow this pattern when quoting OT

3. **Identical idiom** - The "son of X" construction works the same way in both languages

4. **Same application** - Both words apply "sons of God" to humans:
   - OT: Israel as God's children (Deut 14:1; Hos 1:10)
   - NT: Believers as God's children (Rom 8:14; Gal 3:26)

5. **Direct quotation** - Romans 9:26 quotes Hosea 1:10, using υἱοί for בְּנֵי, proving they are equivalent

### Implications
If υἱός is the equivalent of בֵּן, and NT υἱοὶ θεοῦ refers to humans, then Hebrew בְּנֵי אֱלֹהִים linguistically CAN refer to humans. The semantic range of both words includes human beings in divine relationship.
