# Analysis: "Strange Flesh" (σαρκὸς ἑτέρας) in Jude 1:7

## The Interpretive Question

What does Jude mean by σαρκὸς ἑτέρας ("strange flesh" / "flesh of a different kind")?

Two interpretations have been proposed:
1. **Homosexual relations** - flesh "different" from natural design
2. **Angelic flesh** - flesh of a categorically different order

---

## Linguistic Analysis: ἕτερος vs ἄλλος

### The Critical Distinction

Greek has two words commonly translated "another/other":

| Word | Strong's | Meaning | Nuance |
|------|----------|---------|--------|
| **ἕτερος** (heteros) | G2087 | other, different | Different in **KIND** or **nature** |
| **ἄλλος** (allos) | G243 | another, other | Another of the **SAME kind** |

### Evidence from NT Usage

**Jude uses ἕτερος** (G2087) - only translated "strange" once in the entire NT (here in Jude 1:7).

**Paul uses ἄλλος** (G243) in 1 Corinthians 15:39 when distinguishing flesh types:
- "**another** (ἄλλη) flesh of beasts"
- "**another** (ἄλλη) of fishes"
- "**another** (ἄλλη) of birds"

Paul's usage shows ἄλλος for different types **within the same category** of flesh.

**Romans 1:26-27** uses παρὰ φύσιν ("against nature") for homosexuality - NOT ἕτερος.

### Linguistic Implication

Jude's choice of ἕτερος suggests flesh that is **categorically different** - not merely another type within the same category.

**If homosexuality:** Why not use παρὰ φύσιν like Paul? Human male flesh is not a "different kind" from human female flesh - both are human.

**If angelic flesh:** ἕτερος precisely describes flesh of a completely different order - angelic vs human.

## Contextual Analysis: Genesis 19

### Key Observations

1. **The visitors were angels** (Gen 19:1, 15) - explicitly stated
2. **They appeared as men** (Gen 19:5) - the Sodomites called them "men"
3. **The Sodomites did not know they were angels** - they demanded to "know" them as "men"
4. **Lot offered his daughters** (Gen 19:8) - female substitute for male visitors
5. **The Sodomites refused the female substitute** (Gen 19:9) - their desire was specifically for the male visitors

### What This Suggests

**For interpretation 1 (homosexuality):**
- The Sodomites wanted males, not females
- They refused Lot's daughters
- Their sin was homosexual desire

**For interpretation 2 (angelic flesh):**
- The visitors WERE angels, regardless of appearance
- If "strange flesh" means angels, it's what they actually were
- From God's perspective (who inspired Jude), they were pursuing angelic beings

### The Parallel in Judges 19

The Gibeah narrative closely parallels Genesis 19, using **identical Hebrew vocabulary**:

| Element | Genesis 19 | Judges 19 | Hebrew |
|---------|------------|-----------|--------|
| "know" (sexual demand) | 19:5 | 19:22 | יָדַע (yada) **H3045** |
| "wickedly" (host's response) | 19:7 | 19:23 | רעע (ra'a) **H7489** |
| "daughter(s)" offered | 19:8 | 19:24 | בַּת (bat) **H1323** |
| "not known man" | 19:8 | 19:24 | יָדַע (yada) **H3045** |
| **"man/men" (visitor)** | 19:5 | 19:22 | אִישׁ (ish) **H376** |

### Key Observation: "Men" vs "Angels"

| Passage | Speaker | Hebrew | Strong's | Translation |
|---------|---------|--------|----------|-------------|
| Gen 19:1 | **Narrator** | מַלְאָךְ (mal'ak) | H4397 | "angels" |
| Gen 19:5 | **Sodomites** | אִישׁ (ish) | H376 | "men" |
| Jdg 19:22 | **Men of Gibeah** | אִישׁ (ish) | H376 | "man" |

The **narrator** in Genesis 19:1 tells us the visitors were מַלְאָכִים (angels), but the **Sodomites** called them אִישׁ (men) - the same word used in Judges 19. From the mob's perspective, both scenarios involved pursuing "men" (אִישׁ).

**Significance:**
- The linguistic parallel is undeniable - the same Hebrew words describe the same scenario
- The Sodomites thought they were pursuing "men" (אִישׁ) - identical to Gibeah
- But the narrator (and Jude, with hindsight) knows they were actually "angels" (מַלְאָךְ)
- This supports both readings: the subjective sin was the same (homosexual desire for אִישׁ), but the objective reality differed (מַלְאָכִים in Sodom)

## Analysis of "In Like Manner"

### The Grammatical Question

Jude 1:7 contains the phrase τὸν ὅμοιον τρόπον τούτοις ("in like manner to these").

**See related study:** [jude-1-6-7-in-like-manner](../jude-1-6-7-in-like-manner/CONCLUSION.md) for full analysis.

### Two Readings

**Reading A:** τούτοις refers to the **angels** (v6)
- Sodom sinned "in like manner to the angels"
- Both crossed flesh-categories
- Supports interpretation 2 (angelic flesh)

**Reading B:** τούτοις refers to the **inhabitants of the cities** (v7)
- The surrounding cities sinned "like the people of Sodom"
- v6 and v7 are separate examples of judgment
- Does NOT connect Sodom's sin to the angels' sin

### Grammatical Evidence

- τούτοις is **masculine plural**
- Cities are **feminine**
- Angels (v6) are **masculine**

However, NT Greek shows constructio ad sensum - cities can take masculine when inhabitants are meant (Acts 8:5, 8:14; Luke 10:13; Matt 23:37).

**Conclusion from related study:** Grammar alone does not determine the antecedent. Both readings are grammatically defensible.

## Connection to Jude 1:6

### What Jude 6 Says

"The angels which kept not their first estate (arche), but left their own habitation (oiketerion)..."

**See related study:** `../jude-6-everlasting-chains/` and [jude-6-7-angels-sin](../jude-6-7-angels-sin/CONCLUSION.md)

### Key Findings from Related Studies

1. **Arche** (G746) = position, rank, domain - NOT physical nature
2. **Oiketerion** (G3613) = dwelling place - in context, heaven
3. Jude 6 describes the **angelic rebellion** (Rev 12 parallel)
4. The angels were **cast out of heaven** - NOT that they took human form

### Does Jude 6 Describe Sexual Sin?

The jude-6-7-angels-sin study concluded: **No.**

- "First estate" (arche) refers to position/rank, not nature
- "Habitation" (oiketerion) most naturally means heaven
- The parallel in Revelation 12 shows angels cast from heaven
- Nothing in the vocabulary requires or implies sexual activity

If Jude 6 does NOT describe sexual sin, then "in like manner" (if it connects v6 and v7) would not necessarily connect their **sins** but perhaps their **judgments** or some other element.

## 2 Peter 2:4-6 Parallel

### Comparison

| Element | Jude | 2 Peter |
|---------|------|---------|
| Angels | v6: Reserved in chains | v4: Cast to hell, chains of darkness |
| Connection | "in like manner" | **No connecting phrase** |
| Sodom | v7: Fornication, strange flesh | v6: Overthrow, example to ungodly |

### Key Observation

2 Peter does NOT include "in like manner" and does NOT describe Sodom's specific sin. Peter treats angels, flood, and Sodom as **separate examples** of judgment.

If Peter and Jude share a common tradition, the "in like manner" and "strange flesh" are **unique to Jude**.

## Evaluation of Both Interpretations

### Interpretation 1: Homosexuality

**Supporting Evidence:**
1. Genesis 19:4-5 shows men demanding male visitors
2. Genesis 19:8-9 shows refusal of female substitute
3. Judges 19 parallel involves no angels - same sin pattern
4. Romans 1:26-27 connects Sodom's legacy with homosexuality
5. NT terms for homosexuality (arsenokoites) derive from LXX of Lev 18:22; 20:13
6. Ezekiel 16:50 calls Sodom's sin "abomination" (toebah) - same word as Lev 18:22

**Challenges:**
1. Why use ἕτερος ("different kind") for same-species flesh?
2. Human male flesh is not categorically "different" from human female flesh

### Interpretation 2: Angelic Flesh

**Supporting Evidence:**
1. ἕτερος means "different in kind" - angelic flesh is categorically different
2. Genesis 19:1 explicitly states visitors were angels
3. Creates theological parallel with v6 (if "in like manner" connects them)

**Challenges:**
1. Sodomites didn't know visitors were angels
2. Judges 19 parallel has no angels - same sin described
3. Requires assuming Jude 6 describes sexual sin (not established)
4. 2 Peter parallel does not connect the two

---

## Resolution: A Both/And Reading

### The Judges 19 Parallel - What It Proves and Doesn't Prove

The Gibeah narrative (Judges 19) replicates the Genesis 19 scenario using **identical Hebrew vocabulary**:

| Hebrew Word | Strong's | Genesis 19 | Judges 19 |
|-------------|----------|------------|-----------|
| יָדַע (yada) | H3045 | "know" (19:5) | "know" (19:22) |
| רעע (ra'a) | H7489 | "wickedly" (19:7) | "wickedly" (19:23) |
| בַּת (bat) | H1323 | "daughters" (19:8) | "daughter" (19:24) |

**But there are no angels in Gibeah.** The visitor was a human Levite.

**What this proves:**
- The same Hebrew vocabulary describes both events
- Homosexual mob violence existed as a sin pattern without angels
- The narrative structure can occur with purely human actors

**What this does NOT prove:**
- That Jude wasn't highlighting something *additional* about Sodom's case
- That ἕτερος should be understood the same way for both narratives

The Judges 19 parallel is linguistically strong - the same Hebrew words describe the same sin. However, it doesn't prove Jude wasn't *also* noting the angelic dimension unique to Sodom.

### The Both/And Reading

A more nuanced reading recognizes that **both elements may be true simultaneously**:

**The Sodomites' perspective (subjective):**
- They saw "men" (Gen 19:5)
- Their intent was homosexual - they wanted males, refused females
- Their culpable sin was same-sex lust

**Jude's perspective (objective, with hindsight):**
- The visitors WERE angels (Gen 19:1)
- In objective reality, they pursued flesh of a categorically different order
- ἕτερος ("different in kind") is precisely accurate - angelic flesh IS categorically different

### Why This Reading Works

1. **Explains ἕτερος** - Jude uses "different in kind" because, from his inspired hindsight, that's what the flesh actually was. The word choice reflects the objective reality, not the Sodomites' perception.

2. **Explains the Judges 19 distinction** - Gibeah had the same homosexual intent but NOT the same objective reality. Sodom's case was uniquely egregious because they (unknowingly) crossed the human/angel boundary.

3. **Doesn't require the Sodomites to know** - The objective nature of the sin doesn't depend on their awareness. Jude, writing with divine perspective, knows what they were actually pursuing.

4. **Maintains the homosexual element** - Their culpable intent was homosexual; the angelic dimension adds another layer to what actually occurred.

5. **Explains why Jude chose Sodom over Gibeah** - If he just wanted an example of homosexuality, Gibeah was available. He chose the one with angels because that added dimension matters.

### What This Does NOT Require

This reading does NOT require:
- That Jude 6 describes sexual sin (it describes rebellion - leaving position)
- That Genesis 6 "sons of God" were angels (a separate question)
- That "in like manner" connects the sins of v6 and v7 (grammar is ambiguous)

The connection between v6 and v7 (if any) might be:
- Both are examples of divine judgment
- Both involve leaving/crossing proper boundaries (position for angels, flesh-categories for Sodom)
- But NOT necessarily the same type of sin

### Consistency with OT

Leviticus 18:22 calls homosexuality תּוֹעֵבָה (toebah - "abomination")
Ezekiel 16:50 says Sodom committed "abomination" (same word)

This links Sodom's sin to the Levitical prohibition - confirming the homosexual element was present.

---

## Conclusion

**Two readings are viable:**

### Reading 1: Homosexuality Only

"Strange flesh" = flesh "different" from the natural counterpart (men pursuing men). The Judges 19 parallel supports this - same sin pattern, no angels. This is the simpler reading.

### Reading 2: Both/And

The Sodomites' sin was homosexual in intent, AND (from Jude's perspective) they unknowingly pursued angelic flesh. This explains ἕτερος ("different in kind") and why Jude chose Sodom over Gibeah.

**What is certain:**
- The homosexual element is clearly present (Gen 19:4-9; Ezek 16:50)
- The visitors were angels (Gen 19:1)

**What remains uncertain:**
- Whether Jude intended to highlight the angelic dimension
- The exact referent of τούτοις in "in like manner" (see related study)
- Whether Jude intended to connect the angels' sin (rebellion) to Sodom's sin
