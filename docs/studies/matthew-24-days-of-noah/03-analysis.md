# Analysis

## The Central Question

What did Jesus mean by "as the days of Noah" (Matthew 24:37)? Two primary interpretations exist:

| View | What Jesus Referenced |
|------|----------------------|
| **Supernatural View** | The "sons of God"/angel mixing with humans, Nephilim, supernatural corruption |
| **Ordinary Life View** | Normal daily activities, spiritual unawareness, sudden unexpected judgment |

**This analysis will examine Jesus's own words to determine which view He intended.**

---

## Exegetical Analysis: What Did Jesus Actually Say?

### Matthew 24:37-39 - Jesus's Full Explanation

> v.37: "But **as the days of Noe [were]**, so shall also the coming of the Son of man be."
>
> v.38: "**For** [γάρ/gar - explanatory] as in the days that were before the flood they were **eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage**, until the day that Noe entered into the ark,"
>
> v.39: "And **knew not** until the flood came, and took them all away; so shall also the coming of the Son of man be."

**Critical observation:** Jesus provided His own interpretation.

The word "For" (γάρ/gar) in v.38 is **explanatory** - Jesus is explaining what He means by "days of Noah." His explanation consists of:

1. **Eating and drinking** - Normal sustenance activities
2. **Marrying and giving in marriage** - Normal social/family activities
3. **Knew not** - Unawareness/unconcern
4. **Until the day** - Sudden, unexpected timing
5. **Took them all away** - Complete, inescapable judgment

**Jesus did NOT explain "days of Noah" by referencing:**
- Sons of God
- Daughters of men
- Nephilim/giants
- Angels
- Supernatural mixing
- Corruption of bloodlines
- Unusual wickedness

### The Explanatory Power of γάρ (gar)

When Jesus says "as the days of Noah... **for** they were eating and drinking," the conjunction γάρ introduces His authoritative explanation of what "days of Noah" means **for His teaching**. Whatever else may have characterized Noah's days, Jesus selected these elements as relevant to His point.

## The Luke 17 Parallel: Lot Confirms the Interpretation

### Luke 17:26-30

> v.26: "And **as it was in the days of Noe**, so shall it be also in the days of the Son of man."
>
> v.27: "They did eat, they drank, they married wives, they were given in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark, and the flood came, and destroyed them all."
>
> v.28: "**Likewise also as it was in the days of Lot**; they did eat, they drank, they bought, they sold, they planted, they builded;"
>
> v.29: "But the same day that Lot went out of Sodom it rained fire and brimstone from heaven, and destroyed [them] all."
>
> v.30: "Even thus shall it be in the day when the Son of man is revealed."

### The Lot Parallel as Interpretive Key

Jesus draws an explicit parallel between Noah's days and Lot's days:

| Noah's Days | Lot's Days |
|-------------|------------|
| Eating, drinking | Eating, drinking |
| Marrying | Buying, selling |
| - | Planting, building |
| Sudden flood | Sudden fire |
| Destroyed all | Destroyed all |

**Critical observation:** Lot's days had NO "sons of God" element, NO Nephilim, NO angel-human mixing. Yet Jesus says "**Likewise**" (ὁμοίως/homoios) - in the same way.

**If Jesus intended to reference the supernatural "sons of God" element, the Lot parallel would undermine His point**, since Sodom's sin (whatever it was) had nothing to do with angel-human offspring.

The only common elements are:
1. **Ordinary life activities** (eating, drinking, marrying, business)
2. **Spiritual unawareness/unconcern**
3. **Sudden, complete destruction**
4. **A righteous person preserved** (Noah, Lot)

## Analysis of What Jesus Omitted

### Genesis 6 Elements Jesus Could Have Referenced

If Jesus wanted to emphasize the supernatural aspect of Noah's days, He had clear options:

| Genesis 6 Element | Available for Reference | Jesus Used? |
|-------------------|------------------------|-------------|
| "Sons of God" | Yes (6:2, 4) | **NO** |
| "Daughters of men" | Yes (6:2, 4) | **NO** |
| "Giants/Nephilim" | Yes (6:4) | **NO** |
| "Mighty men" | Yes (6:4) | **NO** |
| "Men of renown" | Yes (6:4) | **NO** |
| Wickedness of man | Yes (6:5) | **NO** |
| Evil imagination | Yes (6:5) | **NO** |
| Grieved God | Yes (6:6) | **NO** |
| Corruption | Yes (6:11-12) | **NO** |
| Violence | Yes (6:11, 13) | **NO** |

**Instead, Jesus emphasized elements NOT prominent in Genesis 6:**
- Eating and drinking (not mentioned in Genesis 6)
- Ordinary marrying (Genesis 6:2 has different context)
- Unawareness/ignorance (not emphasized in Genesis 6)

### Why the Silence Matters

Jesus was speaking to a Jewish audience familiar with Genesis. If the "sons of God" narrative was His point, He would have mentioned it. His silence on this element - combined with His explicit explanation of what He DID mean - is interpretively decisive.

## The Purpose of Jesus's Teaching

### Context: Olivet Discourse (Matthew 24)

Jesus is answering: "What shall be the sign of thy coming, and of the end of the world?" (Matthew 24:3)

His "days of Noah" teaching serves to warn about:
1. **Unknowability of timing** - "of that day and hour knoweth no man" (v.36)
2. **Normalcy before judgment** - life continues as usual (v.38)
3. **Sudden interruption** - "knew not until the flood came" (v.39)
4. **Need for watchfulness** - "Watch therefore" (v.42)
5. **Need for readiness** - "be ye also ready" (v.44)

### What Would the Supernatural View Contribute?

If Jesus meant to reference angel-human mixing, what would be the application?

| If Supernatural View | Implied Application |
|----------------------|---------------------|
| Angels mixed with humans | Watch for... angels mixing with humans? |
| Nephilim existed | Watch for... giants? |
| Bloodlines corrupted | Watch for... genetic corruption? |

**None of these fit Jesus's actual application:** "Watch therefore" and "be ye also ready."

### What the Ordinary Life View Contributes

| If Ordinary Life View | Implied Application |
|-----------------------|---------------------|
| People were living normal lives | Don't be caught up in ordinary life |
| People were unaware | Stay spiritually aware |
| Judgment came suddenly | Be ready at all times |
| Only Noah was prepared | Be like Noah - prepared |

**This perfectly matches Jesus's actual application.**

## The "Marrying and Giving in Marriage" Question

### Does This Reference Genesis 6:2?

Some argue that "marrying and giving in marriage" alludes to Genesis 6:2 where the "sons of God" took wives.

**Analysis:**

| Genesis 6:2 Marriage | Matthew 24:38 Marriage |
|---------------------|------------------------|
| "Sons of God" taking daughters of men | General population marrying |
| Unusual/problematic union | Normal social activity |
| Described negatively (contextually) | Listed alongside eating/drinking |
| Unique to Noah's time | Parallel with Lot's time (no such element) |

**Key evidence: The Lot parallel destroys this connection.**

Luke 17:28 describes Lot's days: "they did eat, they drank, they **bought, they sold**, they planted, they builded."

Notice: Luke substitutes "buying and selling" for "marrying" when describing Lot's days. This shows:
1. Jesus was listing **categories of ordinary activity**, not alluding to specific Genesis events
2. The activities are interchangeable examples of normal life
3. No special significance attaches to "marrying" in Jesus's teaching

## Comparison with Related Studies

### From [genesis-6-sons-of-god](../genesis-6-sons-of-god/CONCLUSION.md) Study

**Relevance:** Even if Genesis 6 refers to human intermarriage (Sethite view), Jesus did not reference this. His point was not about the TYPE of marrying but about ORDINARY marrying continuing until sudden judgment.

## Summary of Analysis

### Evidence for Ordinary Life View

1. **Jesus's own explanation** - He defined "days of Noah" as eating, drinking, marrying, ignorance
2. **The Lot parallel** - Proves the point is ordinary life + sudden judgment (no supernatural element)
3. **Jesus's application** - "Watch" and "be ready" fits ordinary life view
4. **Silence on supernatural** - Jesus mentioned none of the Genesis 6 supernatural elements
5. **Audience relevance** - Ordinary life view applies to all generations

### Evidence Against Supernatural View

1. **Jesus never mentioned it** - No sons of God, Nephilim, or angels referenced
2. **Lot parallel contradicts it** - Sodom had no such element, yet Jesus says "likewise"
3. **Application doesn't fit** - "Watch for angel-human mixing" makes no sense
4. **Elements Jesus emphasized** - All are ordinary, non-supernatural activities
5. **Explanatory conjunction** - γάρ shows Jesus is defining what He means

### Conclusion of Analysis

**Jesus was NOT referring to angels mixing with humans.** He was warning that:

1. Before His coming, life will seem normal
2. People will be absorbed in ordinary activities
3. They will be spiritually unaware
4. Judgment will come suddenly and completely
5. Only those who are "watching" and "ready" will be preserved

The "days of Noah" reference serves to illustrate **sudden judgment interrupting ordinary life**, not to reference any supernatural event.
