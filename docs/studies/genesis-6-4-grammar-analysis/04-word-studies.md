# Word Studies: Genesis 6:4 Grammar Analysis

## H5303 - נְפִיל (nephil) - "Giants/Nephilim"

**Transliteration:** nᵉphîyl
**Pronunciation:** nef-eel
**Part of Speech:** Masculine noun
**Occurrences:** 3 in OT

### Definition
From H5307 (נָפַל - naphal, "to fall"); properly, a feller, i.e., a bully or tyrant - translated "giant."

### OT Occurrences

| Verse | Text |
|-------|------|
| Genesis 6:4 | "There were **giants** in the earth in those days" |
| Numbers 13:33 | "And there we saw the **giants**, the sons of Anak, which come of the giants" |

### Significance for Grammar Analysis
The word "Nephilim" appears with the definite article (הַנְּפִלִים - "THE Nephilim"), indicating a known, identifiable group. This supports the reading that they were already existing, not being introduced as new offspring.

## H1961 - הָיָה (hayah) - "To Be/Were"

**Transliteration:** hâyâh
**Pronunciation:** haw-yaw
**Part of Speech:** Verb
**Occurrences:** 75+ in OT

### Definition
A primitive root; to exist, to be or become, to come to pass.

### Form in Genesis 6:4
- **הָיוּ** (hayu) - Qal Perfect, 3rd person plural
- Translation: "were" / "existed"

### Grammatical Significance
The **Perfect tense** (Qal Perfect) indicates:
1. A completed state or fact
2. Something that WAS already the case
3. Background information in narrative

This is NOT the verb form used to describe something coming into existence as a result of a following action. The Perfect tense states: "The Nephilim **were** (already)" - as a fact.

## H935 - בּוֹא (bo') - "To Come/Enter"

**Transliteration:** bô'
**Pronunciation:** bo
**Part of Speech:** Verb
**Occurrences:** 2,579 in OT

### Definition
A primitive root; to go or come (in a wide variety of applications).

### Form in Genesis 6:4
- **יָבֹאוּ** (yavo'u) - Qal Imperfect, 3rd person masculine plural
- Translation: "came in" / "were coming in" / "kept coming in"

### Grammatical Significance
The **Imperfect tense** indicates:
1. Ongoing or habitual action
2. Action in progress relative to another event
3. In subordinate clauses: provides background or circumstantial information

The Imperfect here describes what was happening during the time period, NOT a single causative event. It describes the circumstances, not the origin of the Nephilim.

## H3205 - יָלַד (yalad) - "To Bear/Beget"

**Transliteration:** yâlad
**Pronunciation:** yaw-lad
**Part of Speech:** Verb
**Occurrences:** 499 in OT

### Definition
A primitive root; to bear young; to beget; to bring forth.

### Form in Genesis 6:4
- **יָלְדוּ** (yaledu) - Qal Perfect, 3rd person plural
- Translation: "bore" / "gave birth"

### Grammatical Significance
The **Perfect tense** here indicates:
1. A completed action (they bore children)
2. The result of the unions described

Note: The offspring are NOT called "Nephilim" in this clause. They are called:
- הֵמָּה הַגִּבֹּרִים - "they [were] the mighty men"
- אַנְשֵׁי הַשֵּׁם - "men of renown"

The text distinguishes between:
1. **Nephilim** - who already existed (main clause)
2. **Offspring of the unions** - called "mighty men" and "men of renown" (subordinate clause)

## H834 - אֲשֶׁר (asher) - "Which/When/That"

**Transliteration:** 'ăsher
**Pronunciation:** ash-er
**Part of Speech:** Relative particle

### Definition
A primitive relative pronoun; who, which, what, that; also as conjunction: when, since, because.

### Function in Genesis 6:4
Introduces a **temporal/relative clause**: "when/at the time that"

```
אֲשֶׁר יָבֹאוּ בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים
"when the sons of God came in..."
```

### Grammatical Significance
The אֲשֶׁר clause is SUBORDINATE to the main clause. It provides:
- Temporal context ("when")
- Circumstantial information
- NOT the origin or cause of what was stated in the main clause

## H310 - אַחַר (achar) - "After"

**Transliteration:** 'achar
**Pronunciation:** akh-ar
**Part of Speech:** Noun/Adverb

### Definition
From H309; the hind part; hence, after.

### Form in Genesis 6:4
- **אַחֲרֵי־כֵן** (acharei-khen) - "after that"
- Construct form + demonstrative

### Critical Significance
The phrase "and also **after that**" (וְגַם אַחֲרֵי־כֵן) is devastating to the hybrid theory:

1. If Nephilim were offspring of the unions, they could only exist AFTER, not before
2. The text says they existed "in those days" AND "also after that"
3. This only makes sense if Nephilim PRE-EXISTED the unions

---

## Summary: What the Key Words Reveal

| Word | Form | Grammatical Function |
|------|------|---------------------|
| הָיוּ | Perfect | States fact: Nephilim EXISTED |
| יָבֹאוּ | Imperfect | Describes ongoing action in subordinate clause |
| יָלְדוּ | Perfect | States result: offspring were born |
| אֲשֶׁר | Relative | Introduces subordinate/temporal clause |
| אַחֲרֵי־כֵן | Temporal | "after that" - proves pre-existence |

The word studies confirm the grammatical analysis: The Nephilim were **already present** when the sons of God unions began. The offspring of those unions were called "mighty men" and "men of renown," NOT Nephilim.
