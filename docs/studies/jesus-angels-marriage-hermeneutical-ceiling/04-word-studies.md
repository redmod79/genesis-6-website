# Word Studies: Jesus on Angels and Marriage

## 1. G2465 -- isangelos (equal unto the angels)

### Lexicon Data
- **Greek:** ἰσάγγελος (isangelos)
- **Strong's:** G2465
- **Transliteration:** isangelos
- **Part of Speech:** Adjective (adj)
- **Pronunciation:** ee-sang-el-los
- **Definition:** Like an angel, i.e. angelic; equal unto the angels
- **Compound:** From isos (G2470, "equal") + angelos (G32, "angel/messenger")
- **BLB Count:** 1 occurrence

### Occurrences
- **Luke 20:36** -- "Neither can they die any more: for they are **equal unto the angels** (isangeloi); and are the children of God, being the children of the resurrection."

This is a **hapax legomenon** -- it appears only once in the entire New Testament. This makes it significant: Luke (or Luke's source) coined or selected this specific compound adjective to express what Matthew and Mark describe with the phrase "as the angels."

### Greek Grammar (from parser analysis)
```
ἰσάγγελοι  [A-NPM] Nominative Plural Masculine
```
- **Nominative:** Subject complement (predicate adjective) -- describing what the resurrected saints ARE
- **Plural:** Matches the plural subjects (those accounted worthy)
- **Masculine:** Grammatical agreement

### Significance

The compound is critically important:

1. **isos (G2470)** means "equal in quantity or quality." It is not a weaker word like "similar" or "comparable." It denotes genuine equality in the quality being compared.

2. **The compound form** (isos + angelos) creates a single-concept adjective: "angel-equal." This is stronger than "like angels" (hos angeloi) used in Matthew/Mark. Luke's isangeloi predicates a quality of BEING, not merely a point of comparison.

3. **No qualifier appears with isangeloi.** Matthew has "angels of God in heaven." Mark has "angels which are in heaven." But Luke's isangeloi stands unqualified -- "equal unto THE ANGELS." This is a categorical statement about angelic nature, not limited to a subset of angels.

4. **Context links it to nature, not behavior:** Luke 20:36 connects isangeloi to "neither can they die any more" -- the comparison is about the NATURE of resurrected beings (immortality, non-marriage) being equal to angelic nature.

## 2. G1060 -- gameo (to marry)

### Lexicon Data
- **Greek:** γαμέω (gameo)
- **Strong's:** G1060
- **Transliteration:** gameo
- **Part of Speech:** Verb (v)
- **Definition:** To wed (of either sex); to marry (a wife)
- **BLB Count:** 29 occurrences

### Key Translations (29 total)
| Count | Translation |
|-------|-------------|
| 8 (27.6%) | marry |
| 3 (10.3%) | to marry |
| 2 (6.9%) | shall marry |
| 2 (6.9%) | marrieth |
| 2 (6.9%) | let them marry |
| 2 (6.9%) | is married |

### Usage in Our Passages

**Matthew 22:30:** γαμοῦσιν (gamousin) -- Present Active Indicative, 3rd Person Plural
- "they [do not] **marry**"

**Mark 12:25:** γαμοῦσιν (gamousin) -- Present Active Indicative, 3rd Person Plural
- "they [do not] **marry**"

**Luke 20:34:** γαμοῦσιν (gamousin) -- Present Active Indicative, 3rd Person Plural
- "The children of this world **marry**"

**Luke 20:35:** γαμοῦσιν (gamousin) -- Present Active Indicative, 3rd Person Plural
- "[they do not] **marry**"

### Significance

gameo is the standard, ordinary Greek word for marriage. It is used throughout the NT for normal human marriage (Matt 5:32; 19:9-10; 1 Cor 7:9, 28, 33, 36, 38, 39; 1 Tim 4:3; 5:11, 14). There is nothing euphemistic or unusual about this word. Jesus is speaking about literal marriage.

The active voice (gamousin) traditionally refers to the male action of taking a wife.

## 3. G1061 -- gamisko/gamizo (to give in marriage)

### Lexicon Data
- **Greek:** γαμίσκω/γαμίζω (gamisko/gamizo)
- **Strong's:** G1061
- **Transliteration:** gamisko
- **Part of Speech:** Verb (v)
- **Definition:** To espouse (a daughter to a husband); to give in marriage
- **Derived from:** G1062 (gamos, "a wedding")
- **BLB Count:** 1 occurrence (some MSS use gamizo G1061 in multiple passages)

### Key Translation
| Count | Translation |
|-------|-------------|
| 1 (100%) | are given in marriage |

### Usage in Our Passages

**Matthew 22:30:** γαμίζονται (gamizontai) -- Present Passive Indicative, 3rd Person Plural
- "they are [not] **given in marriage**"

**Mark 12:25:** γαμίζονται (gamizontai) -- Present Passive Indicative, 3rd Person Plural
- "they are [not] **given in marriage**"

**Luke 20:34:** γαμίσκονται (gamiskontai) -- Present Passive Indicative, 3rd Person Plural
- "**are given in marriage**"

**Luke 20:35:** γαμίζονται (gamizontai) -- Present Passive Indicative, 3rd Person Plural
- "[are not] **given in marriage**"

### Significance

gamisko/gamizo is the passive counterpart to gameo. While gameo describes the act of marrying (typically the groom), gamizo/gamisko describes being given in marriage (typically the bride, given by the father). Together, the pair gameo + gamizo covers the ENTIRETY of the marriage institution -- both the active marrying and the passive being given in marriage. Jesus uses both words to leave no aspect of marriage unaddressed.

The **passive voice** (gamizontai/gamiskontai) is significant. In the Sadducees' question, the woman was "given" to seven brothers. Jesus addresses both sides of the marriage transaction.

**Luke's variant:** Luke 20:34 uses gamiskontai (from gamisko), while Luke 20:35 and the Matthew/Mark parallels use gamizontai (from gamizo). These are variant forms of the same concept.

## 4. G32 -- angelos (angel/messenger)

### Lexicon Data
- **Greek:** ἄγγελος (angelos)
- **Strong's:** G32
- **Transliteration:** angelos
- **Part of Speech:** Masculine Noun (n-m)
- **Definition:** A messenger; especially an angel; by implication, a pastor
- **BLB Count:** 186 occurrences (181 in KJV translation data)

### Key Translations
| Count | Translation |
|-------|-------------|
| 71 (39.2%) | angel |
| 59 (32.6%) | angels |
| 10 (5.5%) | the angel |
| 10 (5.5%) | an angel |
| 7 (3.9%) | of angels |
| 3 (1.7%) | messenger |

### Usage in Our Passages

**Matthew 22:30:** ἄγγελοι (angeloi) -- Nominative Plural Masculine
- "as the **angels** of God in heaven"
- Greek: ὡς ἄγγελοι ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ (hos angeloi en to ourano)

**Mark 12:25:** ἄγγελοι (angeloi) -- Nominative Plural Masculine
- "as the **angels** which are in heaven"
- Greek: ὡς ἄγγελοι ἐν τοῖς οὐρανοῖς (hos angeloi en tois ouranois)

**Luke 20:36:** [embedded in compound ἰσάγγελοι]
- "equal unto the **angels**"

### The "In Heaven" Phrase -- Scope Analysis

| Gospel | Greek | English | Qualifier |
|--------|-------|---------|-----------|
| Matthew | ὡς ἄγγελοι [τοῦ Θεοῦ] ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ | as [the] angels [of God] in heaven | "of God in heaven" |
| Mark | ὡς ἄγγελοι ἐν τοῖς οὐρανοῖς | as [the] angels in the heavens | "in the heavens" |
| Luke | ἰσάγγελοι | equal-to-angels | **NONE** |

**Critical grammatical observation:** In both Matthew and Mark, the phrase "in heaven" (en to ourano / en tois ouranois) is a **locative prepositional phrase** that could modify either:
1. The noun "angels" -- describing WHERE the angels are (locative)
2. The verb "are" (eisin) -- describing WHERE the comparison applies

The word order in Greek places "in heaven" after "angels" and before "are" (eisin):
- Matthew: ἀλλ' ὡς ἄγγελοι ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ εἰσιν
- Mark: ἀλλ' εἰσὶν ὡς ἄγγελοι ἐν τοῖς οὐρανοῖς

**The decisive evidence is Luke's parallel.** Luke omits "in heaven" entirely and replaces the phrase-comparison (hos angeloi) with the compound adjective (isangeloi). If "in heaven" were a theologically limiting qualifier (restricting the statement to faithful angels only), Luke could not omit it without changing the meaning. The fact that Luke considers isangeloi (angel-equal) a faithful representation of Jesus's teaching demonstrates that "in heaven" describes WHERE angels ARE, not which angels are meant.

## 5. Additional Vocabulary Notes

### G5613 -- hos (as, like)
Used in Matthew 22:30 and Mark 12:25 as the comparative particle. hos introduces a comparison of similarity: resurrected saints will be "like" or "as" angels. This is a comparison of KIND, not of degree.

### G386 -- anastasis (resurrection)
- **Matthew 22:30:** ἐν τῇ ἀναστάσει (en te anastasei) -- "in the resurrection" (locative dative)
- **Luke 20:35:** τῆς ἀναστάσεως (tes anastaseos) -- "the resurrection" (genitive, objective)
- **Luke 20:36:** τῆς ἀναστάσεως (tes anastaseos) -- "the resurrection" (genitive, descriptive -- "children of the resurrection")

### G2661 -- kataxioo (to count worthy)
- **Luke 20:35:** καταξιωθέντες (kataxiothentes) -- Aorist Passive Participle, Nominative Plural Masculine
- Unique to Luke's account. Those who "attain" (tuchein) that age are those who have been "counted worthy." This adds a soteriological dimension: the resurrection state (including being isangeloi) is for the redeemed.

### G1410 -- dunamai (to be able)
- **Luke 20:36:** δύνανται (dunantai) -- Present Middle/Passive Indicative, 3rd Plural
- "Neither **can** they die any more" -- the inability to die is stated as a power/capability issue. They CANNOT die. This is not a choice but a condition of nature. Similarly, the connection to isangeloi suggests the non-marriage is also a matter of nature, not mere prohibition.
